\section{Control header}
\textbf{Process owner:} Owner or board designee\par\textbf{Trigger:} new task or scheduled review; immediate escalation when the defined risk threshold is exceeded.\par\textbf{Records:} dated input, decision, exceptions, approval and revision history.\par\textbf{Applicability:} fictional examples are educational; adapt this procedure to actual jurisdictions and contracts.
\section{Execution steps}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=8pt]
\item \textbf{Define purpose.} Identify throughput, quality, safety or strategic benefit and alternatives.\par\textit{Verification:} retain dated evidence and state whether the condition passed, failed, or needs remediation.
\item \textbf{Estimate full cost.} Include acquisition, installation, training, maintenance and working capital.\par\textit{Verification:} retain dated evidence and state whether the condition passed, failed, or needs remediation.
\item \textbf{Model cash impact.} Forecast incremental after-tax cash flows consistently and NPV assumptions.\par\textit{Verification:} retain dated evidence and state whether the condition passed, failed, or needs remediation.
\item \textbf{Stress-test.} Challenge rate, utilization, cost overruns and resale assumptions.\par\textit{Verification:} retain dated evidence and state whether the condition passed, failed, or needs remediation.
\item \textbf{Decide.} Document approval authority, payback policy and kill criteria.\par\textit{Verification:} retain dated evidence and state whether the condition passed, failed, or needs remediation.
\item \textbf{Follow up.} Compare realized benefits with investment case at agreed checkpoints.\par\textit{Verification:} retain dated evidence and state whether the condition passed, failed, or needs remediation.
\end{enumerate}
\section{Exception management}
For a failed verification, halt irreversible commitments when practical, log the exception, define containment, assign action owner and due date, and seek approval from the accountable decision maker. A verbal decision does not replace retained evidence.
\section{Review and improvement}
At every recurring review evaluate cycle time, deviations, leading indicators, customer consequence, corrective actions and control effectiveness. Retire controls that do not reduce meaningful risk; revise when constraints change.
