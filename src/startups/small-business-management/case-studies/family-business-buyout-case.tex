\section{Situation}
Two siblings debate succession of a family distribution company. One works in the company and the other wants liquidity; the business depends on the founder for its largest accounts.
\section{Case evidence (fictional)}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=3pt]
\item Seller reports 95000 annual EBITDA but documentation is incomplete
\item Largest customer represents 41 percent of sales
\item A 70000 owner loan is reported in the preliminary balance sheet
\item The buyer requires financing and a 12-month transition
\end{itemize}

\section{Student tasks}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=5pt]
\item Construct a conflict-of-interest and governance process.
\item Prepare a quality-of-earnings diligence register and normalize owner dependence.
\item Separate enterprise valuation from actual equity proceeds.
\item Develop a succession and customer-retention transition plan.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Required evidence packet}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=3pt]
\item Decision statement with clearly stated constraints and alternatives.
\item Calculation sheet where numerical claims are made; show units and sensitivity.
\item Risk register with owner, response and residual uncertainty.
\item Recommendation, go/no-go threshold and 30-day follow-up plan.
\end{itemize}

\section{Instructor review notes}
A successful answer does not depend on the same choice as an instructor example. Judge the quality of evidence, arithmetic, critical assumptions, operational feasibility and treatment of adverse effects. Withhold approval when missing financial or compliance information could reverse the recommendation.
