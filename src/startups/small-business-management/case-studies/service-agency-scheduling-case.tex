\section{Situation}
A three-person dispatch business misses appointment changes and loses repeat revenue. One founder proposes a subscription scheduling tool and another favors a concierge call service.
\section{Case evidence (fictional)}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=3pt]
\item Interviews: 8 operators, 5 report repeated rescheduling problems
\item A software pilot would cost 6000 to launch and 900 monthly to run
\item Concierge delivery would cost 32 per customer per month, and the target selling price is 75
\item Customers say they value error reduction more than additional dashboard features
\end{itemize}

\section{Student tasks}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=5pt]
\item Define buyer, user and evidence quality; separate interview claims from paid behavior.
\item Compare software and concierge unit economics; name the unknown delivery volume.
\item Produce a minimum viable pilot with privacy, outage and support controls.
\item Choose a first 30-day test and its kill threshold.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Required evidence packet}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=3pt]
\item Decision statement with clearly stated constraints and alternatives.
\item Calculation sheet where numerical claims are made; show units and sensitivity.
\item Risk register with owner, response and residual uncertainty.
\item Recommendation, go/no-go threshold and 30-day follow-up plan.
\end{itemize}

\section{Instructor review notes}
A successful answer does not depend on the same choice as an instructor example. Judge the quality of evidence, arithmetic, critical assumptions, operational feasibility and treatment of adverse effects. Withhold approval when missing financial or compliance information could reverse the recommendation.
