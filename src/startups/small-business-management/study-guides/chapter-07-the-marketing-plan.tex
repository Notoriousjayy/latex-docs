\section{Learning outcome}
Build a target-segment marketing plan with measurable acquisition hypotheses.
\section{Prerequisites}
Read chapters 3, 6.\par\textbf{Required baseline:} ability to record assumptions and distinguish observations from opinions.
\section{Chapter concept map}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=3pt]
\item value proposition
\item market segmentation
\item target market definition
\item marketing strategy
\item sales forecasting
\end{itemize}

\section{Technical study notes}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=3pt]
\item Segmentation groups customers with similar needs and responses; choosing an attainable segment is different from estimating everyone who might conceivably buy.
\item A marketing plan ties positioning to channels, budget, sales process, conversion denominators and an accountable owner for feedback.
\item Forecasts become more useful when driven by measurable reach and conversion instead of a top-down percentage of a broad market.
\end{itemize}

\section{Reading and analysis protocol}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=5pt]
\item Read the chapter learning objectives, then mark terms and claims that need primary evidence.
\item For each chapter theme, distinguish the entrepreneur decision, information required, parties affected and failure condition.
\item Write a one-paragraph synthesis in your own words; cite the textbook chapter and separate contemporary practice notes.
\item Complete the applied activity and attach the underlying evidence, calculations or interview log.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Worked application and deliverable}
\textbf{Business exercise.} Estimate a reachable segment, funnel conversion and a monthly sales forecast.\par\textbf{Required submission.} Target-segment and marketing forecast. Include a dated assumption register and an explicit proceed/revise/stop recommendation.\par\textbf{Assurance checks.} Channel performance, acquisition cost, retention and data provenance.
\section{Common reasoning failures}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=3pt]
\item Conflating TAM with serviceable customers
\item Using clicks instead of paying-customer outcomes
\end{itemize}
\noindent\textbf{Correction:} require counterevidence and identify the responsible decision-maker before approving the next stage.
\section{Discussion prompt}
What funnel observation would justify halting a campaign?\par\medskip\textbf{Evidence expected:} at least two alternatives, one quantitative or independently checkable observation, a trade-off, and a decision threshold.
\section{Exit assessment}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=5pt]
\item Explain each term from the concept map without copying the textbook.
\item Defend the applied decision using an assumption, evidence and counterargument.
\item Show an objective test or inspection that could prove a key assumption wrong.
\item State what would change the recommendation and who has approval authority.
\end{enumerate}
