\section{Learning outcome}
Explain how small enterprises compete using customer proximity, specialized needs and responsive execution.
\section{Prerequisites}
Entry module: no previous chapters required.\par\textbf{Required baseline:} ability to record assumptions and distinguish observations from opinions.
\section{Chapter concept map}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=3pt]
\item small-firm significance
\item entrepreneurial motivation
\item competitive differentiation
\item customer focus and niches
\item desired legacy
\end{itemize}

\section{Technical study notes}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=3pt]
\item Small businesses matter as employers, innovators and local providers, but economic importance does not remove the need for a viable customer and cash model.
\item A defensible niche combines customer knowledge, response speed, quality and a business configuration that a larger competitor may not prioritize.
\item Founder motivation should be evaluated alongside transferable capability, resilience, time demands and the consequences for relationships.
\end{itemize}

\section{Reading and analysis protocol}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=5pt]
\item Read the chapter learning objectives, then mark terms and claims that need primary evidence.
\item For each chapter theme, distinguish the entrepreneur decision, information required, parties affected and failure condition.
\item Write a one-paragraph synthesis in your own words; cite the textbook chapter and separate contemporary practice notes.
\item Complete the applied activity and attach the underlying evidence, calculations or interview log.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Worked application and deliverable}
\textbf{Business exercise.} Write a founder fit assessment and compare a narrow niche with a large incumbent.\par\textbf{Required submission.} Founder fit and strategic niche memo. Include a dated assumption register and an explicit proceed/revise/stop recommendation.\par\textbf{Assurance checks.} Mission, stakeholder consequences, and customer interviews.
\section{Common reasoning failures}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=3pt]
\item Treating a passion as validated demand
\item Ignoring workload and relationship consequences
\end{itemize}
\noindent\textbf{Correction:} require counterevidence and identify the responsible decision-maker before approving the next stage.
\section{Discussion prompt}
What evidence would make a founder pursue a smaller defensible segment instead of a large undifferentiated market?\par\medskip\textbf{Evidence expected:} at least two alternatives, one quantitative or independently checkable observation, a trade-off, and a decision threshold.
\section{Exit assessment}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=5pt]
\item Explain each term from the concept map without copying the textbook.
\item Defend the applied decision using an assumption, evidence and counterargument.
\item Show an objective test or inspection that could prove a key assumption wrong.
\item State what would change the recommendation and who has approval authority.
\end{enumerate}
