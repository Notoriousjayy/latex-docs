\section{Learning outcome}
Distinguish unmet customer needs from technically interesting ideas and reject infeasible opportunities early.
\section{Prerequisites}
Read chapters 1, 2.\par\textbf{Required baseline:} ability to record assumptions and distinguish observations from opinions.
\section{Chapter concept map}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=3pt]
\item startup idea sources
\item innovation and pain points
\item outside-in and inside-out analysis
\item strategic focus
\item feasibility screening
\end{itemize}

\section{Technical study notes}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=3pt]
\item Separate an invention, new market, new way of delivery and improved existing offering. The type of opportunity changes how uncertainty should be tested.
\item Outside-in feasibility looks at market conditions and rivals; inside-out feasibility looks at team, capital, access and execution capability. Neither analysis is sufficient alone.
\item A screening matrix is a prioritization tool, not certainty. Write disconfirming tests and exit triggers before committing irreversible money.
\end{itemize}

\section{Reading and analysis protocol}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=5pt]
\item Read the chapter learning objectives, then mark terms and claims that need primary evidence.
\item For each chapter theme, distinguish the entrepreneur decision, information required, parties affected and failure condition.
\item Write a one-paragraph synthesis in your own words; cite the textbook chapter and separate contemporary practice notes.
\item Complete the applied activity and attach the underlying evidence, calculations or interview log.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Worked application and deliverable}
\textbf{Business exercise.} Interview five potential customers and score three ideas against observable evidence.\par\textbf{Required submission.} Feasibility screen and evidence log. Include a dated assumption register and an explicit proceed/revise/stop recommendation.\par\textbf{Assurance checks.} Interviews, alternatives, operating capabilities, and stop rules.
\section{Common reasoning failures}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=3pt]
\item Confusing compliments with intent to buy
\item Assuming market size without a defined segment
\end{itemize}
\noindent\textbf{Correction:} require counterevidence and identify the responsible decision-maker before approving the next stage.
\section{Discussion prompt}
Which assumption would invalidate the venture fastest, and what low-cost test can falsify it?\par\medskip\textbf{Evidence expected:} at least two alternatives, one quantitative or independently checkable observation, a trade-off, and a decision threshold.
\section{Exit assessment}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=5pt]
\item Explain each term from the concept map without copying the textbook.
\item Defend the applied decision using an assumption, evidence and counterargument.
\item Show an objective test or inspection that could prove a key assumption wrong.
\item State what would change the recommendation and who has approval authority.
\end{enumerate}
