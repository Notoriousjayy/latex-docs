\section{Learning outcome}
Translate customer evidence into a business model and an evidence-backed plan.
\section{Prerequisites}
Read chapters 3, 5.\par\textbf{Required baseline:} ability to record assumptions and distinguish observations from opinions.
\section{Chapter concept map}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=3pt]
\item business model logic
\item lean startup experimentation
\item business plan purpose
\item investor scrutiny
\item risks and execution
\end{itemize}

\section{Technical study notes}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=3pt]
\item A business model explains how value is created, delivered and captured. A business plan tests whether those relationships are supported by evidence and execution capability.
\item Lean experimentation reduces uncertainty by testing the riskiest assumptions before heavy fixed investment rather than using the term as an excuse to skip planning.
\item Investor-facing claims require evidence strength, scenario assumptions, material downside disclosure and an explicit use of requested capital.
\end{itemize}

\section{Reading and analysis protocol}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=5pt]
\item Read the chapter learning objectives, then mark terms and claims that need primary evidence.
\item For each chapter theme, distinguish the entrepreneur decision, information required, parties affected and failure condition.
\item Write a one-paragraph synthesis in your own words; cite the textbook chapter and separate contemporary practice notes.
\item Complete the applied activity and attach the underlying evidence, calculations or interview log.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Worked application and deliverable}
\textbf{Business exercise.} Write an executive summary and assumption register with owners, tests and decisions.\par\textbf{Required submission.} Business model and executive summary. Include a dated assumption register and an explicit proceed/revise/stop recommendation.\par\textbf{Assurance checks.} Evidence for market, operations, team, funding and exit assumptions.
\section{Common reasoning failures}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=3pt]
\item Treating projections as observed facts
\item Leaving critical risks out of the pitch
\end{itemize}
\noindent\textbf{Correction:} require counterevidence and identify the responsible decision-maker before approving the next stage.
\section{Discussion prompt}
Which three claims must be supported before inviting outside capital?\par\medskip\textbf{Evidence expected:} at least two alternatives, one quantitative or independently checkable observation, a trade-off, and a decision threshold.
\section{Exit assessment}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=5pt]
\item Explain each term from the concept map without copying the textbook.
\item Defend the applied decision using an assumption, evidence and counterargument.
\item Show an objective test or inspection that could prove a key assumption wrong.
\item State what would change the recommendation and who has approval authority.
\end{enumerate}
