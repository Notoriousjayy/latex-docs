\section{Learning outcome}
Reconcile profit, financial position and cash movements without conflating them.
\section{Prerequisites}
Read chapters 6.\par\textbf{Required baseline:} ability to record assumptions and distinguish observations from opinions.
\section{Chapter concept map}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=3pt]
\item income statement
\item balance sheet
\item cash-flow statement
\item working capital
\item ratio interpretation
\end{itemize}

\section{Technical study notes}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=3pt]
\item An income statement measures financial performance during a period; a balance sheet captures the position at a date; a cash-flow statement bridges the cash account.
\item Selling on credit can increase revenue and receivables without increasing cash; investments and financing can change cash without becoming operating profit.
\item Ratios need consistent definitions and meaningful comparators. Liquidity alone does not prove long-term solvency or competitive advantage.
\end{itemize}

\section{Reading and analysis protocol}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=5pt]
\item Read the chapter learning objectives, then mark terms and claims that need primary evidence.
\item For each chapter theme, distinguish the entrepreneur decision, information required, parties affected and failure condition.
\item Write a one-paragraph synthesis in your own words; cite the textbook chapter and separate contemporary practice notes.
\item Complete the applied activity and attach the underlying evidence, calculations or interview log.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Worked application and deliverable}
\textbf{Business exercise.} Build and cross-check a simple three-statement model and short liquidity analysis.\par\textbf{Required submission.} Three-statement reconciliation. Include a dated assumption register and an explicit proceed/revise/stop recommendation.\par\textbf{Assurance checks.} Accounting identities, definitions, periods and reconciliation checks.
\section{Common reasoning failures}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=3pt]
\item Calling revenue cash received
\item Ignoring balance-sheet movements
\end{itemize}
\noindent\textbf{Correction:} require counterevidence and identify the responsible decision-maker before approving the next stage.
\section{Discussion prompt}
How can a profitable firm lack enough cash for payroll?\par\medskip\textbf{Evidence expected:} at least two alternatives, one quantitative or independently checkable observation, a trade-off, and a decision threshold.
\section{Exit assessment}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=5pt]
\item Explain each term from the concept map without copying the textbook.
\item Defend the applied decision using an assumption, evidence and counterargument.
\item Show an objective test or inspection that could prove a key assumption wrong.
\item State what would change the recommendation and who has approval authority.
\end{enumerate}
