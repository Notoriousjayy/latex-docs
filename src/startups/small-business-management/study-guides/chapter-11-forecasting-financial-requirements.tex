\section{Learning outcome}
Build a driver-based financial forecast and distinguish solvency from profitability.
\section{Prerequisites}
Read chapters 7, 10.\par\textbf{Required baseline:} ability to record assumptions and distinguish observations from opinions.
\section{Chapter concept map}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=3pt]
\item pro forma profitability
\item asset needs
\item financing gap
\item cash-flow projection
\item cash budget
\end{itemize}

\section{Technical study notes}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=3pt]
\item Pro forma income predicts accounting performance; a cash budget predicts payment capacity. Both can be right while implying different near-term decisions.
\item Forecast additional assets and funding as business drivers change, including inventory, receivables and capital purchases, not only expenses.
\item Collection timing, payment terms and minimum-cash requirements usually dominate the earliest funding shortfall for a growing firm.
\end{itemize}

\section{Reading and analysis protocol}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=5pt]
\item Read the chapter learning objectives, then mark terms and claims that need primary evidence.
\item For each chapter theme, distinguish the entrepreneur decision, information required, parties affected and failure condition.
\item Write a one-paragraph synthesis in your own words; cite the textbook chapter and separate contemporary practice notes.
\item Complete the applied activity and attach the underlying evidence, calculations or interview log.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Worked application and deliverable}
\textbf{Business exercise.} Create a 13-week cash forecast with minimum-cash threshold and financing trigger.\par\textbf{Required submission.} Cash budget and financing gap analysis. Include a dated assumption register and an explicit proceed/revise/stop recommendation.\par\textbf{Assurance checks.} Base/downside/upside, assumptions, sensitivity, minimum cash.
\section{Common reasoning failures}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=3pt]
\item Forecasting sales without collections lag
\item Hiding uncertain estimates behind decimals
\end{itemize}
\noindent\textbf{Correction:} require counterevidence and identify the responsible decision-maker before approving the next stage.
\section{Discussion prompt}
Which sales/collection assumption causes the earliest cash deficit?\par\medskip\textbf{Evidence expected:} at least two alternatives, one quantitative or independently checkable observation, a trade-off, and a decision threshold.
\section{Exit assessment}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=5pt]
\item Explain each term from the concept map without copying the textbook.
\item Defend the applied decision using an assumption, evidence and counterargument.
\item Show an objective test or inspection that could prove a key assumption wrong.
\item State what would change the recommendation and who has approval authority.
\end{enumerate}
