\section{Buying policy: simplest adequate kit first}
The source manual's \emph{Tools and Supplies} chapter centers its process on a portable apron, carryall tray, cleaning cloths, two all-purpose/glass spray categories (\emph{Red Juice} and \emph{Blue Juice}), detail brush, non-scratch pad, mop and vacuum. These are \emph{functions}, not an endorsement that any particular branded chemical is appropriate for every surface. The substitutions below are independently selected budget examples.\sources{Manual pp. 4--9 and room kit lists pp. 10, 25 and 42.}
\begin{notebox}\textbf{Procurement goal:} cover routine soap/grease soil, loose dust, glass smudges, bathroom deposits and floor debris with the fewest safe tools. Rent, borrow or retain an already working vacuum before buying an expensive replacement. Buy specialized bathroom descaler, stone care or disinfectant only where the surfaces / situation require it.\end{notebox}

\section{Life-cycle economics}
\subsection{Compare in-use costs, not only shelf price}
For a concentrate, \(V_c\) is concentrate volume, \(d\) is the number of parts water per one part concentrate as \emph{specified on its label}:
\[
V_{\text{ready to use}} = V_c(1+d),\qquad
C_{\text{fl oz}} = \frac{P_c}{V_c(1+d)}.
\]
\textbf{Illustration only (not a dilution instruction):} if a hypothetical labeled product allows 1 part concentrate to 9 parts water, a 128-fl-oz container yields 1,280 fl oz. A \$9.98 container then costs about \$0.0078 per prepared fl oz, excluding water, bottle and labor. Never apply the example dilution to a real cleaner without its label.
\[
\operatorname{CostPerService} = \sum_i (q_i p_i) + \frac{\text{durable tool cost}}{\text{expected service uses}} + \text{laundering cost}.
\]
\[
\operatorname{LifecycleCost}=\text{initial purchase}+\text{refills}+\text{consumables}+\text{water/energy}+\text{repair/replacement}.
\]
\textbf{Worked cloth comparison:} a \$18.98 48-pack is about \$0.40 per cloth before laundering. A cloth surviving 60 service uses has a purchase-only cost under \$0.01 per use, but wash cost, loss and early failure matter. \emph{Sixty uses is a modeling assumption, not a durability claim for the listed product.}
\subsection{Decision rule for alternatives}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Reject the alternative if incompatible with any designated surface or chemical safety requirement.
\item Normalize price by use (per cloth, per washable cover, per ready-to-use fluid ounce, per service hour).
\item Compare ease of use and cleanup: fewer passes, fewer residues and minimal replacement are valuable only if first-pass quality is preserved.
\item Prefer available generic/house brand when same function, surface compatibility, labeling and performance are sufficient.
\item Do not buy redundant \emph{polish, fragrance, bleach, disinfectant, specialty spray} without a documented need.
\end{enumerate}
\section{Procurement control: substitutions and exceptions}
\subsection{Categories not automatically included}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Bleach/disinfectant}: keep separate; add only under risk-based disinfection protocol, following EPA registration and label; no universal dilution or cross-mixing.
\item \textbf{Acid bath descaler}: obtain only for hard-water deposits and compatible surfaces; never on marble/limestone, and never combine with bleach.
\item \textbf{Abrasive cream, steel wool or razor blade}: no default stock for delicate domestic surfaces; use only as an explicitly approved finish-specific exception.
\item \textbf{Furniture polish}: optional only where finish allows and resident wants the finish; avoid silicone residue on incompatible surfaces.
\item \textbf{Vacuum upgrade}: purchase only after assessing existing suction, hard-floor modes, filters, storage, repair cost and household needs.
\end{itemize}
\begin{safetybox}\textbf{No assumed equivalence.} The legacy book's color nicknames \emph{Red Juice} and \emph{Blue Juice} are functional roles, not chemical specifications. Do not use color alone to identify a cleaner or infer disinfecting action. Avoid household ``recipes'' that mix vinegar, bleach, ammonia or cleaners.\end{safetybox}
