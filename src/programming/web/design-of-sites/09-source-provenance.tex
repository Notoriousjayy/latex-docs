\section{Evidence grading and the historical boundary}

\Provenance
\begin{groundedbox}Publisher and O'Reilly bibliographic records confirm the edition, authors, 13 groups and 107 pattern labels. The publicly visible publisher table of contents also confirms five foundational chapters and research appendixes. The full copyrighted pattern contents were \emph{not} retrieved or reproduced for this suite [B1--B2].\end{groundedbox}
\textbf{Evidence grades:}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{B: Book-index verified.} Original pattern IDs, pattern titles, group membership and historical book structure.
\item \textbf{S: Standards verified.} Claims directly supported by a current, versioned normative document or project reference.
\item \textbf{M: Modern extension.} Interpretation of present-day platform behavior or architecture based on S references; requires contextual adaptation.
\item \textbf{R: Recommendation.} Author-created implementation/testing guidance. Treat as a design proposal, not an attributable book claim.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Evidence coverage and limitations}

\begin{enumerate}
\item The available O'Reilly integration exposed a catalog search result, not the full readable book. The Pearson/InformIT publicly exposed detailed table of contents was used to verify all 107 headings.
\item A pattern's modern action and acceptance test are author-created \emph{inferences based on its title, family and current engineering practice}; they are not certified to describe the internal content of each historical pattern.
\item Examples are illustrative; security-sensitive code fragments omit production-specific dependencies and have not been integration-tested in an application.
\item Product-specific regulations, statutory accessibility obligations, tax and privacy rules, and payment compliance must be evaluated for the deployment jurisdiction and use case.
\item The source crosswalk is conceptual: design patterns cannot, by themselves, prove particular DDD or microservice boundaries.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Maintenance and editorial control}

\textbf{Proposed cadence:} review standards URLs and security-control references quarterly; review usability evidence and field metrics after each significant deployment; update the source-index mapping only when correcting transcription mistakes. Use a change log entry with owner, date and exact affected document/section.

\textbf{Approval roles:} product and UX approve user outcomes; frontend engineers approve component semantics; domain architects approve data/API boundaries; application security reviews risk; SRE reviews operability and rollback. This role assignment is a recommendation.
