\section{Choosing the right generating function}
Begin by answering four questions: what is the atom, what is size, is order relevant, and are labels distinguishable? Translating to an OGF or EGF without these answers loses the combinatorial meaning of multiplication and division by factorials.

\section{Formal power series: algebra without convergence}
A formal power series is determined coefficientwise; product coefficients satisfy $[z^n]A(z)B(z)=\sum_{k=0}^n a_k b_{n-k}$. Inversion needs a nonzero constant term. Analytic convergence matters only when later applying singularity analysis. Confusing formal and analytic claims is a recurring source of incorrect proofs.
\section{A complete Catalan derivation}
\subsection{Specification}
Consider ordered binary trees with $n$ internal nodes, counting the empty tree as size zero. Each nonempty tree is a root plus an ordered left and right subtree. Therefore $T(z)=1+zT(z)^2$. Set $U(z)=T(z)-1$ to get $U=z(1+U)^2$; Lagrange inversion gives
\[
[z^n]U(z)=\frac{1}{n}[u^{n-1}](1+u)^{2n}=\frac1n\binom{2n}{n-1}=\frac{1}{n+1}\binom{2n}{n}.
\]
Check $1,1,2,5,14,42$ at sizes $0\ldots5$. The dominant singularity $\rho=1/4$ produces the $4^n n^{-3/2}$ growth law with leading constant $1/\sqrt\pi$.
\section{Bivariate parameters and marked constructions}
Let $F(z,u)=\sum_{n,k}f_{n,k}z^nu^k$ where $u$ marks a statistic. The first derivative $\partial_u F(z,u)|_{u=1}$ sums that statistic over objects of size $n$. Under a uniform model over $f_n=[z^n]F(z,1)$ objects,
\[
\Expect[K_n]=\frac{[z^n]\partial_u F(z,u)|_{u=1}}{[z^n]F(z,1)}.
\]
For labelled objects use $z^n/n!$ instead; factorials cancel in the ratio. To compute variance, use $\Expect[K_n(K_n-1)]$ from the second derivative, then $\Var(K_n)=\Expect[K_n(K_n-1)]+\Expect[K_n]-\Expect[K_n]^2$.
\section{Singularity analysis decision procedure}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Locate the radius of convergence from denominators, discriminants, implicit-function breakdown, or coefficient growth.
\item Check whether singularities of minimum modulus are unique; if not, retain every contribution.
\item Establish a local expansion in a suitable analytic domain rather than inferring coefficients solely from a real-axis limit.
\item Apply a justified transfer theorem to each term; retain an explicit remainder estimate.
\item Verify the scale and constant numerically using stabilized ratios, preferably high precision.
\end{enumerate}
\section{Finite-$n$ checks and counterexamples}
For every symbolic claim, print the first eight coefficients and independently enumerate objects of size at most five. Include at least one counterexample model: unordered trees cannot be counted using the binary ordered-tree equation; random BSTs do not share the uniform Catalan-shape law; periodic generating functions can have multiple dominant singularities.
