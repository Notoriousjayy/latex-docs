\section{Guided technical analysis}
\subsection{Substitution and telescoping}
For $a_n=a_{n-1}+2n-1$ and $a_0=0$, summing the differences gives $a_n=\sum_{k=1}^n(2k-1)=n^2$. Verify $n=0$ and $1$ explicitly.
\subsection{Higher-order linear relations}
For $a_n=2a_{n-1}+a_{n-2}$ with $a_0=0,a_1=1$, the characteristic roots are $1\pm\sqrt2$ and
\[
a_n=\frac{(1+\sqrt2)^n-(1-\sqrt2)^n}{2\sqrt2}.
\]
The growing root determines the leading exponential rate; the other root is necessary for the exact formula and initial conditions.
\subsection{Divide and conquer}
For $T(2^k)=2T(2^{k-1})+2^k$, $T(1)=0$, division by $2^k$ yields the exact $T(n)=n\log_2 n$ on powers of two. For arbitrary $n$, replace idealized halving with its true floor/ceiling recurrence, then derive valid estimates rather than assuming exactness.
