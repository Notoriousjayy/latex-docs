\section{Athlete Assessment and Assumptions}

Jordan's six scheduled boxing and swimming sessions, plus running, create a comparatively high total workload. The plan therefore emphasizes targeted mobility, brief minimum-effective sessions, and one longer recovery sequence instead of prescribing a full-body stretch after every workout.

\midrule

Swimming & Shoulders, upper back, thoracic movement, chest, lats, hips, and lower back. \\
Running & Hamstrings, quadriceps, gluteals, hip flexors, adductors, calves, and ankle dorsiflexion. \\

\section{Key Programming Principles}

\begin{callout}{Machine rules from Precor guidance}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Use the wrist straps every time, but never depend on the straps without holding the handlebars.
  \item Warm up and begin conservatively. Keep the arms straight in the supported positions.
  \item Keep the knees supported and the feet placed as the placard shows for each seated stretch.
  \item Move slowly and smoothly. Do not bounce, force the stretch, or rock the equipment.
  \item Use the abdominal and gluteal muscles to enter and leave the stretch instead of pulling back with the arms.
  \item Breathe normally and return the seat to its forward position when finished.
\end{itemize}
\end{callout}

\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}L{1.55in}Y@{}}
\toprule
\rowcolor{Midnight}
\color{white}\textbf{Principle} & \color{white}\textbf{Application} \\
\midrule
Intensity & Stop at a mild-to-moderate stretch, approximately 3--4/10. Joint pain is never a target sensation. \\
Before explosive work & Prioritize dynamic mobility. Use a 10-second machine hold only when a specific restriction interferes with comfortable mechanics, then reactivate dynamically. \\
After training & Use 20--30 second static holds after breathing and heart rate have substantially settled. \\
Progression & Change duration, frequency, repetitions, or range one at a time. \\
Symmetry & Use equal time on both sides for the first four weeks. Record repeatable differences rather than forcing the tighter side. \\
Recovery & Longer sessions belong after training or on recovery days, not immediately before sparring, sprinting, hills, or hard intervals. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}

Short static holds under 60 seconds generally show little effect on maximal performance in the research literature; nevertheless, the dynamic emphasis in this plan is the more sport-specific choice for boxing, hard running, and intense swimming. Stretching can improve range of motion, but it should not be treated as a guarantee against injury.

\section{Precor 240i Exercise Reference}

The following setup language paraphrases the Precor instructional placard and owner's manual. Exact contact points can differ across placard revisions; use the placard attached to the machine whenever a discrepancy appears.

\begin{stretchbox}{1. Lower Back}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}L{1.28in}Y@{}}
\textbf{Primary emphasis} & Lower-back region and surrounding trunk musculature. \\
\textbf{Why for Jordan} & May help restore comfortable movement after the boxing guard, repetitive running impact, and sustained swimming positions. \\
\textbf{Placard setup} & Place the seat forward. Attach the wrist straps; hold the upper bar with palms down. Support both knees as shown, keep the lower legs aligned with the pads, and place the feet correctly. \\
\textbf{Controlled action} & Keep the arms straight. Allow the seat to move only through the placard-demonstrated path until mild tension appears. Use the abdomen and gluteals to control both directions; do not pull with the arms. \\
\textbf{Dose} & Pre-training only if needed: 10 sec at 2--3/10. Post/recovery: 20--30 sec, 1--2 sets. Bilateral. \\
\textbf{Breathing} & Exhale gently into the position; continue two to four normal breaths. \\
\textbf{Common errors} & Pulling with the arms, bouncing, holding the breath, or forcing spinal rounding. \\
\textbf{Easier / progress} & Reduce seat travel. Increase duration before range, then add a second set if needed. \\
\textbf{Stop signs} & Sharp back pain, radiating leg pain, numbness, tingling, or unusual weakness. \\
\end{tabularx}
\end{stretchbox}

\begin{stretchbox}{2. Hamstrings}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}L{1.28in}Y@{}}
\textbf{Primary emphasis} & Hamstrings and posterior thigh. \\
\textbf{Why for Jordan} & Supports comfortable running stride, boxing stance changes, and posterior-chain recovery. \\
\textbf{Placard setup} & Attach the straps and hold the lower bar palms down. Extend one leg straight along the path shown on the placard; point or flex the foot. Keep the other knee supported. Use the upper bar only as the placard's more intense option after control is established. \\
\textbf{Controlled action} & Keep the working knee straight without force. Move the seat smoothly until posterior-thigh tension appears, return under trunk control, and switch legs. \\
\textbf{Dose} & 10--30 sec per side, 1--2 sets. Intensity 3--4/10 after training. \\
\textbf{Breathing} & Slow exhale on entry; relaxed breathing during the hold. \\
\textbf{Common errors} & Aggressively locking the knee, reaching with the shoulders, bouncing, or forcing the toes. \\
\textbf{Easier / progress} & Reduce leg extension or seat travel. Progress duration first; change to the upper-bar option only later. \\
\textbf{Stop signs} & Burning, electrical, or tingling sensations; posterior-knee pain; or symptoms traveling into the calf or foot. \\
\end{tabularx}
\end{stretchbox}

\begin{stretchbox}{3. Gluteals and Hips}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}L{1.28in}Y@{}}
\textbf{Primary emphasis} & Gluteal region and hip rotators. \\
\textbf{Why for Jordan} & Relevant to boxing rotation and stance changes, swimming kick mechanics, and running stride control. \\
\textbf{Placard setup} & Hold the lower bar palms down. Place the working ankle on the top edge of the kneepad, or at the knee/thigh position shown on the machine's placard for the available range. Keep the support side stable. \\
\textbf{Controlled action} & Maintain a controlled pelvis and move only until a stretch is felt in the buttock or outer hip. Complete the hold and switch legs. \\
\textbf{Dose} & 10--30 sec per side, 1--2 sets. \\
\textbf{Breathing} & Exhale into the position; keep the jaw and shoulders relaxed. \\
\textbf{Common errors} & Pressing the knee downward, twisting sharply, collapsing the chest, or forcing the ankle position. \\
\textbf{Easier / progress} & Use the least demanding placard-supported ankle position. Progress hold time before moving the ankle higher. \\
\textbf{Stop signs} & Hip pinching, sharp groin discomfort, knee pain, or numbness. \\
\end{tabularx}
\end{stretchbox}

\begin{stretchbox}{4. Hips, Legs, and Back}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}L{1.28in}Y@{}}
\textbf{Primary emphasis} & Integrated diagonal line through the hip, posterior leg, and back. \\
\textbf{Why for Jordan} & Matches the diagonal and rotational demands common to punching, footwork, swimming, and running. \\
\textbf{Placard setup} & Hold the upper bar palms down. Place both hands left of center and extend the left leg toward the right side of center. Point or flex the foot. Mirror both the leg and hand positions for the other side. \\
\textbf{Controlled action} & Maintain the placard alignment and move slowly until moderate tension appears. Return under control before changing sides. \\
\textbf{Dose} & 10--30 sec per side, 1--2 sets. \\
\textbf{Breathing} & Breathe into the back and side ribs without holding the breath. \\
\textbf{Common errors} & Changing only the leg but not the hand position, rotating abruptly, or chasing depth. \\
\textbf{Easier / progress} & Reduce the diagonal leg reach and seat travel. Progress duration, then comfortable reach. \\
\textbf{Stop signs} & Back pain, nerve-like leg symptoms, hip pinching, or loss of control. \\
\end{tabularx}
\end{stretchbox}

\begin{stretchbox}{5. Inner Thighs and Groin}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}L{1.28in}Y@{}}
\textbf{Primary emphasis} & Hip adductors and groin region. \\
\textbf{Why for Jordan} & Supports lateral boxing movement, stance transitions, and lower-limb control during running and swimming. \\
\textbf{Placard setup} & Hold the lower bar palms down. With the seat forward, place the feet facing one another on the top edge of the kneepad exactly as the placard shows. \\
\textbf{Controlled action} & Keep the arms straight and move slowly until mild-to-moderate inner-thigh tension appears. Do not press the knees down. \\
\textbf{Dose} & 10--30 sec, 1--2 sets. Bilateral. \\
\textbf{Breathing} & Relax the abdomen and exhale without collapsing the trunk. \\
\textbf{Common errors} & Bouncing, forcing the knees apart, losing foot contact, or ignoring medial-knee pressure. \\
\textbf{Easier / progress} & Use less seat movement. Progress time before range. \\
\textbf{Stop signs} & Sharp groin pain, medial-knee pain, catching, or instability. \\
\end{tabularx}
\end{stretchbox}

\begin{stretchbox}{6. Upper Back}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}L{1.28in}Y@{}}
\textbf{Primary emphasis} & Upper-back and thoracic region. \\
\textbf{Why for Jordan} & Supports comfortable posture, scapular movement, punching recovery, and swimming mechanics. \\
\textbf{Placard setup} & Attach the straps and hold the upper bar with palms facing up. Maintain supported knees and straight arms. \\
\textbf{Controlled action} & Move into the supported stretch slowly. Keep the gaze neutral initially. The placard permits looking up or gently right or left to change emphasis, but only after the neutral version is symptom-free. \\
\textbf{Dose} & 10--30 sec, 1--2 sets. Use only one gaze direction per repetition. \\
\textbf{Breathing} & Direct relaxed breaths into the upper back and side ribs. \\
\textbf{Common errors} & Shrugging, excessive neck extension, bouncing, or pulling hard with the hands. \\
\textbf{Easier / progress} & Keep the gaze neutral and reduce seat travel. Add a gentle gaze variation only later. \\
\textbf{Stop signs} & Neck pain, dizziness, headache, arm tingling, numbness, or shoulder pain. \\
\end{tabularx}
\end{stretchbox}

\begin{stretchbox}{7. Shoulders}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}L{1.28in}Y@{}}
\textbf{Primary emphasis} & Shoulder region and adjacent upper-back tissue. \\
\textbf{Why for Jordan} & Directly relevant to punching volume, guard posture, and repeated overhead swimming. \\
\textbf{Placard setup} & Cross the arms and grasp the upper bar with palms down. Complete the stretch, then reverse the crossed-hand position. \\
\textbf{Controlled action} & Keep the shoulders away from the ears and move only to a comfortable stretch. The placard allows looking up or gently right or left to intensify the position; begin with a neutral gaze. \\
\textbf{Dose} & 10--30 sec per crossed-hand position, 1 set initially. \\
\textbf{Breathing} & Exhale slowly and avoid bracing the neck or jaw. \\
\textbf{Common errors} & Forcing the crossed grip, shrugging, twisting the neck, or stretching through front-shoulder pinching. \\
\textbf{Easier / progress} & Reduce seat travel and keep the gaze neutral. Progress hold duration before adding a gaze change. \\
\textbf{Stop signs} & Pinching, instability, sharp shoulder pain, arm numbness, tingling, or loss of strength. \\
\end{tabularx}
\end{stretchbox}

\begin{stretchbox}{8. Quadriceps}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}L{1.28in}Y@{}}
\textbf{Primary emphasis} & Quadriceps, with potential emphasis on rectus femoris depending on hip position. \\
\textbf{Why for Jordan} & Supports recovery from running, boxing footwork, kicking, and repeated knee extension. \\
\textbf{Placard setup} & Flip the seat back. Stand beside the machine facing forward as the placard shows. Hold the bar for support, place the top of one foot on the top edge of the kneepad, keep the back straight, and bend the supporting leg. \\
\textbf{Controlled action} & Keep both knees tracking comfortably and the pelvis controlled. Bend only far enough to create a stretch in the front of the supported thigh. Switch legs. \\
\textbf{Dose} & 10--30 sec per side, 1--2 sets. \\
\textbf{Breathing} & Exhale gently; maintain an upright trunk without breath-holding. \\
\textbf{Common errors} & Arching the lower back, rotating the knee, pushing through kneecap pain, or releasing the support bar. \\
\textbf{Easier / progress} & Bend the supporting leg less. Progress duration before depth. \\
\textbf{Stop signs} & Knee pain, loss of balance, cramping, hip pinching, or an unstable support surface. \\
\end{tabularx}
\end{stretchbox}

\section{Progress Tracking}

Repeat the three checks every two weeks under similar conditions. Use the same warm-up, time of day when practical, and testing order.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Wall shoulder-flexion check.} Keep the back and ribs against a wall; raise both arms overhead without arching. Record comfort, symmetry, and whether the hands approach the wall.
  \item \textbf{Active straight-leg raise.} Lie on the back and raise one straight leg without lifting the opposite leg or pelvis. Record comfortable range and symmetry.
  \item \textbf{Knee-to-wall ankle check.} Keep the heel down while moving the knee toward a wall. Record the greatest comfortable toe-to-wall distance on each side.
\end{enumerate}

The objective is smoother motion, improved comfort, and reduced asymmetry--not a forced maximum score.

\section{Safety and Adjustment Rules}

\subsection{Before every use}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Confirm that the machine is stable, dry, and not marked out of service.
  \item Inspect the wrist straps, pads, handles, placard, and visible fasteners.
  \item Wear the wrist straps and maintain an active grip on the handlebars.
  \item Obtain an in-person demonstration if any placard position is unclear.
\end{itemize}
