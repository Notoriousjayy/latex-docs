\section{Source boundary}
Chapters 18--26 and 37--45 explore matter, forces, flexible or articulated motion, collisions, drag, weight, and repetition. This reference adds numerical simulation practices and automated checks not attributable to the book.
\section{Motion and integration}
Newtonian translation follows $m\ddot{x}=\sum F$. A ballistic vertical example is $y(t)=y_0+v_0t+\tfrac12gt^2$ with $g<0$ in an upward-positive coordinate system. Semi-implicit Euler evaluates $v_{k+1}=v_k+a_kh$ followed by $x_{k+1}=x_k+v_{k+1}h$. Explicit Euler reverses the position update order and has different energy behavior.
\section{Fixed-step accumulator}
Accumulate wall-clock $dt$ and advance simulation with a fixed $h$ while accumulator $\ge h$. Cap steps per render to prevent unbounded catch-up; report clamped time or slow-motion behavior instead of dropping silent simulation work. Interpolate visual transforms with $\alpha=\mathrm{accumulator}/h$ to decouple physics and display.
\section{Mass, impulse, and spring}
\[J=\Delta p=m(v^+-v^-),\qquad \ddot{x}+2\zeta\omega_0\dot{x}+\omega_0^2(x-x_*)=0.\]
Under $0<\zeta<1$ the spring is underdamped, $\zeta=1$ critically damped, and $\zeta>1$ overdamped. Integration can diverge even with physically valid inputs if $h$ is too large. Constrain parameters and test stability empirically.
\section{Contact and animation blending}
Contact-sensitive locomotion needs foot pinning, root-motion reconciliation, and collision-safe transitions. Do not assume linear blending between poses preserves joint limits, penetration constraints, or world-space contacts. Decide who owns locomotion displacement: physics body, animation root, or a reconciler. A single owner prevents double application.
\section{Evaluation experiments}
Hold start state and $h$ constant while varying damping or drag; record max overshoot, settling time, endpoint drift, and frame-rate invariance. Benchmark with deterministic initial conditions and a declared floating-point tolerance. Verify total simulated time rather than number of render callbacks.
\section{Failure catalog}
Unstable large time steps; frame-rate-dependent gravity; tunneling through thin colliders; discontinuity at animation-physics handoff; double root motion; arbitrary rotations interpolated without coordinate conventions.
