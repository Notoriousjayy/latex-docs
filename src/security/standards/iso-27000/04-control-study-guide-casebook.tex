\section{Study method}
Use each prompt as an oral examination or case-based design question. A correct answer should establish intended outcome, accountable owner, scoped assets, implementation strategy, evidence of operation, an adversarial test and exception handling. Exact ISO wording must be reviewed in the licensed source.

\subsection{Lab 1: Unmanaged secrets in code}
\textbf{Relevant references:} 8.4, 8.5, 8.8, 8.24, 8.25, 8.28, 8.29.\par\textbf{Scenario:} A deployment token is found in a public historical commit; service remains active.\par\textbf{Deliverable:} Define risk owner, revocation, history remediation, retrospective, repository preventive controls, evidence and re-test.\par\textbf{Grading anchors:} The proposed answer must contain a decision owner, a risk scenario, selected controls and SoA impact, an executable test, and objective proof.\par
\subsection{Lab 2: Supplier dependency compromise}
\textbf{Relevant references:} 5.19--5.23, 8.25, 8.30.\par\textbf{Scenario:} A supplier publishes a signed but malicious package update used by a customer application.\par\textbf{Deliverable:} Describe supplier tiering, provenance, release gate, incident path, alternate supplier and contractual obligations.\par\textbf{Grading anchors:} The proposed answer must contain a decision owner, a risk scenario, selected controls and SoA impact, an executable test, and objective proof.\par
\subsection{Lab 3: Cloud access escalation}
\textbf{Relevant references:} 5.15--5.18, 5.23, 8.2--8.5, 8.15.\par\textbf{Scenario:} A CI role can grant itself administrator privileges in a production subscription.\par\textbf{Deliverable:} Design SoD, short-lived credentials, policy-as-code, anomaly alerts and an effective negative authorization test.\par\textbf{Grading anchors:} The proposed answer must contain a decision owner, a risk scenario, selected controls and SoA impact, an executable test, and objective proof.\par
\subsection{Lab 4: Data classification and egress}
\textbf{Relevant references:} 5.12--5.14, 8.3, 8.11--8.12.\par\textbf{Scenario:} Sensitive PII appears in development logs and an externally shared analytics report.\par\textbf{Deliverable:} Identify controller, retention, de-identification, DLP, lawful handling and corrective actions.\par\textbf{Grading anchors:} The proposed answer must contain a decision owner, a risk scenario, selected controls and SoA impact, an executable test, and objective proof.\par
\subsection{Lab 5: Ransomware in critical operations}
\textbf{Relevant references:} 5.24--5.30, 8.7, 8.13--8.16.\par\textbf{Scenario:} Backup monitoring is green but no recovery test has run in 18 months.\par\textbf{Deliverable:} Explain why existence of backups does not show restore readiness; design exercise and acceptance criteria.\par\textbf{Grading anchors:} The proposed answer must contain a decision owner, a risk scenario, selected controls and SoA impact, an executable test, and objective proof.\par
\subsection{Lab 6: Physical system failure}
\textbf{Relevant references:} 7.1--7.14, 5.29--5.30.\par\textbf{Scenario:} Facility cooling fails during a maintenance window for a primary server room.\par\textbf{Deliverable:} Model site/utility dependency, alarms, protected maintenance access and tested failover.\par\textbf{Grading anchors:} The proposed answer must contain a decision owner, a risk scenario, selected controls and SoA impact, an executable test, and objective proof.\par

Control design & Tool only & Procedure described & Complete workflow with preventive/detective effect\\

\section{Preparation checklist}
\begin{itemize}
\item Explain 27000 terminology and 27001 conformance basis without conflating them with 27002 guidance.
\item Know 37+8+14+34 themes and how 27002 attributes provide cross-cutting views.
\item Distinguish implementation evidence from design statements and distinguish risk acceptance from risk elimination.
\item Demonstrate a control in a lab and test its failure path.
\item Explain why an excluded Annex A control requires justification even when not selected as necessary.
\end{itemize}
