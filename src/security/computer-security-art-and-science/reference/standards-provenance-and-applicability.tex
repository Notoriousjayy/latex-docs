\section{Reference hierarchy}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Primary book (historical foundation).} Bishop, \emph{Computer Security: Art and Science}, 2nd ed., Pearson, 2019, ISBN 978-0-321-71233-2. Chapter titles and sequence are used for a nonreproducing independent study companion.
\item \textbf{Final authoritative guidance.} Reference current publication status, dated edition and publisher landing page; adopt only requirements applicable to the system's legal and contractual scope.
\item \textbf{Threat intelligence/knowledge bases.} ATT\&CK describes observed adversary behaviors and possible detection hypotheses; it is not a compliance checklist or a claim of compromise.
\item \textbf{Local engineering policy.} Severity rules, scoring thresholds, ownership models and CI enforcement choices are proposed project decisions, not claims from the book or standard.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Version and citation control}
Every mapped statement records publication title, edition, exact control/requirement identifier where verified, URL, review date, owner, applicability, local implementation, assessment method, evidence pointer and status. A thematic association is marked \emph{illustrative}; it does not become a normative equivalence unless an authoritative mapping supports it.
\section{Book-to-practice interpretation rules}
Book-derived models explain why a property matters or what conditions it requires. The supplement specifies how to build evidence and select contemporary controls. Historical protocols and cryptographic primitives are for study, not a recommendation to deploy obsolete algorithms. Do not include copyrighted standard text or the uploaded textbook in this public repository.
