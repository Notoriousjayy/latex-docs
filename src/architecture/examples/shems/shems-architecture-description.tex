\begin{notebox}{Purpose and status}
This document develops a concrete example for a fictitious residential energy
management platform. It connects stakeholder questions to viewpoints, views,
design decisions, and review evidence. All system behavior and technology
selections below are illustrative proposals. This outline does not demonstrate
conformance to ISO/IEC/IEEE 42010:2022.
\end{notebox}

\section{Identity, purpose and boundary}
SHEMS coordinates household generation, storage, and controllable loads to
reduce energy cost while respecting homeowner preferences. ISO/IEC/IEEE 42010
addresses the description of an architecture and does not prescribe its design,
notation, or security technology.~\cite{iso-catalogue}

{\small

\toprule
\tablehead{Record} & \tablehead{Illustrative specification}\\
\midrule
Description identity & SHEMS-AD-001; revision 1.0; proposed target architecture.\\
Entity of interest & SHEMS v1.0, including the local gateway, control endpoints,
cloud optimizer, and application data store.\\
Ownership and roles & Homeowner: household assets. SHEMS service organization:
service operation and AD stewardship. Utility: external integration partner.\\
In scope & Meter-data ingestion, scheduling, authorized load control,
telemetry, remote services, and software maintenance.\\
External environment & Utility services; household meter, solar and battery
interfaces; homeowner; internet connectivity.\\
Boundary assumption & SHEMS controls only explicitly enrolled loads. External
metering and power equipment retain their own protection functions.\\

\subsection{How to use this outline}
Read the concern register first, then the viewpoint specifications and example
views. Use the correspondence register to connect representations, and the
decision record to identify assumptions that still require evidence. The final
page provides a review map and source references.

\section{Viewpoint specifications}
\pageintro{Each viewpoint below defines a project convention for constructing and
reviewing its associated view. The view content appears on the following pages.}

\begin{specbox}{VP-01\enspace Functional and energy optimization}
\small
\textbf{Audience and coverage:} SH-01, SH-02; P-01, P-02; C-01, C-03;
aspects A-01 and A-02. Governs \id{V-01}.

\textbf{Representation:} A project-defined logical topology with stable element
and flow identifiers, an ordered control scenario, and an energy-cost model.
Direction, boundaries, and timing assumptions are stated in the legend or text.

\textbf{Analysis:} Trace each scheduling input to its source; compare candidate
schedules with a declared cost baseline; check homeowner constraints and
unfulfilled-request reporting.
\end{specbox}

\begin{specbox}{VP-02\enspace Security and privacy}
\small
\textbf{Audience and coverage:} SH-01, SH-03, SH-04; P-01, P-03; C-02, C-05;
aspects A-01, A-03, and A-04. Governs \id{V-02}.

\textbf{Representation:} A flow-protection table, role-permission table, and
identity lifecycle description. Tables reuse V-01 identifiers and distinguish
selected controls from unresolved choices.

\textbf{Analysis:} Review trust crossings, authentication and authorization,
credential renewal and revocation, replay handling, and household-data access
and retention.
\end{specbox}

\begin{specbox}{VP-03\enspace Operational resilience}
\small
\textbf{Audience and coverage:} SH-01, SH-02, SH-04; P-01, P-02;
C-03, C-04, C-06; aspects A-01 and A-02. Governs \id{V-03}.

\textbf{Representation:} A failure-scenario table recording the triggering
condition, proposed response, and evidence needed to assess that response.

\textbf{Analysis:} Exercise connectivity loss, expired schedules, missing
telemetry, unreachable loads, and recovery. Distinguish request acceptance
from evidence that the requested load reduction occurred.
\end{specbox}

\begin{specbox}{VP-04\enspace Deployment and maintenance}
\small
\textbf{Audience and coverage:} SH-03, SH-04; P-03; C-05, C-06;
aspect A-04. Governs \id{V-04}.

\textbf{Representation:} An update sequence, version inventory, and recovery
record that identify device cohorts, image provenance, and observed health.

\textbf{Analysis:} Check compatibility before rollout, verify image signatures,
evaluate staged deployment results, and exercise recovery to an authorized
image without defeating version-security policy.
\end{specbox}

\textbf{Common convention.} Each view component records its source, revision,
governing representation, and legend. A diagram type alone is insufficient to
explain the meaning of its elements or the questions it is intended to answer.

\subsection{VC-02: proposed operating scenario}
\begin{enumerate}
\item GW-01 acquires household measurements through F-01. A nominal five-second
sample interval is an example target, subject to interface capability and testing.
\item OPT-01 combines permitted telemetry, tariff inputs, and requested
participation to propose a time-bounded schedule.
\item GW-01 checks the schedule against enrolled loads, local constraints,
expiry, and homeowner overrides before issuing F-03 commands.
\item Feedback updates the observed execution state. Reports distinguish an
accepted request from confirmed execution and measured energy outcomes.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Savings method.} Compare actual or simulated cost with a declared
counterfactual using the same tariff period and documented assumptions. Account
for exported energy, storage losses, and relevant operating costs. No savings
percentage is asserted in this example.

\textbf{Notation choice.} A SysML 1.x block definition diagram describes structural
types and relationships; internal block, activity, or sequence representations
can expose interfaces, processing, and interactions.~\cite{omg-sysml}

\section{V-02: security and privacy view}
\pageintro{View components: VC-03 flow protection; VC-04 permissions and identity lifecycle.}

\subsection{VC-03: protection of the identified flows}
{\small
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}L{0.59in}L{1.34in}Y@{}}
\toprule
\tablehead{Flow} & \tablehead{Trust crossing} & \tablehead{Proposed treatment and status}\\
\midrule
F-01 & Equipment to gateway & Document the actual meter, solar, and battery protocols.
Identity and protection mechanisms remain device-dependent and unresolved.\\
F-02 & Household to cloud & Propose TLS 1.3 with mutual authentication, unique gateway
identity, and account-scoped authorization for telemetry and schedules.\\
F-03 & Gateway to endpoints & Select a supported authenticated protocol with integrity
and replay protection. Inventory and interoperability evidence are still needed.\\
F-04 & Service to data store & Use an authenticated service identity, least-privilege
data permissions, and protected transport where this is a network connection.\\
F-05 & User to local service & Authenticate the homeowner and authorize changes against
the household enrollment and preference records.\\
F-06 & Utility to cloud & Authenticate the integration, authorize participation,
validate request scope and expiry, and report the outcome.\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}}

TLS 1.3 provides channel confidentiality and integrity with peer authentication;
client authentication must be deliberately configured.~\cite{tls13}
AES-128 names a block cipher, not a complete device-authentication protocol.
Authenticated-encryption modes such as GCM add defined integrity properties,
but do not replace identity establishment or authorization.~\cite{aes,gcm}

\subsection{VC-04: permissions and identity lifecycle}
{\small
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}L{1.12in}Y@{}}
\toprule
\tablehead{Role} & \tablehead{Illustrative authority and boundary}\\
\midrule
Homeowner & View own household data; enroll loads; set preferences; override scheduling.\\
Utility partner & Submit requests within enrolled participation terms; receive the
agreed status and reporting data; no general household administration.\\
Maintainer & Deploy approved software to assigned device cohorts; no unrestricted
access to household energy histories.\\
Service operator & Inspect service health and necessary diagnostic metadata;
use separately authorized access for sensitive support investigations.\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Identity:} Bind a device to the correct household during enrollment;
record credential issuance, renewal, revocation, and device removal.
\item \textbf{Command handling:} Check authorization, freshness, and duplicate
identifiers before execution. Do not use TLS 0-RTT for actuator-changing requests
in this proposed design; early data has replay limitations.~\cite{tls13}
\item \textbf{Privacy:} Define collection granularity, allowed uses, retention,
deletion, and support access. The retention period is an open decision.
\end{itemize}

\section{V-03 and V-04: operation and maintenance}
\pageintro{View components: VC-05 failure scenarios; VC-06 update sequence and deployment record.}

\subsection{VC-05: operational resilience scenarios}
{\small
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}L{1.08in}Y L{1.33in}@{}}
\toprule
\tablehead{Condition} & \tablehead{Proposed architectural response} & \tablehead{Evidence needed}\\
\midrule
Cloud connection lost & GW-01 retains local preference enforcement and overrides;
uses a still-valid approved schedule within its limits. & Disconnect and restore
connectivity during a control scenario.\\
Schedule expires & Stop initiating actions from the expired schedule and enter a
documented local fallback policy. & Expiry and clock-error scenarios;
fallback policy review.\\
Telemetry becomes stale & Mark data quality; suspend decisions that require fresh
measurements; report the degraded condition. & Missing, delayed, and
out-of-order sample scenarios.\\
Endpoint unreachable & Record the action as unconfirmed; do not report the requested
reduction as achieved without evidence. & Lost-command and missing-feedback
scenarios.\\
Service recovers & Reconcile observed device state with pending requests; reject
expired or already executed actions. & Reconnection, duplicate-delivery,
and restart scenarios.\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}}

\textbf{Parameters to resolve:} maximum telemetry age, schedule validity,
retry limits, outage detection, recovery objectives, and per-load fallback
behavior. These require stakeholder agreement and device evidence; no numerical
service guarantee is implied by this outline.

\subsection{VC-06: deployment and maintenance sequence}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Inventory and qualify.} Record hardware type, installed firmware,
protocol capabilities, device ownership, and the supported upgrade path.
\item \textbf{Authorize the release.} Identify the approved image, its signature,
target cohort, compatibility requirements, and recovery constraints.
\item \textbf{Deploy in stages.} Begin with a limited cohort; assess device health
and continued control behavior before expanding the rollout.
\item \textbf{Recover deliberately.} On failure, select an authorized compatible
image that satisfies the version-security policy. Preserve enough diagnostic
evidence to explain the failure.
\item \textbf{Reconcile the record.} Update observed versions and rollout status;
identify devices needing intervention and notify the operational role.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{notebox}{Evidence connects maintenance to the architecture}
\small
Record a release identifier, target device IDs, observed versions, signature
verification results, rollout health, and recovery outcomes. Relate these
records to C-05 and C-06 so that maintainability is assessable beyond a diagram.
\end{notebox}

relationship. Checking that every communication flow has such an entry is a
coverage rule. A populated entry can still identify an unresolved design choice.

\subsection{Decision D-01: hybrid control and optimization}
\textbf{Proposed choice.} Keep immediate preference enforcement, control
validation, and fallback behavior in GW-01. Use OPT-01 for tariff-aware planning
and longer-horizon optimization.

\textbf{Alternatives.} Cloud-led control would concentrate decision logic but
increase dependence on connectivity. Edge-only optimization would reduce that
dependency while shifting algorithm execution and maintenance to household
equipment. The hybrid option divides those responsibilities.

\textbf{Rationale and consequences.} The split addresses C-01 and C-04, but
requires schedule validity, state reconciliation, and consistent policy versions.
Lower cost or acceptable latency must be supported by workload measurements,
device capability, outage behavior, and a total-cost comparison. Neither benefit
is presumed here.
