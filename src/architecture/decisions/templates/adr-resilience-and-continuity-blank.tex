\section{Domain-specific decision inputs}
Complete the following prompts with referenceable designs, measurements,
threat assessments or operational constraints; mark inapplicable prompts
explicitly rather than removing necessary decision context.
\begin{itemize}
\item Business impact tiers, user journeys, dependency graph, correlated failure and blast radius.
\item Failure domains: zones, regions, networks, identity control planes, DNS, storage and third parties.
\item Availability target, error budget, recovery time objective (RTO) and recovery point objective (RPO).
\item Redundancy topology, failover policy, replication/consistency behavior, backup isolation and restoration runbook.
\item Chaos/failure testing scope, incident command roles, rollback and planned failback.
\end{itemize}
\section{Domain-specific alternatives and trade-offs}

\begin{itemize}
\item Recovery objectives against measured recovery performance and budget.
\item Single versus multi-region, active-passive versus active-active, complexity versus availability.
\item Data integrity after split-brain, partial failure, stale reads and failback.
\end{itemize}

\section{Domain-specific validation and review gates}
The proposed decision is ready for acceptance only after its applicable
tests have objective pass/fail thresholds, an owner and dated evidence.
\begin{itemize}
\item Controlled zone/service outage and failover timing against defined RTO.
\item Backup restoration and reconciliation against RPO; evidence from isolated recovery environment.
\item Post-failover access controls, incident alerts, runbook usability and failback rehearsal.
\end{itemize}
