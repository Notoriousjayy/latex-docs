\docsection{Purpose, scope, and provenance}
The source catalog defines 62 separately numbered project management artifacts across five process groups. This repository currently contains 58 integrated numbered LaTeX documents; source templates for 4.3 Earned Value Status, 4.4 Risk Audit, 4.5 Contractor Status Report, and 4.6 Formal Acceptance are not present. Each integrated artifact provides (1) proposed implementation guidance, (2) its source-derived working form, (3) source diagnostic questions, and (4) a review and approval record.
\textbf{Source preservation.} Original checklist questions and form labels are carried forward from the user-provided Word files. Their broad or occasionally unrelated wording is retained rather than silently rewritten. The separate governance guidance, roles, links, gates, and evidence recommendations are \emph{proposed implementation content} and should be tailored and approved before operational use.
\textbf{No implied approval.} Blank documents are not proof of a functioning CCoE, accepted risk, completed control, or approved baselines. The supplied example workbook and book illustrations are not real organizational measurements. Original upload PDFs and workbooks are not modified by this package.

\docsection{Suggested document-control conventions}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Assign a single accountable approval authority to each baseline and use the document ID as a stable identifier. Maintain owner, date, revision, approver, status, and retained evidence link.
\item Distinguish \textbf{draft}, \textbf{reviewed}, \textbf{approved}, \textbf{superseded}, and \textbf{archived} states. Preserve signed approval and change history, not only a working copy.
\item Create explicit links from requirements (2.4) to the requirements traceability matrix (2.5), WBS (2.8), delivery evidence, and formal acceptance (4.6) when that source template is available.
\item Record change requests (3.2), their disposition (3.3), and decisions (3.4) before revising controlled scope, schedule, cost, or requirements baselines.
\item Record assumptions (2.7), project risks (2.32), realized issues (3.10), and their resolution separately. An issue is not automatically the same as a risk or change request.
\item Protect personal performance information in documents 3.8 and 3.9; apply least privilege, relevant employment policies, and retention requirements.
\end{enumerate}

\docsection{Cross-artifact governance relationships}
\textbf{Risk.} The Risk Management Plan (2.31) defines the project approach; the Risk Register (2.32) records identified risks, ownership, responses, status, and residual risk. The Probability and Impact Assessment (2.33) supports consistent assessment using the source-defined categories and scales, the Probability and Impact Matrix (2.34) supports prioritization, and the Risk Data Sheet (2.35) carries detailed risk-level information. Risk tracking and reporting connect to the Issue Log (3.10), Project Performance Report (4.1), and Risk Audit (4.4) when its source is available. The templates do not pre-populate scores or assessed risks.

\textbf{Procurement.} The Procurement Management Plan (2.36) defines procurement planning and oversight; Source Selection Criteria (2.37) provides the evaluation structure without assigning supplier scores or decisions. Contract oversight and evidence should connect to the Contractor Status Report (4.5) when available, Procurement Audit (5.1), and Contract Close-Out (5.2).

\textbf{Stakeholders.} The Stakeholder Register (1.2) records identified stakeholders; the Stakeholder Analysis Matrix (1.3) evaluates relevant stakeholder characteristics; the Stakeholder Management Plan (2.38) defines how their engagement is managed. The Communications Management Plan (2.30) defines communication requirements and mechanisms that support that engagement, while the 2.38 form captures stakeholder-specific communication needs and engagement approaches. The Responsibility Assignment Matrix (2.27), Change Management Plan (2.39), Decision Log (3.4), and Project Performance Report (4.1) provide complementary role, change, decision, and monitoring evidence rather than duplicate stakeholder records.

\textbf{Change.} The Change Management Plan (2.39) defines change governance; Change Request (3.2) captures submissions and impact assessment, Decision Log (3.4) records approval decisions, and Change Log (3.3) tracks authorized changes through execution, verification, and closure. Cross-reference affected requirements and baselines (including 2.5) and stakeholder engagement (2.38); do not treat submission, approval, implementation, and closure as one event.

\docsection{CCoE self-assessment linkage}
The supplied CCoE assessment reference organizes questions into seven criteria: Recognize, Define, Measure, Analyze, Improve, Control, and Sustain. The reference describes a 1--5 scale and a criterion-average scorecard. These assessment inputs are separate from the project-management templates. Any assessment finding should be documented with evidence, mapped to a proposed improvement and owner, and then governed through the applicable initiation, scope, WBS, risk, change, and status artifacts.
\textbf{Suggested bridge:} diagnostic item $\rightarrow$ validated observation $\rightarrow$ risk / improvement opportunity $\rightarrow$ charter or authorized change $\rightarrow$ requirement / work package $\rightarrow$ accepted outcome. No assessment score is fabricated here.

\docsection{Package contents and usage}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Available process-group folders hold editable \texttt{.tex} sources. The canonical CCoE catalog and source links are in \texttt{readme.md}; PDFs are generated by the repository build system and are not committed.
\item Open an individual PDF for print or review. Edit its corresponding \texttt{.tex}, then compile with pdfLaTeX; supply project-specific data and obtain official approvals.
\item The original assessment workbooks remain separate source material and are not changed or copied into this output package.
\end{enumerate}
