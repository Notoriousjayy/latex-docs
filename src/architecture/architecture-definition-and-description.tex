\chapter{How to use this guide}

\section{What this guide contains}

This guide collects, classifies and explains the rules that govern how an
architecture is expressed and documented. It is organised as a working
instrument rather than a narrative: the catalogue in Part~II is the substance,
and the surrounding chapters exist to make it usable.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Part~I --- Orientation} establishes the scope of the rules, the
        distinction between an architecture and a description of one, the five
        kinds of specification the rules apply to, the conceptual model that
        gives the rules their vocabulary, and a glossary.
  \item \textbf{Part~II --- The rule catalogue} presents 132 catalogued items:
        123 rules classified as requirements, prohibitions, recommendations,
        permissions or explanatory guidance, and 9 provisions whose status or
        interpretation needs a decision by the adopting organisation. A compact
        index precedes the detailed records.
  \item \textbf{Part~III --- Application} supplies a suggested workflow, a
        reusable description outline with each field marked required,
        conditional, recommended or optional, a review checklist keyed to rule
        identifiers, and a completeness report.
\end{itemize}

\section{Reading a rule record}

Each rule record carries a stable identifier, a title, a classification badge
and the specification type it applies to, followed by these fields.

\begin{tcolorbox}[enhanced, sharp corners, boxrule=0.5pt, colframe=rulegrey,
                  colback=white, left=4mm, right=4mm, top=3mm, bottom=3mm]
\small
\rf{Obligation}{The provision stated in plain language, at its correct strength.}
\rf{Conditions}{The qualifications, scope limits and exceptions attached to it ---
   including whether it applies unconditionally or only when some condition holds.}
\rf{Meaning}{What the provision does in practice, and the misreadings it invites.}
\rf{Evidence}{Artefacts an architect or reviewer could inspect.}
\rf{Check}{A review question or acceptance check.}
\rf{Related}{Other rule identifiers bearing on this one.}
\end{tcolorbox}

\risknote{The \textbf{Evidence} and \textbf{Check} fields, and any statement
marked \ig, are \emph{implementation guidance}: they are this guide's
suggestions for how a provision might be satisfied and verified, not prescribed
artefacts. No provision requires a particular register, matrix, template or
document. Where a rule's own text names an artefact, it is stated in the
\textbf{Obligation} field instead.}

\section{Classification scheme}

The classifications preserve distinctions that matter for review. A reviewer
may raise a finding against a requirement or a prohibition; a recommendation
not followed is a point for discussion, not a defect; a permission cannot be
breached at all.

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}l l L{78mm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Badge} & \textbf{Classification} & \textbf{Meaning} \\
\midrule
\badge{navy}{Requirement}   & Requirement           & An obligation. Expressed with \emph{shall} in the source material. \\
\badge{brick}{Prohibition}  & Prohibition           & An action or condition that is disallowed. \\
\badge{teal}{Recommendation}& Recommendation        & An advised practice. Expressed with \emph{should}. \\
\badge{steel}{Permission}   & Permission            & An explicitly allowed option. Expressed with \emph{may}, or as a stated possibility. \\
\badge{slate}{Explanatory}  & Explanatory guidance  & Definitions, possibilities, examples and supporting explanation. Not reviewable. \\
\badge{amber}{Ambiguous}    & Ambiguous provision   & Wording whose status or interpretation requires a decision. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\why{The strength distinctions are the most commonly lost information when
rules are summarised. Two failure modes recur in practice: promoting an example
list into a mandatory schema, and promoting a recommendation to \emph{consider}
something into a requirement to \emph{implement} it. Both produce review
findings that cannot be sustained. Where this guide records a list of examples,
it is labelled as such, and no rule in the catalogue converts one into a field
list.}

\chapter{Establishing the correct scope}

\section{An architecture is not an architecture description}

The single most important distinction in this material is that between an
architecture and the description of one.

An \textbf{architecture} is the fundamental concepts or properties of an entity
in its environment, together with the principles governing that entity's
realisation and evolution. It is intangible and abstract. It is not a document,
and it is not something a set of documentation rules can create, validate or
improve.

An \textbf{architecture description} is a work product used to express an
architecture. It is tangible. It is produced by an architecting effort, for a
purpose specific to that effort, and it is the thing these rules govern.

\why{Every rule in this catalogue is a rule about \emph{expression}. When a
reviewer says a description fails a rule, the claim is that the architecture has
been inadequately expressed --- not that the architecture is deficient. The
converse also holds: a fully conforming description can express a poor
architecture, and a fine architecture can be described badly. Conflating the two
produces review findings that the architecture team cannot act on and that the
documentation team cannot own.}

Several consequences follow directly, and they bound what may legitimately be
required of a project on the authority of these rules.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{No design process is prescribed.} The rules say nothing about
        how architecting is done, in what sequence, by whom, or under what
        method. A description may precede, follow or accompany the creation of
        the architecture it expresses.
  \item \textbf{No technology stack, style or pattern is prescribed.} Layering,
        tiering, service decomposition and every other structural choice remain
        entirely open.
  \item \textbf{No notation, language, tool or format is prescribed.} Both
        document-centric and model-based practice are expressly admissible, and
        no particular level of formality is required
        (\rref{AD-005}).
  \item \textbf{No guarantee of quality is offered.} Completeness and
        correctness of the content are not addressed. Consistency can be
        partially checked --- through correspondences and coverage --- but that
        is a much weaker claim than correctness.
\end{itemize}

\risknote{Avoid claims of certification or guaranteed architectural correctness.
A conformance claim asserts that a \emph{specification} meets a stated set of
requirements. It is not an assessment of the architecture, of the entity, of the
project, or of the organisation, and it should never be presented as one.}

\section{The entity of interest}

The subject of an architecture description is called the \textbf{entity of
interest}. The term is deliberately broad: it covers software items and
products, systems of every kind including systems of systems and families of
systems, enterprises and organisations, processes, businesses, data, applications,
technology collections, missions, services, product lines and business domains.
The entity may be concrete or abstract, and the rules take no position on what
constitutes an entity in any given field.

Each entity of interest sits in an \textbf{environment} --- the totality of
surrounding things, conditions and influences bearing on it, and of its
influences on them, across its life cycle. Identifying both the entity and its
expected environment is required (\rref{AD-001},
\rref{AD-002}).

\note{The rules place no requirements on the entity of interest itself or on its
environment. They govern the use of architectural concepts in a description. A
requirement on the entity --- that it be available, secure or maintainable ---
is a concern to be identified and addressed, not an obligation arising from
these rules.}

\section{Purpose as the organising constraint}

A description states its intended purpose (\rref{AD-003}), and
that purpose is not decorative. Four of the principal identification
requirements --- stakeholders, perspectives, concerns and aspects --- are each
qualified as those \emph{relevant to the architecture and consistent with the
purpose of the description}.

\tip{Write the purpose statement so that it can actually exclude something. A
purpose such as ``to describe the architecture'' cannot discriminate, and leaves
every subsequent relevance judgement unreviewable in both directions: neither an
omission nor a piece of padding can be challenged against it. A purpose such as
``to support the migration investment decision by the steering group in
Q3, covering the payments domain only'' does real work.}

The purpose of the description is distinct from the purpose of the entity. The
same architecture may be expressed by several descriptions --- built for
different audiences, different life-cycle stages, different time periods or
different contexts of use --- each legitimate, each with its own purpose, and
each conforming or not on its own terms.

\chapter{The five specification types}

\section{What each type is, and when its rules apply}

The rules divide into five sets, each attaching to a different kind of
specification. A given project engages some of them and not others. The most
common error in adopting this material is assuming that all five apply to every
project --- they do not.

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{longtable}{@{}L{30mm} L{56mm} L{50mm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Specification type} & \textbf{What it is} & \textbf{When its rules apply} \\
\midrule
\endfirsthead
\toprule
\textbf{Specification type} & \textbf{What it is} & \textbf{When its rules apply} \\
\midrule
\endhead
\bottomrule
\endfoot
Architecture description
  & The work product expressing the architecture of an entity of interest.
  & Whenever a description is produced and conformance is claimed for it.
    Nearly every project. \\
\addlinespace
Architecture description framework
  & Conventions, principles and practices for describing architectures, established
    within a domain of application or a community of stakeholders.
  & Only when your organisation \emph{authors} a framework, or claims conformance
    for one. Using somebody else's framework does not engage these rules. \\
\addlinespace
Architecture description language
  & A means of expression --- representations, conventions and associated rules,
    with syntax and semantics --- for describing an architecture.
  & Only when your organisation \emph{defines} a language or profile, or claims
    conformance for one. Using an existing modelling language does not engage
    these rules. \\
\addlinespace
Architecture viewpoint
  & A set of conventions for creating, interpreting and using a view, framing one
    or more concerns.
  & Whenever a viewpoint is authored, and whenever a viewpoint is included in a
    description --- including one adopted from elsewhere. Very commonly engaged. \\
\addlinespace
Model kind
  & A category of model distinguished by its key characteristics and modelling
    conventions.
  & When a model kind is authored or a conformance claim is made for one.
    Adopting an established modelling technique does not by itself engage these
    rules. \\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}
\end{center}

\risknote{No project is obliged to create its own framework, its own description
language or its own model kinds. A conforming architecture description can be
produced using no framework at all (\rref{ADF-019}), an
off-the-shelf modelling language, and established modelling techniques. The
framework and language rule sets exist for those who publish such artefacts, and
they are evaluated as artefacts in their own right.}

\section{Where the obligation lands}

The distinction that causes most confusion in review is between what a
\emph{description} must do and what a \emph{viewpoint} must do. Both rule sets
are commonly engaged at once, but they bite in different places.

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}L{68mm} L{68mm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{The description must\ldots} & \textbf{The viewpoint must\ldots} \\
\midrule
identify stakeholders, perspectives, concerns and aspects, and relate them
  (\rref{AD-006}--\rref{AD-018})
  & identify the concerns it frames, the aspects refining them, and the typical
    stakeholders holding them
    (\rref{VP-002}--\rref{VP-004}) \\
\addlinespace
include or reference each viewpoint it uses, and version-identify it
  (\rref{AD-019}, \rref{AD-020})
  & be specifiable independently, and may sit in a description, a framework, a
    language, or on its own (\rref{VP-012}) \\
\addlinespace
carry one or more views per viewpoint, each adhering to its viewpoint's
  conventions (\rref{AD-024}, \rref{AD-029})
  & supply those conventions --- model kinds and legends for constructing views
    (\rref{VP-005}) \\
\addlinespace
demonstrate that every concern, perspective and aspect is addressed by a view
  (\rref{AD-026}--\rref{AD-028})
  & define what addressing looks like, through its framed concerns and its view
    methods (\rref{VP-002}, \rref{VP-008}) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\tip{When a description adopts a viewpoint written elsewhere, the description
inherits that viewpoint's deficiencies. \rref{AD-021} requires
that the specification of each included viewpoint satisfy the viewpoint rules.
Assess borrowed viewpoints on intake, not at review.}

\section{Adherence and conformance are different claims}

A description \emph{conforms} when it meets the requirements for descriptions.
A description \emph{adheres} to a framework when it identifies and considers the
applicability of each element that framework identifies
(\rref{ADF-017}). The two claims are independent: a
description can conform without adhering to any framework, and a framework can
impose adherence rules of its own beyond the baseline
(\rref{ADF-018}).

\risknote{Tailoring is not available. Where a conformance claim is made, the
requirements may not be selectively omitted, weakened or substituted
(\rref{CF-007}). Where a requirement genuinely cannot be met, the
correct response is to record the limitation
(\rref{AD-009}) and the non-conformance with its rationale
(\rref{AD-010}) --- and to accept that the requirement remains
unmet. \textbf{Documenting a deviation does not satisfy the obligation it
deviates from.}}

\chapter{The conceptual model}

\section{The concepts and how they connect}

The rules are expressed in a small vocabulary of related concepts. Getting the
relationships right --- particularly their cardinalities --- is what makes the
coverage rules reviewable.

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{longtable}{@{}L{44mm} L{92mm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Relationship} & \textbf{Reading} \\
\midrule
\endfirsthead
\toprule
\textbf{Relationship} & \textbf{Reading} \\
\midrule
\endhead
\bottomrule
\endfoot
Entity of interest $\rightarrow$ environment
  & The entity is situated in an environment that influences it and is influenced
    by it. \\
Architecture $\rightarrow$ entity
  & The architecture comprises the fundamental concepts or properties of the
    entity considered in its environment. \\
Description $\rightarrow$ architecture
  & A description expresses an architecture. One architecture may be expressed by
    several descriptions, each with its own purpose. \\
Stakeholder $\rightarrow$ entity
  & A stakeholder has an interest, right, share or claim in the entity. \\
Stakeholder $\rightarrow$ perspective
  & A stakeholder sees the entity from one or more perspectives. \\
Perspective $\rightarrow$ concern
  & A perspective results in one or more concerns. \\
Concern $\rightarrow$ stakeholder
  & A concern is a matter of importance to one or more stakeholders. The relation
    is many-to-many. \\
Aspect $\rightarrow$ entity
  & An aspect is part of the entity's character or nature. \\
Aspect $\leftrightarrow$ concern
  & Aspects refine concerns; the relation is many-to-many. \\
Viewpoint $\rightarrow$ concern
  & A viewpoint frames one or more concerns. A concern may be framed by more than
    one viewpoint. \\
Viewpoint $\rightarrow$ view
  & \textbf{A viewpoint governs one or more views} within a description. \\
View $\rightarrow$ concern
  & A view addresses concerns, in accordance with its governing viewpoint. A view
    may address more than one concern. \\
View $\rightarrow$ view component
  & A view is composed of one or more view components. \\
View component $\rightarrow$ view
  & \textbf{A view component may belong to more than one view.} \\
Model kind $\rightarrow$ component
  & A model kind determines the conventions for model-based view components. \\
Legend $\rightarrow$ component
  & A legend documents the conventions for a view component, and is what governs
    a component that has no model kind. \\
Correspondence $\rightarrow$ elements
  & A correspondence is an identified or named relation among two or more
    description elements --- possibly across descriptions, possibly reflexive. \\
Correspondence method $\rightarrow$ correspondence
  & A method supplies the rules, practices or models governing a correspondence. \\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}
\end{center}

\section{Cardinalities that are commonly assumed wrongly}

\risknote{\textbf{A viewpoint does not govern exactly one view.} A viewpoint
governs \emph{one or more} views within a description, and several views under
one viewpoint are still describing one architecture. A functional viewpoint may
legitimately govern both a functional-chain view expressing behaviour and a
function-tree view expressing decomposition. Do not raise a finding for
``duplicate'' views under one viewpoint, and do not require a strict pairing
(\rref{AD-024}).}

\risknote{\textbf{Not every view component is model-based.} A component is
governed by a model kind \emph{or} by a legend. Non-model components ---
narrative, expert opinion, symbology tables, tabular records --- are
first-class, and the rules provide for them explicitly: the governing model kind
is identified only \emph{if any} (\rref{AD-038}), and a legend is
required precisely where there is none (\rref{AD-042}). A
viewpoint must supply both model kinds and legends
(\rref{VP-005}).}

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}L{56mm} L{80mm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Do not assume} & \textbf{The rules actually say} \\
\midrule
One view per viewpoint
  & One \emph{or more} views per viewpoint (\rref{AD-024}). \\
One concern per view
  & A view may address more than one concern (\rref{AD-030}). \\
Each component belongs to one view
  & A component may be part of more than one view (\rref{AD-041}). \\
Every component has a model kind
  & Only if it has one; otherwise a legend (\rref{AD-038},
    \rref{AD-042}). \\
Every view covers the whole entity
  & Some \emph{or all} of the entity, with respect to its viewpoint
    (\rref{AD-033}). \\
A description uses exactly one framework
  & One, several, or none (\rref{ADF-019}). \\
Correspondences relate distinct elements
  & Elements need not be distinct; a correspondence may be reflexive
    (\rref{AD-047}). \\
Correspondences stay inside one description
  & They may span descriptions; a description may itself be an element of another
    (\rref{AD-048}). \\
Concerns come only from stakeholders present
  & Concerns may be identified with respect to the environment, a scenario, a
    situation or a use case (\rref{AD-015}). \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\section{The three coverage chains}

Three chains run through the description rules, and a review works each of them
end to end. Each begins with an identification obligation and terminates in a
view.

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}l L{112mm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Chain} & \textbf{Sequence} \\
\midrule
Concerns
  & identified (\rref{AD-015}) $\rightarrow$ associated with
    stakeholders (\rref{AD-016}) $\rightarrow$ framed by at least one
    viewpoint (\rref{AD-022}) $\rightarrow$ addressed by at least one
    view (\rref{AD-027}) $\rightarrow$ addressed by that view's
    components (\rref{AD-033}) \\
\addlinespace
Perspectives
  & identified (\rref{AD-011}) $\rightarrow$ associated with
    stakeholders (\rref{AD-012}) $\rightarrow$ their resulting concerns
    enumerated (\rref{AD-014}) $\rightarrow$ associated with covering
    viewpoints (\rref{AD-023}) $\rightarrow$ addressed by at least one
    view (\rref{AD-026}) \\
\addlinespace
Aspects
  & identified (\rref{AD-017}) $\rightarrow$ associated with the
    concerns that apply (\rref{AD-018}) $\rightarrow$ addressed by at
    least one view (\rref{AD-028}) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\why{Framing and addressing are separate obligations and are separately
reviewable. To \emph{frame} a concern is to shape and give expression to it ---
the work a viewpoint does. To \emph{address} a concern is to answer it --- the
work a view does. A description can satisfy every framing rule and still fail
every addressing rule, which is exactly what happens when a viewpoint set is
adopted wholesale and the views are never built out against it.}

\note{Where a chain cannot be completed, the gap is recorded, not concealed:
\rref{AD-009} requires a statement of the limitations or
constraints that prevented an identified stakeholder or consideration from being
addressed, and \rref{AD-064} advises a rationale for it. This
records the gap --- it does not close it.}

\chapter{Glossary}

The definitions below are the working vocabulary of the catalogue. Terms are
grouped by role rather than alphabetically, because the distinctions that matter
are between adjacent terms.

\section{The subject and its expression}

\begin{description}[style=nextline,leftmargin=6mm,labelindent=0pt,font=\sffamily\bfseries\color{navy}]
\item[Architecture]
  The fundamental concepts or properties of an entity in its environment,
  together with the principles governing that entity's realisation and
  evolution. Intangible and abstract; understood through its concepts,
  properties and principles. \emph{Not} a document, and not the subject of these
  rules.
\item[Architecture description]
  A work product used to express an architecture. Tangible; produced by an
  architecting effort for a purpose specific to that effort. This is what the
  rules govern. One architecture may be expressed by several descriptions.
\item[Entity of interest]
  The subject of an architecture description --- a system, software item,
  enterprise, process, service, product line, business domain, data structure,
  mission or other subject, concrete or abstract.
\item[Description element]
  An identified or named part of an architecture description: an occurrence of
  an architectural concept within it. Stakeholders, concerns, perspectives,
  aspects, viewpoints, views, components, model kinds, legends, decisions,
  rationale, correspondences and correspondence methods are all description
  elements. For the purpose of correspondences, a whole description may be
  treated as an element of another description.
\end{description}

\section{Who cares, and about what}

\begin{description}[style=nextline,leftmargin=6mm,labelindent=0pt,font=\sffamily\bfseries\color{navy}]
\item[Stakeholder]
  A role, position, individual, organisation or class of these, having an
  interest, right, share or claim in the entity of interest. Includes parties
  whose concerns are contrary to the entity's success.
\item[Stakeholder perspective]
  A \emph{way of thinking} about the entity of interest, especially as it
  relates to concerns --- shaped by role, training, experience, knowledge,
  culture and proximity in the life cycle. Perspectives group stakeholders and
  result in concerns. Not a container of diagrams, and not a synonym for
  viewpoint.
\item[Concern]
  A matter of relevance or importance to a stakeholder. May be identified with
  respect to the entity or independently --- with respect to its environment, a
  scenario, a situation or a use case. Concerns are relatively subjective:
  they follow from current stakeholder interests and influences.
\item[Aspect]
  A part of the entity's \emph{character or nature} --- structural, behavioural,
  functional, informational, spatial, programmatic. Aspects are relatively
  objective: they arise from expert agreement about practice in a domain. They
  refine concerns, and examining them can surface concerns not yet identified.
\end{description}

\tip{Perspective, concern and aspect are the three ``architecture
considerations'', and they are routinely conflated. A quick discriminator: a
perspective is a property of a \emph{person}; a concern is a property of the
\emph{relationship} between a person and the entity; an aspect is a property of
the \emph{entity}.}

\section{How the architecture is expressed}

\begin{description}[style=nextline,leftmargin=6mm,labelindent=0pt,font=\sffamily\bfseries\color{navy}]
\item[Architecture viewpoint]
  A set of conventions for the creation, interpretation and use of an
  architecture view, framing one or more concerns. The conventions are recorded
  in a viewpoint specification. A viewpoint governs one or more views. In some
  communities, ``view specification'' is used synonymously.
\item[Architecture view]
  An information part comprising a portion of an architecture description,
  governed by a viewpoint and addressing the concerns that viewpoint frames. A
  view may address more than one concern and need not cover the whole entity.
\item[View component]
  A separable portion of one or more views, governed by the applicable model
  kind or legend. May be model-based or not, and may belong to several views at
  once.
\item[Model kind]
  A category of model distinguished by its key characteristics and modelling
  conventions --- use case models, activity models, threat models, component
  models, connectivity models, analytic models. It determines the conventions
  for model-based view components.
\item[Legend]
  An informal documentation of conventions within a description. A legend
  documents the conventions for a view component, and is what governs a
  component with no model kind. It must actually guide interpretation, not
  merely label symbols.
\item[View method]
  Guidance, heuristics, metrics, patterns, rules and examples aiding the
  construction and use of views. Divided into \emph{construction} methods (how
  views are prepared), \emph{interpretive} methods (how they are understood) and
  \emph{analysis} methods (how they are checked, reasoned about, transformed and
  evaluated).
\end{description}

\section{Relating things, and explaining choices}

\begin{description}[style=nextline,leftmargin=6mm,labelindent=0pt,font=\sffamily\bfseries\color{navy}]
\item[Correspondence]
  An identified or named relationship between two or more description elements
  --- expressing equivalence, composition, refinement, consistency,
  traceability, dependency, constraint, satisfaction or obligation. May span
  descriptions; may relate an element to itself.
\item[Correspondence method]
  The rules, practices or models specifying a particular relation between
  description elements. A method may originate in the elements, in the
  description, in a viewpoint or model kind specification, or in a framework or
  language specification. Usually cross-model, cross-view or cross-description.
\item[Architecture decision]
  A collection of choices made in the overall context of an architecture ---
  of concepts, elements, frameworks, layering schemes, technologies, tactics,
  patterns, styles or option sets.
\item[Architecture rationale]
  The explanation, justification or reasoning behind a decision: the basis for
  making it, impact on quality attributes, alternatives and trade-offs
  considered, potential consequences, architectural principles, and citations
  to further information.
\end{description}

\section{The codifying artefacts}

\begin{description}[style=nextline,leftmargin=6mm,labelindent=0pt,font=\sffamily\bfseries\color{navy}]
\item[Architecture description framework]
  Conventions, principles and practices for describing architectures,
  established within a domain of application or a community of stakeholders. It
  promotes structured organisation, consistency, reuse and completeness of views
  and models, and typically supplies a structuring formalism --- often a grid of
  perspectives against aspects.
\item[Architecture description language]
  A means of expression with syntax and semantics --- a set of representations,
  conventions and associated rules --- intended for describing an architecture.
  A description may use more than one, even a different one per viewpoint,
  though doing so requires care.
\item[Specification]
  An information part identifying, in a complete, precise and verifiable manner,
  the requirements, design, behaviour or other expected characteristics of an
  entity. Conformance claims are made about specifications.
\end{description}

\section{Conformance and tailoring}

These provisions determine what a conformance claim asserts, which body of requirements each of the five claims is evaluated against, and why the requirements cannot be tailored.

\begin{rulerec}{CF-001}{Locus of the normative provisions}{Explanatory guidance}{Conformance (all types)}{slate}
\rf{Class}{\badge{slate}{Explanatory guidance}\quad\badge{slate}{Conformance (all types)}}
\rf{Obligation}{The obligations that can be conformed to are confined to the provisions governing architecture descriptions, frameworks and languages, and viewpoints and model kinds. The conceptual material that introduces the vocabulary supplies meaning for those provisions but is not itself a source of obligations.}
\rf{Conditions}{Applies to interpretation of the whole rule set.}
\rf{Meaning}{Statements drawn from the conceptual and explanatory material must not be recast as obligations. Where this guide labels an item as explanatory guidance, a reviewer may not raise a finding against it.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{Not applicable --- this rule constrains how the other rules are read.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{When a reviewer raises a finding, can it be traced to a rule classified here as a requirement or a prohibition?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{IX-005}, \rref{IX-001}}
\end{rulerec}
\begin{rulerec}{CF-002}{Conformance claim for an architecture description}{Requirement}{Conformance (all types)}{navy}
\rf{Class}{\badge{navy}{Requirement}\quad\badge{slate}{Conformance (all types)}}
\rf{Obligation}{A claim that an architecture description conforms must demonstrate that the specification of that architecture description satisfies the requirements applicable to architecture descriptions.}
\rf{Conditions}{Applies only when a conformance claim is actually made. Producing an architecture description without claiming conformance triggers no obligation.}
\rf{Meaning}{Conformance is claimed for and evaluated against the description as specified --- not against the architecture, the entity, the process that produced it, or the tooling used.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{A written conformance statement identifying the architecture description under claim, and a demonstration mapping each applicable requirement to where it is satisfied.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Is there an explicit conformance statement, and does it map every AD-class requirement to satisfying content?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{CF-007}, \rref{AD-001}}
\end{rulerec}
\begin{rulerec}{CF-003}{Conformance claim for a framework}{Requirement}{Conformance (all types)}{navy}
\rf{Class}{\badge{navy}{Requirement}\quad\badge{slate}{Conformance (all types)}}
\rf{Obligation}{A claim that an architecture description framework conforms must demonstrate that the specification of that framework satisfies the requirements applicable to frameworks.}
\rf{Conditions}{Applies only when a framework conformance claim is made. A project that merely uses somebody else's framework does not thereby claim framework conformance.}
\rf{Meaning}{The object under evaluation is the framework specification as a published artefact, evaluated independently of any architecture description that adheres to it.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{A framework specification document with a conformance statement and a requirement-by-requirement demonstration.}

\rf{Meaning}{Conformance attaches to the language specification, not to models expressed in the language and not to tools implementing it.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{A language specification with a conformance statement and a mapping to each language requirement.}

\section{Description: identification and overview}

The opening obligations fix the subject, its environment and the purpose of the description, and delegate the content of identifying information to the organisation.

\begin{rulerec}{AD-001}{Identify the entity of interest}{Requirement}{Architecture description}{navy}
\rf{Class}{\badge{navy}{Requirement}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture description}}
\rf{Obligation}{An architecture description must identify the entity whose architecture it expresses.}
\rf{Conditions}{Unconditional. The entity may be concrete or abstract, and may be a system, software item, enterprise, service, product line, business domain, process, technology collection or other subject.}
\rf{Meaning}{Everything else in the description is scoped by this identification. Without it, coverage claims about concerns, aspects and views have no referent.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{A named and bounded subject statement in the front matter, distinguishing the entity from neighbouring entities.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Can a reader state, from the description alone, exactly what is and is not the subject?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{AD-002}, \rref{AD-003}, \rref{AD-033}}
\end{rulerec}
\begin{rulerec}{AD-002}{Identify the expected environment}{Requirement}{Architecture description}{navy}
\rf{Class}{\badge{navy}{Requirement}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture description}}
\rf{Obligation}{An architecture description must identify the expected environment of the entity of interest.}
\rf{Conditions}{Unconditional. The environment covers surrounding things, conditions and influences --- developmental, technological, business, operational, organisational, political, economic, legal, regulatory, ecological, social and physical.}
\rf{Meaning}{Architecture is defined relative to an environment, so an unidentified environment leaves the fundamental concepts and properties underdetermined. Qualified environments (development, test, operational) are contexts within a context and can be named separately.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{A context statement or context view identifying external entities and influences.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Are the external influences that shape the architecture named, rather than assumed?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{AD-001}, \rref{AD-015}}
\end{rulerec}
\begin{rulerec}{AD-003}{State the intended purpose of the description}{Requirement}{Architecture description}{navy}
\rf{Class}{\badge{navy}{Requirement}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture description}}
\rf{Obligation}{An architecture description must include a statement of its intended purpose.}
\rf{Conditions}{Unconditional. The purpose of the description is distinct from the purpose of the entity.}
\rf{Meaning}{Purpose is the yardstick used by several later obligations: stakeholders, perspectives, concerns and aspects are each qualified as those relevant and consistent with the purpose of the description. A vague purpose statement makes those relevance judgements unreviewable.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{An explicit purpose statement naming the architecting effort's objective and the intended audience and uses.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Does the purpose statement actually discriminate --- could it be used to justify excluding an irrelevant concern?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{AD-006}, \rref{AD-011}, \rref{AD-015}, \rref{AD-017}, \rref{AD-054}}
\end{rulerec}
\begin{rulerec}{AD-004}{Include identifying and supplementary information}{Requirement}{Architecture description}{navy}
\rf{Class}{\badge{navy}{Requirement}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture description}}
\rf{Obligation}{An architecture description must include identifying information and supplementary information, with the specific content determined by the project or the organisation.}
\rf{Conditions}{The obligation to include is unconditional; the content is delegated. Typical candidates --- issue date and status, authors, reviewers, approving authority, issuing organisation, change history, summary, scope, context, glossary, version control and configuration management data, and references --- are examples, not a mandatory field list.}
\rf{Meaning}{This is a two-part obligation: the organisation or project must settle what identifying and supplementary information means for it, and the description must then carry it. A reviewer checks against the organisation's own definition, not against the example list.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{A documented house standard for description front matter, plus front matter in the description that matches it.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Is there a defined organisational expectation for this information, and does the description meet it? Have example items been mistaken for mandatory ones?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{AD-025}, \rref{AD-031}, \rref{AD-005}}
\end{rulerec}
\begin{rulerec}{AD-005}{No prescribed format, production method or level of formality}{Explanatory guidance}{Architecture description}{slate}
\rf{Class}{\badge{slate}{Explanatory guidance}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture description}}
\rf{Obligation}{No format or medium for an architecture description is prescribed; no method of production is prescribed --- descriptions may be hand-built, tool-generated, or derived from other information sources and models; and no particular extent of formal modelling is prescribed. Informal methods can be used effectively, while formal methods are often less ambiguous.}
\rf{Conditions}{Applies throughout. Where an architecture description is intended as a reference for other descriptions, its entity of interest is abstract or a generalisation, and its purpose is to express a reference architecture forming a basis for further descriptions.}
\rf{Meaning}{Document-centric and model-based practice are both admissible. A reviewer may not require a particular file format, repository, notation or modelling tool on the authority of these rules; such requirements can only come from the organisation, a framework or a language.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{Not applicable --- this rule bounds what may be required.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Does any finding rest on a preference for a format, tool or notation rather than on a stated requirement?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{AD-042}, \rref{MK-001}, \rref{CF-007}}
\end{rulerec}

\section{Description: stakeholders}

Stakeholder identification is filtered by two qualifiers, and the group closes with the disclosure obligations for limitations and non-conformances.

\begin{rulerec}{AD-006}{Identify stakeholders}{Requirement}{Architecture description}{navy}
\rf{Class}{\badge{navy}{Requirement}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture description}}
\rf{Obligation}{An architecture description must identify the stakeholders whose concerns are considered fundamental to the architecture of the entity of interest and consistent with the purpose of the description.}
\rf{Conditions}{Doubly qualified: fundamental to the architecture, and consistent with the description's purpose. Not every party with an interest need be listed --- only those meeting both qualifiers. Stakeholders may be roles, positions, individuals, organisations or classes of these.}
\rf{Meaning}{The filter is deliberate and works in both directions: a reviewer can challenge an omission (a fundamental concern with no holder) and can equally challenge padding that is inconsistent with the stated purpose. Adversarial and hostile parties are legitimate stakeholders where their concerns are fundamental.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{A stakeholder register with, for each entry, the basis on which it was judged fundamental and purpose-consistent.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{For each identified concern, is at least one holder identified, and for each stakeholder, is the fundamental-and-consistent test evidenced?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{AD-003}, \rref{AD-016}, \rref{AD-007}}
\end{rulerec}
\begin{rulerec}{AD-007}{Identify possible impacts on stakeholders}{Recommendation}{Architecture description}{teal}
\rf{Class}{\badge{teal}{Recommendation}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture description}}
\rf{Obligation}{An architecture description should identify the possible impacts of the architecture on current and future stakeholders.}
\rf{Conditions}{Advised, not obligatory. Covers both current and future stakeholders.}
\rf{Meaning}{Impact analysis runs in the opposite direction to concern elicitation: rather than asking what stakeholders want from the architecture, it asks what the architecture does to them --- including to parties who have not yet appeared and cannot yet speak.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{An impact register or impact section relating architectural characteristics to affected parties.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{If impacts are not recorded, is that a deliberate decision or an oversight? Absence is not a conformance failure.}
\rf{Related}{\rref{AD-006}, \rref{AD-009}}
\end{rulerec}
\begin{rulerec}{AD-008}{Take account of prior evaluation recommendations}{Requirement}{Architecture description}{navy}
\rf{Class}{\badge{navy}{Requirement}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture description}}
\rf{Obligation}{Recommendations made in previous evaluations of this architecture, or of related architectures, must be given consideration.}
\rf{Conditions}{Conditional on such evaluations existing. Where no prior evaluation of this or a related architecture exists, the condition is unmet and the obligation does not bite.}
\rf{Meaning}{The obligation is to consider, not to adopt. Consideration that leads to rejection satisfies the rule, provided the consideration happened. Note that the provision does not state where the consideration is to be recorded --- see the interpretation issue.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{Minutes, a disposition log or a review record showing each prior recommendation and its disposition with reasons.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Were prior evaluations located at all? For each recommendation, is there evidence of a disposition rather than silence?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{IX-006}, \rref{AD-054}, \rref{AD-063}}
\end{rulerec}
\begin{rulerec}{AD-009}{State known resource limitations and other constraints}{Requirement}{Architecture description}{navy}
\rf{Class}{\badge{navy}{Requirement}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture description}}
\rf{Obligation}{An architecture description must include a statement of known resource limitations or other constraints that prevented it from addressing identified stakeholders and identified architecture considerations --- that is, concerns, stakeholder perspectives and aspects.}
\rf{Conditions}{Conditional on such limitations existing and having had that preventive effect. The scope is precise: it covers identified stakeholders and identified considerations that went unaddressed.}
\rf{Meaning}{This is the honest-gap provision. It applies where something was identified as relevant and then not addressed for reasons of time, money, access or authority. Recording the limitation does not discharge the coverage obligations it explains --- the coverage rules remain unmet, and the statement records why.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{A limitations section listing each unaddressed identified stakeholder, concern, perspective or aspect together with the limiting constraint.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Cross-check every identified concern, perspective and aspect against view coverage; is every gap either closed or explained here?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{AD-010}, \rref{AD-064}, \rref{AD-022}, \rref{AD-026}, \rref{AD-027}, \rref{AD-028}, \rref{CF-007}}
\end{rulerec}
\begin{rulerec}{AD-010}{Identify and explain non-conformances}{Requirement}{Architecture description}{navy}
\rf{Class}{\badge{navy}{Requirement}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture description}}
\rf{Obligation}{Non-conformances must be identified and explained with rationales.}
\rf{Conditions}{Conditional on non-conformances existing.}
\rf{Meaning}{Departures are disclosed rather than concealed, and each carries a reason. Because tailoring is unavailable, a description carrying non-conformances cannot sustain an unqualified conformance claim.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{A non-conformance register: the provision departed from, the nature of the departure, and the rationale.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Is each disclosed non-conformance accompanied by a rationale, and is the conformance claim qualified accordingly?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{AD-009}, \rref{CF-007}, \rref{CF-002}}
\end{rulerec}

\section{Description: stakeholder perspectives}

Perspectives are ways of thinking about the entity. They are identified, anchored to stakeholders, and made to yield concerns.

\begin{rulerec}{AD-011}{Identify stakeholder perspectives}{Requirement}{Architecture description}{navy}
\rf{Class}{\badge{navy}{Requirement}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture description}}
\rf{Obligation}{An architecture description must identify the stakeholder perspectives considered relevant to the architecture of the entity of interest and consistent with the purpose of the description.}
\rf{Conditions}{Doubly qualified in the same way as stakeholder identification. Examples of perspectives include strategic, organisational, operational, logical, physical and technological ways of thinking.}
\rf{Meaning}{A perspective is a way of thinking about the entity --- shaped by role, training, experience, culture and proximity in the life cycle --- not a container of diagrams. Perspectives group stakeholders and give rise to concerns.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{A perspective register with, for each entry, its characterisation and its relevance basis.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Are the listed perspectives genuinely ways of thinking, or have view categories been mislabelled as perspectives?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{AD-012}, \rref{AD-014}, \rref{AD-023}, \rref{AD-026}}
\end{rulerec}
\begin{rulerec}{AD-012}{Associate each perspective with its stakeholders}{Requirement}{Architecture description}{navy}
\rf{Class}{\badge{navy}{Requirement}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture description}}
\rf{Obligation}{An architecture description must associate each identified stakeholder perspective with the identified stakeholders holding that perspective.}
\rf{Conditions}{Applies to each identified perspective. The association is drawn to the stakeholders already identified in the description.}
\rf{Meaning}{Perspectives are anchored to real parties, which prevents an abstract grid of perspectives that corresponds to nobody. The relation is many-to-many in practice: a stakeholder can hold several perspectives and a perspective can be held by several stakeholders.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{A perspective-to-stakeholder matrix, or a holders field on each perspective record.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Does every identified perspective have at least one identified stakeholder attached?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{AD-006}, \rref{AD-011}}
\end{rulerec}
\begin{rulerec}{AD-013}{Look for present and future perspectives}{Recommendation}{Architecture description}{teal}
\rf{Class}{\badge{teal}{Recommendation}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture description}}
\rf{Obligation}{Within the scope of the description's intended purpose, the architecting effort should identify present or future stakeholder perspectives that may be relevant to the entity of interest.}
\rf{Conditions}{Advised, and bounded by the intended purpose. The obligation attaches to the architecting effort rather than to the description as an artefact.}
\rf{Meaning}{This is an elicitation practice: actively search for perspectives, including ones that will only matter later in the life cycle, rather than recording only those that presented themselves.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{Elicitation workshop records, a candidate-perspective list showing which were carried forward and which set aside.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Is there evidence of a search for perspectives, as distinct from a list of the ones that happened to be volunteered?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{AD-011}, \rref{AD-007}}
\end{rulerec}
\begin{rulerec}{AD-014}{Enumerate the concerns resulting from each perspective}{Requirement}{Architecture description}{navy}
\rf{Class}{\badge{navy}{Requirement}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture description}}
\rf{Obligation}{For each identified stakeholder perspective, an architecture description must enumerate the concerns resulting from that perspective, drawn from among the concerns identified in the description.}
\rf{Conditions}{Applies to each identified perspective. The enumeration is drawn from the identified concern set --- it does not introduce concerns outside it.}
\rf{Meaning}{Perspectives result in concerns, and this rule makes that derivation explicit and closed. If a perspective yields a concern not present in the concern register, the register is incomplete.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{A perspective-to-concern mapping whose right-hand side is a subset of the concern register.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Does every perspective enumerate at least one concern, and does every enumerated concern appear in the concern register?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{AD-011}, \rref{AD-015}, \rref{AD-016}}
\end{rulerec}

\section{Description: concerns}

Concerns are identified against the same double qualifier and associated with their holders.

\begin{rulerec}{AD-015}{Identify concerns}{Requirement}{Architecture description}{navy}
\rf{Class}{\badge{navy}{Requirement}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture description}}
\rf{Obligation}{An architecture description must identify the concerns considered relevant to the architecture of the entity of interest and consistent with the purpose of the description.}
\rf{Conditions}{Doubly qualified as before. Concerns may be identified with respect to the entity itself or independently --- with respect to its environment, a scenario, a situation or a use case. Concerns may relate to past, present or future matters.}
\rf{Meaning}{A concern is a matter of relevance or importance to a stakeholder, and may manifest as a need, goal, expectation, responsibility, requirement, design constraint, assumption, dependency, quality attribute, decision, risk or issue. Concerns arise throughout the life cycle, including at retirement and disposal.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{A concern register with a relevance basis for each entry. Concerns phrased as interrogative questions at a detail level matched to the purpose tend to communicate more effectively.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Is each concern stated precisely enough that a view could be judged to address it or not?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{AD-016}, \rref{AD-018}, \rref{AD-022}, \rref{AD-027}, \rref{VP-002}}
\end{rulerec}
\begin{rulerec}{AD-016}{Associate each concern with its stakeholders}{Requirement}{Architecture description}{navy}
\rf{Class}{\badge{navy}{Requirement}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture description}}
\rf{Obligation}{An architecture description must associate each identified concern with the identified stakeholders holding that concern.}
\rf{Conditions}{Applies to each identified concern. The association is generally many-to-many.}
\rf{Meaning}{Every concern has an owner. Orphan concerns --- present in the register with no holder --- signal either a missing stakeholder or a concern that fails the fundamental test.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{A concern-to-stakeholder matrix, or a holders field on each concern record.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Does every concern have at least one identified holder, and does every stakeholder hold at least one concern?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{AD-006}, \rref{AD-015}, \rref{AD-014}}
\end{rulerec}

\section{Description: aspects}

Aspects characterise the entity and are tied to the concerns they refine.

\begin{rulerec}{AD-017}{Identify aspects}{Requirement}{Architecture description}{navy}
\rf{Class}{\badge{navy}{Requirement}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture description}}
\rf{Obligation}{An architecture description must identify the aspects considered relevant to the architecture of the entity of interest and consistent with the purpose of the description.}
\rf{Conditions}{Doubly qualified as before. Examples include structural, behavioural, functional, informational, spatial and programmatic aspects.}
\rf{Meaning}{An aspect is a part of the entity's character or nature. Aspects are more objective than concerns: they come from expert agreement about practice in a domain, and examining them helps discern or predict relevant properties --- and can surface concerns not previously identified.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{An aspect register with a relevance basis for each entry.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Are the listed items genuinely characteristics of the entity, rather than concerns or viewpoints relabelled?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{AD-018}, \rref{AD-028}, \rref{VP-003}}
\end{rulerec}
\begin{rulerec}{AD-018}{Associate each aspect with the concerns that apply}{Requirement}{Architecture description}{navy}
\rf{Class}{\badge{navy}{Requirement}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture description}}
\rf{Obligation}{Each identified aspect must be associated with the concerns that apply to it.}
\rf{Conditions}{Applies to each identified aspect. The relation between aspects and concerns is in general many-to-many.}
\rf{Meaning}{Aspects act as refinements of concerns and as a structuring device for addressing them. The association records which concerns a given facet of the entity's character bears on, and is the basis on which viewpoint conventions are often built.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{An aspect-to-concern matrix.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Does every aspect map to at least one identified concern? Is any aspect present that maps to none?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{AD-015}, \rref{AD-017}, \rref{VP-003}}
\end{rulerec}

\section{Description: viewpoints used}

These provisions govern how viewpoints enter a description, and establish the framing half of the coverage obligations.

\begin{rulerec}{AD-019}{Include or reference each viewpoint used}{Requirement}{Architecture description}{navy}
\rf{Class}{\badge{navy}{Requirement}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture description}}
\rf{Obligation}{An architecture description must include, or provide a reference to, each architecture viewpoint used within it.}
\rf{Conditions}{Applies to each viewpoint used. Inclusion by reference is expressly permitted --- the viewpoint specification need not be reproduced inside the description.}
\rf{Meaning}{Viewpoints may be drawn from a framework, a language, an organisational library or written for the description, but in every case the conventions governing each view must be retrievable by a reader of the description.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{A viewpoint register giving, for each viewpoint, either its embedded specification or a resolvable reference.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{For each view present, can a reader reach the governing viewpoint specification?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{AD-020}, \rref{AD-021}, \rref{AD-032}, \rref{VP-012}}
\end{rulerec}
\begin{rulerec}{AD-020}{Version-identify each viewpoint}{Requirement}{Architecture description}{navy}
\rf{Class}{\badge{navy}{Requirement}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture description}}
\rf{Obligation}{Each architecture viewpoint must carry version identification, in the form specified by the organisation or the project.}
\rf{Conditions}{The obligation to version-identify is unconditional; the scheme is delegated to the organisation or project. Note that this obligation arises from the rules on including viewpoints in a description, not from the viewpoint specification content rules.}
\rf{Meaning}{Because viewpoints are reused and evolve, a view cannot be interpreted without knowing which revision of its conventions governed it. The organisation chooses the scheme; having no scheme is not an option.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{A version field on each viewpoint record and a documented versioning scheme.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Is there an organisational versioning scheme, and does each viewpoint carry an identifier conforming to it?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{AD-025}, \rref{AD-037}, \rref{IX-007}}
\end{rulerec}
\begin{rulerec}{AD-021}{Specify included viewpoints in accordance with the viewpoint rules}{Requirement}{Architecture description}{navy}
\rf{Class}{\badge{navy}{Requirement}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture description}}
\rf{Obligation}{The specification of each viewpoint included in an architecture description must satisfy the provisions governing viewpoint specifications.}
\rf{Conditions}{Applies to each included viewpoint, whether written for this description or adopted from elsewhere.}
\rf{Meaning}{Adopting an externally authored viewpoint does not exempt it. If a borrowed viewpoint omits, for example, its framed concerns or its model kinds and legends, the description that uses it inherits the deficiency.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{A conformance check of each viewpoint specification against the viewpoint content rules.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Have adopted third-party viewpoints been assessed, or assumed adequate?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{VP-001}, \rref{VP-002}, \rref{VP-003}, \rref{VP-004}, \rref{VP-005}, \rref{VP-006}, \rref{VP-007}, \rref{VP-010}}
\end{rulerec}
\begin{rulerec}{AD-022}{Every concern is framed by at least one viewpoint}{Requirement}{Architecture description}{navy}
\rf{Class}{\badge{navy}{Requirement}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture description}}
\rf{Obligation}{Each concern identified in the architecture description must be framed by at least one architecture viewpoint.}
\rf{Conditions}{Applies to every identified concern. At least one --- a concern may be framed by more than one viewpoint, and commonly is.}
\rf{Meaning}{This closes the loop between elicitation and expression. To frame a concern is to shape and give expression to it, which is distinct from addressing it in a resulting view. A concern that no viewpoint frames has been identified and then abandoned.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{A concern-to-viewpoint coverage matrix with no empty rows.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Is there any concern in the register with no framing viewpoint? If so, is it covered by a recorded limitation?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{AD-015}, \rref{AD-027}, \rref{AD-009}, \rref{VP-002}}
\end{rulerec}
\begin{rulerec}{AD-023}{Associate each perspective with the viewpoints covering it}{Requirement}{Architecture description}{navy}
\rf{Class}{\badge{navy}{Requirement}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture description}}
\rf{Obligation}{Each stakeholder perspective identified in the architecture description must be associated with the architecture viewpoints that cover that perspective.}
\rf{Conditions}{Applies to every identified perspective.}
\rf{Meaning}{Perspectives frequently serve as the organising axis for a viewpoint set --- rows in a framework grid, for instance. This rule requires that grouping to be made explicit rather than left implicit in the layout of the document.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{A perspective-to-viewpoint matrix.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Does every identified perspective have at least one covering viewpoint recorded?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{AD-011}, \rref{AD-026}, \rref{VP-001}}
\end{rulerec}

\section{Description: views}

The largest group in the catalogue. It establishes the addressing half of the coverage obligations, the required contents of a view, and the latitude a description has in structuring them.

\begin{rulerec}{AD-024}{Include one or more views for each viewpoint used}{Requirement}{Architecture description}{navy}
\rf{Class}{\badge{navy}{Requirement}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture description}}
\rf{Obligation}{An architecture description must include one or more architecture views for each architecture viewpoint it uses.}
\rf{Conditions}{Applies to each viewpoint used. The cardinality is one-to-many, not one-to-one: a single viewpoint may govern several views within one description.}
\rf{Meaning}{A viewpoint that governs several views is still describing one architecture --- for example a functional viewpoint may govern both a functional-chain view expressing behaviour and a function-tree view expressing decomposition. Reviewers should not treat multiple views under one viewpoint as duplication, nor assume a strict pairing.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{A viewpoint-to-view mapping showing at least one view per viewpoint.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Is any viewpoint declared but unrealised by a view? Has a one-view-per-viewpoint assumption been imposed where several are legitimate?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{AD-019}, \rref{AD-032}, \rref{AD-029}}
\end{rulerec}
\begin{rulerec}{AD-025}{Version-identify each view}{Requirement}{Architecture description}{navy}
\rf{Class}{\badge{navy}{Requirement}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture description}}
\rf{Obligation}{Each architecture view must carry version identification, in the form specified by the organisation or the project.}
\rf{Conditions}{Obligation unconditional; scheme delegated.}
\rf{Meaning}{Views change independently of one another and of their viewpoints, so correspondences and review findings need a stable revision anchor.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{A version field on each view and a documented versioning scheme.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Does each view carry an identifier conforming to the organisation's scheme?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{AD-020}, \rref{AD-037}, \rref{AD-031}}
\end{rulerec}
\begin{rulerec}{AD-026}{Every perspective is addressed by at least one view}{Requirement}{Architecture description}{navy}
\rf{Class}{\badge{navy}{Requirement}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture description}}
\rf{Obligation}{Each stakeholder perspective identified in the architecture description must be addressed by at least one view, in accordance with that view's governing viewpoint.}
\rf{Conditions}{Applies to every identified perspective. The addressing must be consistent with the governing viewpoint's conventions --- a view cannot address a perspective by informal assertion.}
\rf{Meaning}{Framing a perspective in a viewpoint is not enough; a view must actually deliver against it. This and the two following rules form the coverage triad that a reviewer works through systematically.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{A perspective-to-view coverage matrix with no empty rows.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Is every perspective addressed, and is the addressing done through the governing viewpoint's conventions rather than around them?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{AD-011}, \rref{AD-023}, \rref{AD-027}, \rref{AD-028}, \rref{AD-029}}
\end{rulerec}
\begin{rulerec}{AD-027}{Every concern is addressed by at least one view}{Requirement}{Architecture description}{navy}
\rf{Class}{\badge{navy}{Requirement}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture description}}
\rf{Obligation}{Each concern identified in the architecture description must be addressed by at least one view, in accordance with that view's governing viewpoint.}
\rf{Conditions}{Applies to every identified concern.}
\rf{Meaning}{The counterpart to framing: framing shapes the concern, addressing answers it. Both are required, and a description can satisfy the framing rule while failing this one.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{A concern-to-view coverage matrix with no empty rows, distinct from the concern-to-viewpoint matrix.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Are framing coverage and addressing coverage checked separately?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{AD-015}, \rref{AD-022}, \rref{AD-026}, \rref{AD-033}}
\end{rulerec}
\begin{rulerec}{AD-028}{Every aspect is addressed by at least one view}{Requirement}{Architecture description}{navy}
\rf{Class}{\badge{navy}{Requirement}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture description}}
\rf{Obligation}{Each aspect identified in the architecture description must be addressed by at least one view, in accordance with that view's governing viewpoint.}
\rf{Conditions}{Applies to every identified aspect.}
\rf{Meaning}{Aspects complete the coverage triad. Because aspects are refinements of concerns, aspect coverage will often follow from concern coverage --- but it is a separate obligation and is separately reviewable.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{An aspect-to-view coverage matrix with no empty rows.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Is aspect coverage demonstrated in its own right rather than inferred from concern coverage?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{AD-017}, \rref{AD-018}, \rref{AD-026}, \rref{AD-027}}
\end{rulerec}
\begin{rulerec}{AD-029}{Each view adheres to its governing viewpoint's conventions}{Requirement}{Architecture description}{navy}
\rf{Class}{\badge{navy}{Requirement}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture description}}
\rf{Obligation}{Each architecture view must adhere to the conventions of its governing architecture viewpoint.}
\rf{Conditions}{Applies to every view.}
\rf{Meaning}{The viewpoint is the contract; the view is the delivery against it. Conventions cover creation, interpretation, presentation and analysis, and may include notation, semantics, required elements and usage guidance. Departures are not silently permitted --- they are known issues to be recorded.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{A per-view conformance note against its viewpoint's conventions, or a checklist derived from the viewpoint specification.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{For each view, has adherence been checked against the specific conventions of its viewpoint rather than against general good practice?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{AD-024}, \rref{AD-034}, \rref{AD-039}, \rref{VP-005}}
\end{rulerec}
\begin{rulerec}{AD-030}{A view may address more than one concern}{Permission}{Architecture description}{steel}
\rf{Class}{\badge{steel}{Permission}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture description}}
\rf{Obligation}{An architecture view is permitted to address more than one concern.}
\rf{Conditions}{Expressly allowed. No maximum is imposed.}
\rf{Meaning}{There is no requirement for a one-concern-per-view decomposition, and a reviewer may not demand that a view be split because it answers several concerns. Combined with the many-to-one relationship from views to viewpoints, this gives considerable latitude in structuring a description.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{Not applicable --- this rule licenses a structure rather than requiring an artefact.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Has a finding been raised merely because a view is multi-purpose?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{AD-027}, \rref{AD-024}, \rref{AD-041}}
\end{rulerec}
\begin{rulerec}{AD-031}{Each view carries identifying and supplementary information}{Requirement}{Architecture description}{navy}
\rf{Class}{\badge{navy}{Requirement}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture description}}
\rf{Obligation}{Each architecture view must include, or provide a reference to, identifying and supplementary information as specified by the organisation or the project.}
\rf{Conditions}{The obligation is unconditional; the content is delegated to the organisation or project. Inclusion by reference is permitted.}
\rf{Meaning}{Views are frequently extracted, circulated and reviewed independently of the whole description, so each must be self-identifying to the standard the organisation has set.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{A standard view header carrying the organisation's required fields.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Is there a defined view-header standard, and does every view satisfy it?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{AD-004}, \rref{AD-025}, \rref{AD-032}}
\end{rulerec}
\begin{rulerec}{AD-032}{Each view identifies its governing viewpoint}{Requirement}{Architecture description}{navy}
\rf{Class}{\badge{navy}{Requirement}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture description}}
\rf{Obligation}{Each architecture view must include, or provide a reference to, the identification of its governing architecture viewpoint.}
\rf{Conditions}{Applies to every view. Inclusion by reference is permitted.}
\rf{Meaning}{Without this link, none of the adherence and coverage obligations can be evaluated, because there is no stated contract to evaluate the view against.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{A governing-viewpoint field in each view header, resolving to the viewpoint register.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Can each view's governing viewpoint be identified from the view itself?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{AD-019}, \rref{AD-024}, \rref{AD-029}}
\end{rulerec}
\begin{rulerec}{AD-033}{Each view carries view components covering the framed concerns}{Requirement}{Architecture description}{navy}
\rf{Class}{\badge{navy}{Requirement}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture description}}
\rf{Obligation}{Each architecture view must include, or provide a reference to, one or more view components that address all of the concerns framed by its governing viewpoint and that cover some or all of the entity of interest with respect to that viewpoint.}
\rf{Conditions}{Two distinct scopes in one provision. Concern coverage is total: all concerns framed by the governing viewpoint must be addressed by the view's components. Entity coverage is partial by permission: some or all of the entity, expressly not necessarily the whole of it.}
\rf{Meaning}{A view need not span the entire entity --- it may legitimately be scoped to one portion by direction, by limited time or resources, or by the narrow scope of the architecting effort. But within its scope it must answer every concern its viewpoint frames. Conflating these two coverage rules is a common review error in both directions.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{A component-to-concern matrix per view, plus an explicit statement of the entity scope each view covers.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Are all viewpoint-framed concerns addressed by components, and is partial entity coverage stated rather than left ambiguous?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{AD-027}, \rref{AD-036}, \rref{AD-040}, \rref{VP-002}}
\end{rulerec}
\begin{rulerec}{AD-034}{Each view records known issues}{Requirement}{Architecture description}{navy}
\rf{Class}{\badge{navy}{Requirement}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture description}}
\rf{Obligation}{Each architecture view must include, or provide a reference to, a record of any known issues within that view with respect to its governing architecture viewpoint.}
\rf{Conditions}{Conditional on such issues existing. Known issues include unresolved matters, risks, exceptions, and deviations from the governing model kinds or legends.}
\rf{Meaning}{This is where departures from convention surface. Open issues can generate decisions to be made; exceptions and deviations can be documented as decision outcomes with rationale. Recording an issue does not repair the deviation --- the view remains non-adherent and the record makes that visible.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{A known-issues section per view, cross-referenced to the decision and rationale records where applicable.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Where a view departs from its viewpoint, model kind or legend, is the departure recorded here or merely absent?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{AD-029}, \rref{AD-039}, \rref{AD-054}, \rref{AD-043}}
\end{rulerec}
\begin{rulerec}{AD-035}{The description may carry information outside any view}{Permission}{Architecture description}{steel}
\rf{Class}{\badge{steel}{Permission}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture description}}
\rf{Obligation}{An architecture description is permitted to include other information that is not part of any architecture view.}
\rf{Conditions}{Expressly allowed. Examples include entity overviews; architecture principles, patterns and styles whose application spans more than one view; referenced bases such as domain or reference architectures; correspondences between views; and architecture rationale.}
\rf{Meaning}{Not everything of architectural value fits inside a view, and material that spans views does not have to be forced into one. Such material assists stakeholders and those maintaining and developing the description.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{Not applicable --- this rule licenses content rather than requiring it.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Has cross-cutting material been artificially assigned to a single view to satisfy a rule that does not exist?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{AD-030}, \rref{AD-041}, \rref{AD-062}}
\end{rulerec}

\section{Description: view components}

Components are the separable units within views. This group carries the model-based and non-model-based distinction and the legend obligation.

\begin{rulerec}{AD-036}{Views are composed of one or more view components}{Requirement}{Architecture description}{navy}
\rf{Class}{\badge{navy}{Requirement}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture description}}
\rf{Obligation}{An architecture view must be composed of one or more view components, in accordance with its governing architecture viewpoint.}
\rf{Conditions}{Applies to every view. Minimum of one component; no maximum.}
\rf{Meaning}{The view is the container, the component is the separable unit. A view is not a single undifferentiated artefact --- its parts are individually identifiable, which is what makes sharing and correspondence possible.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{A component inventory per view.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Is each view decomposed into named components, or is it treated as one monolithic diagram?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{AD-033}, \rref{AD-037}, \rref{AD-041}}
\end{rulerec}
\begin{rulerec}{AD-037}{Version-identify each view component}{Requirement}{Architecture description}{navy}
\rf{Class}{\badge{navy}{Requirement}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture description}}
\rf{Obligation}{Each view component must carry version identification, in the form specified by the organisation or the project.}
\rf{Conditions}{Obligation unconditional; scheme delegated.}
\rf{Meaning}{Components are shared across views and reused across descriptions, so a component's revision must be identifiable independently of the views that contain it.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{A version field on each component and a documented versioning scheme.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Do shared components carry their own version identity rather than inheriting a view's?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{AD-020}, \rref{AD-025}, \rref{AD-041}}
\end{rulerec}
\begin{rulerec}{AD-038}{Each view component identifies its governing model kind, if any}{Requirement}{Architecture description}{navy}
\rf{Class}{\badge{navy}{Requirement}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture description}}
\rf{Obligation}{Each view component must identify its governing model kind, where it has one.}
\rf{Conditions}{Conditional: the qualifier `if any' is material. Model-based components have a governing model kind and must identify it; non-model-based components have none, and the condition is legitimately unmet rather than violated.}
\rf{Meaning}{This rule must not be read as requiring every component to be model-based. The structure expressly admits both model-based components, whose conventions come from a model kind, and non-model-based components, whose conventions come from a legend.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{A governing-model-kind field on each component, permitted to be empty where a legend applies instead.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Where the field is empty, is a legend present? Has a reviewer wrongly required a model kind for narrative or tabular content?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{AD-042}, \rref{AD-039}, \rref{MK-001}, \rref{IX-008}}
\end{rulerec}
\begin{rulerec}{AD-039}{Each view component adheres to its governing viewpoint's conventions}{Requirement}{Architecture description}{navy}
\rf{Class}{\badge{navy}{Requirement}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture description}}
\rf{Obligation}{Each view component must adhere to the conventions of its governing architecture viewpoint.}
\rf{Conditions}{Applies to every component, model-based or not.}
\rf{Meaning}{Adherence is enforced at both levels: the view as a whole and each component within it. A component may additionally be bound by its model kind's conventions or its legend's conventions.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{A per-component adherence note or checklist derived from the viewpoint specification.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Is adherence checked at component granularity, or only at view level?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{AD-029}, \rref{AD-038}, \rref{AD-042}}
\end{rulerec}
\begin{rulerec}{AD-040}{Components may present content selectively within a view}{Permission}{Architecture description}{steel}
\rf{Class}{\badge{steel}{Permission}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture description}}
\rf{Obligation}{Within a view, one or more view components may be used to present selectively some or all of the informational content required by the architecture viewpoint, in order to highlight points of interest.}
\rf{Conditions}{Expressly allowed. Selectivity operates at component level and does not relieve the view of its obligation to address all concerns framed by its viewpoint.}
\rf{Meaning}{Emphasis and abstraction within a view are legitimate. An individual component may deliberately show a subset; completeness is judged across the view's component set, not component by component.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{Not applicable --- this rule licenses a technique rather than requiring an artefact.}

\section{Description: correspondences}

The relational machinery of a description: what must be listed, and what each correspondence must identify.

\begin{rulerec}{AD-046}{Include or reference a list of correspondences}{Requirement}{Architecture description}{navy}
\rf{Class}{\badge{navy}{Requirement}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture description}}
\rf{Obligation}{An architecture description must include, or reference, a list of its description-element correspondences.}
\rf{Conditions}{Unconditional as to the list. Inclusion by reference is permitted.}
\rf{Meaning}{A correspondence is an identified or named relationship between two or more description elements, used to express relations such as equivalence, composition, refinement, consistency, traceability, dependency, constraint, satisfaction and obligation. The description must present them as an enumerable set, not leave them implicit in prose or diagram adjacency.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{A correspondence register or traceability repository referenced from the description.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Is there an enumerable correspondence list, and does it match the relationships actually asserted in the text and diagrams?}

\begin{rulerec}{AD-047}{Each correspondence identifies its participating elements}{Requirement}{Architecture description}{navy}
\rf{Class}{\badge{navy}{Requirement}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture description}}
\rf{Obligation}{Each description-element correspondence must identify one or more participating description elements.}
\rf{Conditions}{Applies to every correspondence. The minimum is one: the elements in a correspondence need not be distinct, and a correspondence may be defined between an element and itself --- for example an activity refined into activities of the same kind, or one that recursively invokes itself.}

\rf{Class}{\badge{steel}{Permission}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture description}}
\rf{Obligation}{A description-element correspondence is permitted to involve elements within a single architecture description or across several architecture descriptions.}
\rf{Conditions}{Expressly allowed. For the purpose of correspondences, an architecture description may itself be treated as a description element of another architecture description.}
\rf{Meaning}{This is what makes multi-description architecting tractable: alternatives can be related for trade-off analysis, descriptions of related entities can be correlated, and a description of a component entity can be linked to that of its containing entity. \section{Description: decisions}

One requirement, surrounded by recommendations and example lists. This is the group most often over-read into a mandatory schema.

\begin{rulerec}{AD-054}{Record essential architecture decisions}{Requirement}{Architecture description}{navy}

\begin{rulerec}{AD-058}{Consider a defined information set when recording decisions}{Recommendation}{Architecture description}{teal}

\rf{Conditions}{Expressly allowed; other mechanisms are not excluded.}
\rf{Meaning}{Decisions are description elements, so the correspondence machinery applies to them as it does to any other element. \section{Framework: adherence and structure}

How a description adheres to a framework, what latitude a framework has to add rules, and how multiple frameworks are reconciled.

\begin{rulerec}{ADF-017}{Criteria for a description's adherence to a framework}{Requirement}{Architecture description framework}{navy}
\rf{Class}{\badge{navy}{Requirement}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture description framework}}
\rf{Obligation}{An architecture description that satisfies the requirements for descriptions adheres to a framework specification when it identifies, and considers the applicability of, each of the following that the framework identifies or specifies: each stakeholder; each stakeholder perspective; each concern; each aspect; each architecture viewpoint; and each correspondence method.}
\rf{Conditions}{Conditional on an adherence claim being made. Adherence is distinct from conformance: a description can conform without adhering to any framework. The test is identify and consider applicability --- not adopt wholesale.}
\rf{Meaning}{Considering applicability and concluding that an element does not apply satisfies the criterion, provided the consideration is evidenced. A description may therefore adhere to a framework while using only part of it, so long as the reasoning is recorded. Note that model kinds and view methods are absent from this list, though they appear in the reconciliation guidance for multiple frameworks.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{An adherence matrix listing every framework-identified element with its disposition --- adopted, adapted, or not applicable with reasons.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Is every framework element dispositioned, and are the not-applicable determinations reasoned?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{ADF-018}, \rref{ADF-019}, \rref{ADF-020}, \rref{CF-003}}
\end{rulerec}
\begin{rulerec}{ADF-018}{A framework may add its own adherence rules}{Permission}{Architecture description framework}{steel}
\rf{Class}{\badge{steel}{Permission}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture description framework}}
\rf{Obligation}{A framework specification is permitted to establish additional rules for adherence.}
\rf{Conditions}{Expressly allowed. Additional rules bind those claiming adherence to that framework; they do not alter the underlying requirements.}
\rf{Meaning}{A framework may be stricter than the baseline --- mandating particular viewpoints, model kinds or correspondence methods. This is how domain governance is layered on without tailoring the requirements themselves.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{An adherence-rules section in the framework specification, distinct from its content catalogue.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Where a framework imposes extra rules, are they stated as adherence rules rather than mixed into descriptive material?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{ADF-017}, \rref{ADF-015}, \rref{CF-007}}
\end{rulerec}
\begin{rulerec}{ADF-019}{A description may adhere to several frameworks, or none}{Permission}{Architecture description framework}{steel}
\rf{Class}{\badge{steel}{Permission}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture description framework}}
\rf{Obligation}{An architecture description may adhere to one or more framework specifications, or to no framework specification at all.}
\rf{Conditions}{Expressly allowed. There is no obligation on a project to adopt a framework, and no prohibition on adopting several.}
\rf{Meaning}{This rule disposes of two common misreadings: that a conforming description must be built under a framework, and that only one framework may be used. Neither holds. A project may equally use no framework and still conform.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{Not applicable --- this rule licenses a choice rather than requiring an artefact.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Has a framework been required where none is needed, or a second framework rejected on principle?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{ADF-017}, \rref{ADF-020}, \rref{AD-061}}
\end{rulerec}
\begin{rulerec}{ADF-020}{Reconciliation is entailed when several frameworks are used}{Explanatory guidance}{Architecture description framework}{slate}
\rf{Class}{\badge{slate}{Explanatory guidance}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture description framework}}
\rf{Obligation}{For an architecture description to adhere to more than one framework specification entails reconciliation, within the description, between each framework's identified stakeholders, concerns, aspects, stakeholder perspectives, architecture viewpoints, model kinds and correspondence methods.}
\rf{Conditions}{Applies where multiple adherence claims are made. Note that this list is broader than the adherence criteria list: it adds model kinds.}
\rf{Meaning}{Two frameworks will use different words for overlapping ideas and will partition the same space differently. Without reconciliation the description carries two parallel vocabularies, and its coverage claims become unverifiable. In practice reconciliation is done with correspondences between the frameworks' elements.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{A cross-framework reconciliation mapping, typically expressed as correspondences.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Where two frameworks are claimed, is there a mapping, or two unreconciled vocabularies?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{ADF-017}, \rref{ADF-019}, \rref{AD-046}, \rref{ADF-016}}
\end{rulerec}
\begin{rulerec}{ADF-021}{A framework may have a structuring formalism with structural categories}{Permission}{Architecture description framework}{steel}
\rf{Class}{\badge{steel}{Permission}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture description framework}}
\rf{Obligation}{A framework is permitted to have a structuring formalism with one or more structural categories, providing ways of representing relationships among elements of the architecture and improving the opportunity to analyse interactions among them.}
\rf{Conditions}{Expressly allowed. Structural categories include constructs such as domains, model kinds, perspectives, aspects, interrogatives, levels of abstraction, subjects of concern, aspects of concern, phases, layers and architectural tiers.}
\rf{Meaning}{The familiar framework grid is one instance of this permission, not an obligation. The categories result from correspondence methods that group description elements into configurations useful for presentation, analysis and management.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{Not applicable --- this rule licenses a structure rather than requiring one.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Has a grid structure been demanded of a framework that organises itself differently?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{ADF-008}, \rref{ADF-012}}
\end{rulerec}
\begin{rulerec}{ADF-022}{Scope of use ranges from generic to particular}{Explanatory guidance}{Architecture description framework}{slate}
\rf{Class}{\badge{slate}{Explanatory guidance}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture description framework}}
\rf{Obligation}{The intended scope of use of a framework ranges on a scale from the very generic --- all industries and application domains, all kinds of entity, multiple purposes --- to the very particular, such as a given industry, application domain or kind of entity, a particular entity, a particular entity at a given stage of its life, or a particular purpose.}
\rf{Conditions}{Applies to interpretation of a framework's declared scope.}
\rf{Meaning}{A generic framework functions as a reference or partial model of the information to be produced, and needs specialisation before use; a domain-specialised framework needs less. Reviewers should calibrate their expectations of specificity to the declared scope rather than to a fixed standard.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{Not applicable --- this rule explains how to read the scope statement.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Has a reference framework been criticised for generality that its declared scope makes appropriate?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{ADF-001}, \rref{ADF-015}}
\end{rulerec}

\rf{Obligation}{A viewpoint specification must include, or reference, any stakeholder perspectives associated with the viewpoint.}
\rf{Conditions}{Conditional through the qualifier `any'. A viewpoint associated with no perspective has none to identify.}
\rf{Meaning}{Where perspectives organise the viewpoint set, this is the field that records the association, and it is what allows a description to demonstrate that each of its perspectives is covered.}

\begin{rulerec}{VP-002}{Identify the concerns framed by the viewpoint}{Requirement}{Architecture viewpoint}{navy}
\rf{Class}{\badge{navy}{Requirement}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture viewpoint}}
\rf{Obligation}{A viewpoint specification must include, or reference, one or more concerns framed by the viewpoint.}
\rf{Conditions}{Minimum of one. A concern may be framed by more than one viewpoint.}
\rf{Meaning}{This is the core of a viewpoint specification. Framing means shaping and giving expression to a concern, as distinct from addressing it in a resulting view. The framed-concern set determines what every view under this viewpoint must address. Stating what kinds of issue the viewpoint is not appropriate for is useful protection against overuse.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{A framed-concern list in the viewpoint specification, and optionally a statement of inappropriate uses.}

\begin{rulerec}{VP-003}{Identify aspects related to those concerns}{Requirement}{Architecture viewpoint}{navy}
\rf{Class}{\badge{navy}{Requirement}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture viewpoint}}
\rf{Obligation}{A viewpoint specification must include, or reference, one or more aspects related to the concerns it frames.}
\rf{Conditions}{Minimum of one. The aspects must relate to the framed concerns.}
\rf{Meaning}{Aspects refine concerns and are used to establish the viewpoint's conventions; they are then evidenced in the resulting views. A viewpoint whose aspects bear no relation to its concerns has not met the obligation.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{An aspect list in the viewpoint specification, related to the framed concerns.}

\rf{Conditions}{Applies to the concerns identified as framed by this viewpoint. When the viewpoint is selected and applied within a description, these typical stakeholders are replaced by the known stakeholders identified in that description.}
\rf{Meaning}{In a reusable viewpoint the stakeholders are types; in an applied one they are the real parties named by the description. Recording the typical audience is what lets an architect judge whether the viewpoint will be useful for a particular entity.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{A typical-stakeholder list in the viewpoint specification.}

\rf{Obligation}{A viewpoint specification must include, or reference, the model kinds and legends to be used when constructing views governed by the viewpoint.}
\rf{Conditions}{Both are named in the provision. A view component is governed by a model kind or by a legend identified by its viewpoint; a model kind or legend may be used by more than one viewpoint within a description.}
\rf{Meaning}{These are the key modelling and notational resources the viewpoint makes available, establishing the vocabularies from which views are built. Because components may be non-model-based, the viewpoint must provide for legends as well as model kinds.}

\rf{Obligation}{A viewpoint specification must include, or reference, the correspondence methods capturing relations within the resulting views and their view components.}
\rf{Conditions}{Applies to the viewpoint's own relational rules. Methods defined by a viewpoint or its model kinds may be applied across components, across views within a description, or across descriptions.}
\rf{Meaning}{Viewpoint-supplied correspondence methods are inherited by any description using the viewpoint and must appear in that description's method list.}

\end{rulerec}
\begin{rulerec}{VP-007}{Reference any sources of information about the viewpoint}{Requirement}{Architecture viewpoint}{navy}
\rf{Class}{\badge{navy}{Requirement}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture viewpoint}}
\rf{Obligation}{A viewpoint specification must include, or reference, references to any sources of information about the viewpoint.}
\rf{Conditions}{Conditional through the qualifier `any'. Sources include authorship, history, literature references and prior art.}
\rf{Meaning}{Viewpoints usually descend from prior knowledge, experience and established practice in a domain. Recording provenance lets adopters assess maturity and consult the underlying material. This obligation to record sources holds regardless of where the viewpoint was obtained.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{A sources section naming author, history, literature and prior art.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Is the viewpoint's provenance recorded, or does it appear without attribution?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{MK-004}, \rref{VP-012}}
\end{rulerec}

\section{Viewpoint: further provisions}

Two recommendations, one requirement on legends, and two permissions governing how a viewpoint may be constructed and where it may live.

\begin{rulerec}{VP-008}{Identify view methods for the viewpoint}{Recommendation}{Architecture viewpoint}{teal}
\rf{Class}{\badge{teal}{Recommendation}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture viewpoint}}
\rf{Obligation}{A viewpoint specification should identify the view methods used to create, interpret or analyse the views governed by that viewpoint.}
\rf{Conditions}{Advised, not obligatory --- although a related provision makes view method definition mandatory where guidance on constructing or using views is necessary, and the conceptual material describes a viewpoint specification as including one or more view methods. See the interpretation issue.}
\rf{Meaning}{View methods fall into construction methods (how views are prepared), interpretive methods (how they are to be understood), and analysis methods (how they are checked, reasoned about, transformed, predicted from and evaluated).}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{A view-method section in the viewpoint specification covering construction, interpretation and analysis.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Given the conditional requirement elsewhere, would omitting view methods here leave view construction or use without necessary guidance?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{VM-001}, \rref{VM-002}, \rref{VP-009}, \rref{IX-004}}
\end{rulerec}
\begin{rulerec}{VP-009}{Identify one or more model kinds}{Recommendation}{Architecture viewpoint}{teal}
\rf{Class}{\badge{teal}{Recommendation}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture viewpoint}}
\rf{Obligation}{A viewpoint specification should identify one or more model kinds.}
\rf{Conditions}{Advised. Stated alongside the view-method recommendation, and overlapping with the mandatory provision covering model kinds and legends.}
\rf{Meaning}{Because the mandatory provision governs, model kinds must be identified in a conforming viewpoint specification; this weaker restatement adds emphasis toward model-based practice rather than relaxing the obligation.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{Satisfied by the same evidence as the mandatory model kind and legend rule.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Has the weaker restatement been mistaken for the governing provision?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{VP-005}, \rref{IX-004}}
\end{rulerec}
\begin{rulerec}{VP-010}{Each legend guides interpretation of the components it documents}{Requirement}{Architecture viewpoint}{navy}
\rf{Class}{\badge{navy}{Requirement}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture viewpoint}}
\rf{Obligation}{Each legend must provide guidance to users in interpreting the view components that it documents.}
\rf{Conditions}{Applies to every legend. A legend is an informal documentation of conventions within a description.}
\rf{Meaning}{A legend is not merely a key of symbols: it must actually enable a reader to interpret the component. This is the interpretive safeguard for non-model-based content, which has no formal syntax and semantics to fall back on. A symbology table is one example of a legend.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{Legends that state intended use, terminology and notation, not only glyph-to-label pairings.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Could a reader unfamiliar with the project interpret the component from its legend alone?}

\end{rulerec}
\begin{rulerec}{VP-011}{A viewpoint specification may use correspondence methods}{Permission}{Architecture viewpoint}{steel}
\rf{Class}{\badge{steel}{Permission}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture viewpoint}}
\rf{Obligation}{The specification of a viewpoint is permitted to use correspondence methods.}
\rf{Conditions}{Expressly allowed.}
\rf{Meaning}{Beyond identifying the methods that govern relations within its resulting views, a viewpoint may itself be constructed using correspondence methods --- for instance to relate its own elements to those of other viewpoints or of a framework.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{Not applicable --- this rule licenses a technique rather than requiring an artefact.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Where a viewpoint asserts relations to other viewpoints, are they expressed through methods?}

\begin{rulerec}{VP-012}{A viewpoint specification may be sited in several ways}{Permission}{Architecture viewpoint}{steel}
\rf{Class}{\badge{steel}{Permission}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture viewpoint}}
\rf{Obligation}{The specification of a viewpoint may be included as part of an architecture description, as part of the specification of a framework or a language, or set out individually under the viewpoint rules.}
\rf{Conditions}{Expressly allowed. All three sitings are equally admissible.}
\rf{Meaning}{This is what supports reusable viewpoint libraries: a viewpoint can be authored once, evaluated on its own, and then adopted by reference into many descriptions. In some communities and frameworks, `view specification' is used as a synonym for viewpoint.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{Not applicable --- this rule licenses a placement rather than requiring one.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Has a standalone or framework-sited viewpoint been rejected for not being embedded in the description?}

\section{Model kind specifications}

Four requirements, three of them conditional through the qualifier ‘any’.

\begin{rulerec}{MK-001}{Specify the model kind's conventions}{Requirement}{Model kind}{navy}
\rf{Class}{\badge{navy}{Requirement}\quad\badge{slate}{Model kind}}
\rf{Obligation}{A model kind specification must include, or reference, its conventions --- such as the definition of the language, notation or modelling technique comprising the model kind.}
\rf{Conditions}{Unconditional. The obligation may be met in a number of ways, including a metamodel, a grammar, or a template that defines the structure and interpretation of models of that kind.}
\rf{Meaning}{A model kind is a category of model distinguished by its key characteristics and modelling conventions; it determines the conventions for model-based view components. A metamodel approach would present the entities, attributes, relationships and constraints of models of this kind; within a description, instances of these become description elements.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{A metamodel, grammar, template, or language definition, or a reference to an existing modelling language with its syntax, semantics and tool support described as needed.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Do the stated conventions determine what a valid model of this kind contains and how it is read?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{AD-038}, \rref{VP-005}, \rref{MK-002}}
\end{rulerec}
\begin{rulerec}{MK-002}{Identify any associated view and correspondence methods}{Requirement}{Model kind}{navy}
\rf{Class}{\badge{navy}{Requirement}\quad\badge{slate}{Model kind}}
\rf{Obligation}{A model kind specification must include, or reference, any view methods and correspondence methods associated with the model kind.}
\rf{Conditions}{Conditional through the qualifier `any'. A model kind with no associated methods has none to identify.}
\rf{Meaning}{Correspondences internal to a single component are normally part of that component's model kind conventions, whereas methods declared here are typically those reaching beyond it. Methods declared by a model kind are inherited by descriptions using it.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{View-method and correspondence-method lists in the model kind specification, permitted to be empty.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Have model-kind-supplied methods been carried into the adopting description's method list?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{AD-051}, \rref{AD-052}, \rref{VM-002}, \rref{VP-006}}
\end{rulerec}
\begin{rulerec}{MK-003}{Include any version identification}{Requirement}{Model kind}{navy}
\rf{Class}{\badge{navy}{Requirement}\quad\badge{slate}{Model kind}}
\rf{Obligation}{A model kind specification must include, or reference, any version identification as specified by the organisation or the project.}
\rf{Conditions}{Doubly qualified: `any' and `as specified by the organisation or project'. Where the organisation has specified no scheme for model kinds, there is nothing to include.}
\rf{Meaning}{The qualification here is weaker than the corresponding rules for viewpoints, views and components, which require version identification outright. Reviewers should apply the weaker form rather than importing the stricter one by analogy.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{A version field in the model kind specification where an organisational scheme exists.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Does the organisation have a versioning scheme for model kinds, and if so is it applied?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{AD-020}, \rref{AD-037}, \rref{ADF-002}}
\end{rulerec}
\begin{rulerec}{MK-004}{Reference any sources of information about the model kind}{Requirement}{Model kind}{navy}
\rf{Class}{\badge{navy}{Requirement}\quad\badge{slate}{Model kind}}
\rf{Obligation}{A model kind specification must include, or reference, any sources of information about the model kind.}
\rf{Conditions}{Conditional through the qualifier `any'.}
\rf{Meaning}{Model kinds are frequently adopted from established techniques, and recording where a technique comes from lets adopters consult the source material and assess its standing. As with viewpoints, the obligation to record sources is independent of where the model kind was obtained.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{A sources section naming origin, literature and prior art.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Is the model kind's provenance recorded?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{VP-007}, \rref{MK-001}}
\end{rulerec}

\section{View methods}

A permission, a conditional requirement, and six recommendations split symmetrically between model and non-model information sources.

\begin{rulerec}{VM-001}{A viewpoint may include view methods}{Permission}{View methods}{steel}
\rf{Class}{\badge{steel}{Permission}\quad\badge{slate}{View methods}}
\rf{Obligation}{A viewpoint specification is permitted to include one or more view methods.}
\rf{Conditions}{Expressly allowed. Note that this permission sits alongside a recommendation to identify view methods and a conditional requirement to define them where guidance is necessary; the conceptual material states more strongly that a viewpoint specification includes one or more view methods. See the interpretation issue.}
\rf{Meaning}{View methods are usually defined in a viewpoint and then referenced or used by model kinds, frameworks and languages. Taking the three provisions together, the practical position is that view methods are optional in form but effectively required wherever construction or use of views needs guidance.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{Not applicable --- this rule licenses inclusion rather than requiring it.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{If no view methods are present, can views actually be constructed and interpreted from the viewpoint alone?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{VM-002}, \rref{VP-008}, \rref{VP-009}, \rref{IX-004}}
\end{rulerec}
\begin{rulerec}{VM-002}{Define view methods where guidance on views is necessary}{Requirement}{View methods}{navy}
\rf{Class}{\badge{navy}{Requirement}\quad\badge{slate}{View methods}}
\rf{Obligation}{View methods must be defined in the specifications of model kinds, viewpoints, languages and frameworks when it is necessary to provide guidance on how views are constructed or used.}
\rf{Conditions}{Conditional on necessity. Where no guidance is necessary, the obligation does not arise. Four sites are named: model kinds, viewpoints, languages and frameworks.}
\rf{Meaning}{The condition is a judgement, and a defensible one requires the project to have asked whether guidance is needed rather than to have assumed it is not. Where guidance is necessary, defining it becomes mandatory in whichever of the four specifications applies.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{A necessity determination recorded for each viewpoint, and defined view methods wherever the determination was affirmative.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Was the necessity question asked, and can the answer be defended for each viewpoint?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{VM-001}, \rref{VP-008}, \rref{ADF-013}, \rref{ADL-002}, \rref{MK-002}, \rref{IX-004}}
\end{rulerec}
\begin{rulerec}{VM-003}{Define how elements are portrayed from a model source}{Recommendation}{View methods}{teal}
\rf{Class}{\badge{teal}{Recommendation}\quad\badge{slate}{View methods}}
\rf{Obligation}{Where a model is used as an information source in creating a view, a view method should define how description elements will be portrayed in the view component.}
\rf{Conditions}{Advised, and conditional on a model being used as an information source.}
\rf{Meaning}{A view component derived from a model is a presentation of that model, not the model itself. This rule asks for the portrayal mapping to be explicit, so that a reader can tell what the depiction asserts about the underlying model.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{A portrayal specification in the view method, mapping model constructs to depicted elements.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{For model-derived components, is the mapping from model to depiction stated?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{VM-004}, \rref{VM-005}, \rref{VM-006}, \rref{AD-038}}
\end{rulerec}
\begin{rulerec}{VM-004}{Define how model data are transformed for use in a component}{Recommendation}{View methods}{teal}
\rf{Class}{\badge{teal}{Recommendation}\quad\badge{slate}{View methods}}
\rf{Obligation}{Where a model is used as an information source in creating a view, a view method should define how model data are transformed or translated for use in the view component.}
\rf{Conditions}{Advised, and conditional in the same way.}
\rf{Meaning}{Transformation is where information is most easily lost or distorted --- through filtering, aggregation, abstraction or reinterpretation. Making the transformation explicit is what allows a depiction to be traced back to its source and re-derived when the model changes.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{A transformation specification, or a documented generation pipeline from model to component.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Can a component derived from a model be regenerated, or is it a hand-edited snapshot?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{VM-003}, \rref{VM-005}, \rref{VM-007}}
\end{rulerec}
\begin{rulerec}{VM-005}{Define how cross-source relationships are portrayed}{Recommendation}{View methods}{teal}
\rf{Class}{\badge{teal}{Recommendation}\quad\badge{slate}{View methods}}
\rf{Obligation}{Where a model is used as an information source in creating a view, a view method should define how relationships between information from different sources are portrayed in the view component.}
\rf{Conditions}{Advised, and conditional in the same way.}
\rf{Meaning}{Components routinely combine material from several sources, and the relationships between those bodies of information are exactly where ambiguity accumulates. This rule asks for the depiction of cross-source relationships to be governed rather than improvised.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{A specification of how cross-source relationships are depicted, cross-referenced to the correspondence methods where applicable.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Where a component draws on several sources, is the depiction of the links between them defined?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{VM-003}, \rref{VM-004}, \rref{VM-008}, \rref{AD-045}}
\end{rulerec}
\begin{rulerec}{VM-006}{Define how non-model content is portrayed as elements}{Recommendation}{View methods}{teal}
\rf{Class}{\badge{teal}{Recommendation}\quad\badge{slate}{View methods}}
\rf{Obligation}{Where a non-model is used as an information source in creating a view, a view method should define how its contents are portrayed in the view component as description elements.}
\rf{Conditions}{Advised, and conditional on a non-model information source being used.}
\rf{Meaning}{The provisions for non-model sources mirror those for model sources exactly, which is a deliberate signal that non-model content is a first-class information source and not an unmanaged residue. Expert opinion, narrative and tabular material all fall here.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{A portrayal specification covering non-model sources, alongside the legend for the resulting component.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Is non-model content governed as deliberately as model-derived content?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{VM-003}, \rref{VM-007}, \rref{VM-008}, \rref{AD-042}}
\end{rulerec}
\begin{rulerec}{VM-007}{Define how non-model data are transformed for use}{Recommendation}{View methods}{teal}
\rf{Class}{\badge{teal}{Recommendation}\quad\badge{slate}{View methods}}
\rf{Obligation}{Where a non-model is used as an information source in creating a view, a view method should define how the non-model-related data are transformed or translated for use in the view component.}
\rf{Conditions}{Advised, and conditional in the same way.}
\rf{Meaning}{Selecting, summarising and reframing non-model material is a transformation like any other, and is if anything more prone to unrecorded editorial judgement than a model-to-diagram transformation.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{A transformation specification for non-model sources.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Is the path from source material to component content traceable?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{VM-004}, \rref{VM-006}, \rref{VM-008}}
\end{rulerec}
\begin{rulerec}{VM-008}{Define how cross-source relationships are portrayed for non-model sources}{Recommendation}{View methods}{teal}
\rf{Class}{\badge{teal}{Recommendation}\quad\badge{slate}{View methods}}
\rf{Obligation}{Where a non-model is used as an information source in creating a view, a view method should define how relationships between information from different sources are portrayed in the view component.}
\rf{Conditions}{Advised, and conditional in the same way.}
\rf{Meaning}{Completes the symmetry between model and non-model source handling. Where a component mixes model-derived and non-model content --- flow diagrams beside an explanatory narrative, for instance --- both sets of provisions are engaged.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{A specification of cross-source relationship depiction covering non-model sources.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{For mixed-source components, have both the model and non-model provisions been applied?}

\chapter{Interpretation issues}

The provisions below are catalogued separately because their status or interpretation is not settled by the source material. They are not presented as requirements. Each records the difficulty, the reading this guide recommends, and what an adopting organisation should decide. A project claiming conformance should take a documented position on each of the items in this chapter rather than leaving them to be resolved differently by each reviewer.

\begin{rulerec}{IX-001}{Obligation-strength wording inside an explanatory note on correspondence methods}{Ambiguous provision}{Architecture description}{amber}
\rf{Class}{\badge{amber}{Ambiguous provision}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture description}}
\rf{Obligation}{An explanatory note attached to the correspondence-method provisions states, in obligation wording, that for each applied correspondence method a description records whether the method holds --- that is, whether an associated correspondence can be shown to be satisfied --- or else records all known violations, a method being violated where an associated correspondence cannot be shown to be satisfied or where no associated correspondence exists.}
\rf{Conditions}{The wording carries requirement strength but sits in material that is not a source of obligations, creating a conflict between placement and phrasing.}
\rf{Meaning}{The status is genuinely unclear. The conservative reading, and the one this guide recommends, is to treat satisfaction-or-violation recording as expected practice: it is the only mechanism by which the correspondence machinery produces a reviewable result, and it is closely aligned with the requirement to record known inconsistencies. Projects claiming conformance should record it and should not rely on the placement to avoid doing so.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{A satisfaction record per applied correspondence method: holds, or a list of known violations.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{For each method in the method list, is there a satisfaction status or a violation list? Has the project taken a documented position on this provision's status?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{AD-051}, \rref{AD-053}, \rref{AD-043}, \rref{AD-044}, \rref{CF-001}}
\end{rulerec}
\begin{rulerec}{IX-002}{Framework version identification appears at two strengths}{Ambiguous provision}{Architecture description framework}{amber}
\rf{Class}{\badge{amber}{Ambiguous provision}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture description framework}}
\rf{Obligation}{Version identification of a framework appears once among the framework's mandatory content and again, at recommendation strength, among the content a framework should specify where expected in a conforming description.}
\rf{Conditions}{Affects framework specifications only.}
\rf{Meaning}{The two provisions cannot both govern. The mandatory statement is the stronger and the unconditional one, so it governs: a conforming framework specification carries version identification. The weaker restatement should not be read as creating an escape from it.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{Version identification present in the framework specification, satisfying the mandatory provision.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Has anyone argued from the weaker provision that framework version identification is optional?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{ADF-002}, \rref{ADF-014}}
\end{rulerec}
\begin{rulerec}{IX-003}{Cross-reference mismatch in the note on applied viewpoints}{Ambiguous provision}{Architecture viewpoint}{amber}
\rf{Class}{\badge{amber}{Ambiguous provision}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture viewpoint}}
\rf{Obligation}{A note explaining that typical stakeholders are replaced by the description's known stakeholders when a viewpoint is applied cross-references the viewpoint content list by an item letter that does not correspond to the stakeholder item.}
\rf{Conditions}{Affects only the reading of that note.}
\rf{Meaning}{This is an editorial defect rather than a substantive one. The intent is unambiguous from the text of the note: on application within a description, typical stakeholders give way to that description's identified stakeholders. Reviewers should apply the intent.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{Applied viewpoints in a description showing the description's identified stakeholders rather than generic types.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Has the mismatch caused the substitution rule to be applied to the wrong viewpoint content item?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{VP-004}, \rref{VP-001}}
\end{rulerec}
\begin{rulerec}{IX-004}{View methods are stated at three different strengths}{Ambiguous provision}{View methods}{amber}
\rf{Class}{\badge{amber}{Ambiguous provision}\quad\badge{slate}{View methods}}
\rf{Obligation}{The conceptual material states that a viewpoint specification includes one or more view methods; the viewpoint content provisions make identifying view methods a recommendation; and the view method provisions make their inclusion a permission while separately requiring their definition where guidance on constructing or using views is necessary.}
\rf{Conditions}{Affects viewpoint specifications and, through them, frameworks and languages that supply viewpoints.}
\rf{Meaning}{The declarative statement carries no obligation strength, so the operative provisions are the recommendation and the conditional requirement. The workable synthesis is: a viewpoint need not include view methods, but must define them where guidance is necessary, and should identify them in any case. In practice a viewpoint intended for use by anyone other than its author will nearly always meet the necessity condition.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{A recorded necessity determination per viewpoint, with view methods defined where the answer is affirmative.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Has a viewpoint been shipped without view methods on the strength of the permission alone, without testing the necessity condition?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{VM-001}, \rref{VM-002}, \rref{VP-008}, \rref{VP-009}}
\end{rulerec}
\begin{rulerec}{IX-005}{Obligation-strength wording in the explanatory material on framework specialisation}{Ambiguous provision}{Architecture description framework}{amber}
\rf{Class}{\badge{amber}{Ambiguous provision}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture description framework}}
\rf{Obligation}{The explanatory discussion of framework specialisation uses obligation wording in stating that concerns specific to a particular effort are to be addressed, requiring additional viewpoints to be specified and used.}
\rf{Conditions}{Occurs in material that is expressly not a source of obligations.}
\rf{Meaning}{No new obligation arises from this passage. Its substance is nonetheless already required by the operative rules: every identified concern must be framed by a viewpoint and addressed by a view, so effort-specific concerns pull in additional viewpoints by that route rather than by this passage.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{Satisfied by the concern-framing and concern-addressing coverage evidence.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Has this passage been cited as an independent requirement rather than as commentary on the coverage rules?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{CF-001}, \rref{AD-022}, \rref{AD-027}, \rref{ADF-022}}
\end{rulerec}
\begin{rulerec}{IX-006}{Where consideration of prior evaluation recommendations is to be recorded}{Ambiguous provision}{Architecture description}{amber}
\rf{Class}{\badge{amber}{Ambiguous provision}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture description}}
\rf{Obligation}{The provision requiring consideration of recommendations from previous evaluations does not name the party who must consider them and does not state that the consideration is to be recorded in the architecture description.}
\rf{Conditions}{Affects descriptions where prior evaluations of this or a related architecture exist.}
\rf{Meaning}{The obligation to consider is clear; its evidential footprint is not. Since an unrecorded consideration cannot be demonstrated in a conformance claim, the practical reading is that the consideration should be recorded somewhere retrievable --- in the description, in its decision records, or in a referenced evaluation disposition log.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{A disposition record for each prior recommendation, referenced from the description.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{If challenged, could the project demonstrate that prior recommendations were considered?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{AD-008}, \rref{AD-054}, \rref{AD-063}}
\end{rulerec}
\begin{rulerec}{IX-007}{Viewpoint version identification arises only through use in a description}{Ambiguous provision}{Architecture viewpoint}{amber}
\rf{Class}{\badge{amber}{Ambiguous provision}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture viewpoint}}
\rf{Obligation}{Version identification of a viewpoint is required by the provisions on including viewpoints in an architecture description, but does not appear among the mandatory content of a viewpoint specification.}
\rf{Conditions}{Affects standalone viewpoint specifications and viewpoint libraries.}
\rf{Meaning}{A viewpoint conformance claim can therefore be made without version identification, while the same viewpoint used inside a description must carry it. Since viewpoints are written to be reused, the sensible practice is to version-identify them at authoring time regardless of which claim is being made.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{A version identifier on every published viewpoint specification.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Do library viewpoints carry versions, or is versioning applied only when they are adopted?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{AD-020}, \rref{VP-005}, \rref{CF-005}}
\end{rulerec}
\begin{rulerec}{IX-008}{Mandatory model kinds in viewpoints against optional model kinds in components}{Ambiguous provision}{Architecture viewpoint}{amber}
\rf{Class}{\badge{amber}{Ambiguous provision}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture viewpoint}}
\rf{Obligation}{A viewpoint specification must identify model kinds and legends, while a view component is required to identify its governing model kind only where it has one, and takes a legend where it has none.}
\rf{Conditions}{Affects the relationship between viewpoint specifications and the components of views governed by them.}
\rf{Meaning}{These provisions are compatible rather than contradictory, but the pairing is regularly misapplied. The viewpoint must make both kinds of convention available; an individual component then draws on whichever fits. It does not follow that every component must be model-based, and it does not follow that a viewpoint may supply model kinds alone.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{A viewpoint providing both model kinds and legends, and a component inventory in which some entries legitimately have no governing model kind.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Has a reviewer required every component to be model-based, or accepted a viewpoint that provides no legends?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{AD-038}, \rref{AD-042}, \rref{VP-005}, \rref{VP-010}}
\end{rulerec}
\begin{rulerec}{IX-009}{The condition governing the advised framework content is undefined}{Ambiguous provision}{Architecture description framework}{amber}
\rf{Class}{\badge{amber}{Ambiguous provision}\quad\badge{slate}{Architecture description framework}}
\rf{Obligation}{The content a framework should specify is introduced by a condition --- that the content is expected in a conforming architecture description --- without stating who determines that expectation or how.}
\rf{Conditions}{Affects the whole set of advised framework content: perspectives, aspects, structuring formalisms, model kinds, legends, languages, correspondence methods, view methods and version identification.}
\rf{Meaning}{The most workable reading is that the framework author decides, when specifying the framework, what descriptions adhering to it will be expected to contain, and then supplies the corresponding content. On that reading the condition is self-referential to the framework's own adherence rules, which the framework is permitted to establish.}
\rf{Evidence\,\ig}{An explicit statement in the framework of what it expects adhering descriptions to contain, aligned with the content it supplies.}
\rf{Check\,\ig}{Does the framework state its expectations of adhering descriptions, or leave the condition unresolved?}
\rf{Related}{\rref{ADF-006}, \rref{ADF-007}, \rref{ADF-008}, \rref{ADF-009}, \rref{ADF-010}, \rref{ADF-011}, \rref{ADF-012}, \rref{ADF-013}, \rref{ADF-014}, \rref{ADF-018}}
\end{rulerec}

existing entity. Nothing in this chapter creates an obligation.}

\section{Stage 1 --- Frame the effort}

Fix the subject and the reason for describing it before anything else, because
four later identification rules are qualified by the purpose.

\begin{itemize}
  \item Identify the entity of interest and bound it explicitly against
        neighbouring entities (\rref{AD-001}).
  \item Identify the expected environment --- external entities and the
        developmental, technological, business, operational, organisational,
        political, economic, legal, regulatory, ecological, social and physical
        influences that bear on it (\rref{AD-002}).
  \item Write a purpose statement specific enough to exclude something
        (\rref{AD-003}).
  \item Settle what identifying and supplementary information the organisation
        expects, if no house standard already exists
        (\rref{AD-004}).
  \item Retrieve recommendations from any previous evaluations of this or
        related architectures, and plan how their disposition will be recorded
        (\rref{AD-008}, \rref{IX-006}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Stage 2 --- Decide the codifying artefacts}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Decide whether to adopt a framework --- one, several or none
        (\rref{ADF-019}) --- and record why
        (\rref{AD-061}).
  \item If adopting more than one, plan the reconciliation between their
        vocabularies now, not at review (\rref{ADF-020}).
  \item Decide which description languages will be used, and record why
        (\rref{AD-061}). Using several requires care.
  \item Establish the organisation's version identification schemes. Several
        rules require version identification \emph{as specified by the
        organisation or project}, so an absent scheme blocks them
        (\rref{AD-020}, \rref{AD-025},
        \rref{AD-037}).
  \item Establish the decision recording and selection strategy
        (\rref{AD-056}).
\end{itemize}

\tip{If your organisation authors its own framework, language or model kinds,
those artefacts have their own rule sets and their own conformance claims. Build
and review them as separate deliverables --- see
\rref{ADF-001} onward, \rref{ADL-001} onward
and \rref{MK-001} onward. If you merely \emph{use} someone
else's, those rule sets are not engaged.}

\section{Stage 3 --- Elicit the architecture considerations}

Work outward from stakeholders, and expect to iterate: aspects surface concerns,
and concerns surface stakeholders.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Identify stakeholders whose concerns are fundamental and
        purpose-consistent (\rref{AD-006}), including parties
        whose interests are adverse.
  \item Identify perspectives and attach their holders
        (\rref{AD-011}, \rref{AD-012}); search
        actively for present and future ones
        (\rref{AD-013}).
  \item Identify concerns and attach their holders
        (\rref{AD-015}, \rref{AD-016}).
        Phrasing concerns as questions at a detail level matched to the purpose
        tends to communicate better.
  \item Enumerate each perspective's resulting concerns from the concern
        register (\rref{AD-014}).
  \item Identify aspects and relate them to the concerns that apply
        (\rref{AD-017}, \rref{AD-018}).
  \item Consider the architecture's possible impacts on current and future
        stakeholders (\rref{AD-007}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Stage 4 --- Select and specify viewpoints}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Select viewpoints such that every identified concern is framed by at
        least one (\rref{AD-022}) and every perspective is
        associated with covering viewpoints
        (\rref{AD-023}).
  \item Assess every adopted viewpoint against the viewpoint content rules on
        intake, whether it came from a framework, a language, a library or a
        publication (\rref{AD-021}).
  \item Include or reference each viewpoint and version-identify it
        (\rref{AD-019}, \rref{AD-020}).
  \item For each viewpoint, determine whether guidance on constructing or using
        views is necessary; where it is, define view methods
        (\rref{VM-002}, \rref{IX-004}).
  \item Record why each viewpoint was selected
        (\rref{AD-060}).
\end{itemize}

\why{Treating the viewpoint set as a contract, agreed before detailed
architecting begins, is what makes the later coverage review uneventful.
Architect and stakeholders can agree in advance which concerns will be framed
and what notational conventions will be used to address them, which removes most
of the surprise from acceptance.}

\section{Stage 5 --- Build views and components}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Produce one \emph{or more} views per viewpoint
        (\rref{AD-024}); do not force a one-to-one pairing.
  \item Compose each view from one or more components
        (\rref{AD-036}); share components across views rather
        than duplicating content (\rref{AD-041}).
  \item For each component, identify its governing model kind if it has one
        (\rref{AD-038}); where it has none, write a legend that
        genuinely guides interpretation
        (\rref{AD-042}, \rref{VP-010}).
  \item Version-identify views and components
        (\rref{AD-025}, \rref{AD-037}).
  \item Record each view's governing viewpoint, its identifying information, its
        components and its known issues
        (\rref{AD-031}--\rref{AD-034}).
  \item State each view's entity coverage explicitly --- partial coverage is
        permitted, ambiguity is not helpful
        (\rref{AD-033}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Stage 6 --- Establish correspondences and check consistency}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Compile the correspondence-method list, including methods inherited from
        adopted frameworks, languages, viewpoints and model kinds
        (\rref{AD-051}, \rref{AD-052},
        \rref{AD-053}).
  \item Record correspondences with their participating elements, participating
        descriptions and governing methods
        (\rref{AD-046}--\rref{AD-050}).
  \item Record, for each applied method, whether it holds or what its known
        violations are (\rref{IX-001}).
  \item Analyse consistency across views, components and other elements
        (\rref{AD-044}), and record any known inconsistencies
        (\rref{AD-043}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Stage 7 --- Record decisions and rationale}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Apply the selection criteria to identify essential decisions and record
        them (\rref{AD-054}).
  \item Record rationale for every essential decision --- this is a requirement,
        unlike its neighbours (\rref{AD-062}).
  \item Record rejected alternatives and the reasoning
        (\rref{AD-055}), and evidence that alternatives were
        genuinely compared (\rref{AD-063}).
  \item Capture relations among decisions, where useful, as correspondences
        (\rref{AD-059}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Stage 8 --- Close the gaps honestly}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Walk the three coverage chains and list every unclosed link.
  \item For identified stakeholders and considerations that were not addressed,
        state the limitation or constraint that prevented it
        (\rref{AD-009}) and give a rationale
        (\rref{AD-064}).
  \item Identify and explain any non-conformances with rationales
        (\rref{AD-010}).
  \item Take a documented position on each interpretation issue
        (\rref{IX-001}--\rref{IX-009}).
\end{itemize}

\risknote{A recorded gap is still a gap. \rref{AD-009} and
\rref{AD-010} make the position auditable; they do not satisfy
the coverage requirements they explain. Separate genuinely inapplicable
conditions --- a conditional rule whose condition is unmet --- from requirements
that are simply not met. The first is not a finding; the second is, however well
documented.}

\section{Stage 9 --- Review and claim}

Run the checklist in Chapter~\ref{ch:checklist}. If a conformance claim is to be
made, state which of the five claims it is
(\rref{CF-002}--\rref{CF-006}), and confirm that no
part of it relies on tailoring (\rref{CF-007}).

\chapter{Review checklist}
\label{ch:checklist}

The checklist restates every catalogued rule as a review line, grouped as the
catalogue is grouped. The \textbf{Force} column governs how a negative answer is
treated.

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}l L{110mm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Force} & \textbf{Treatment of a negative finding} \\
\midrule
\textit{must}     & A finding. The description, framework, language, viewpoint or model kind does not conform. \\
\textit{must not} & A finding. \\
\textit{should}   & A discussion point. Record the decision not to follow it; it is not a conformance failure. \\
\textit{may}      & Not reviewable. The line is present so that a permission is not mistaken for an obligation. \\
\textit{---}      & Explanatory. Present to guard against misreading; nothing to verify. \\
\textit{?}        & Unresolved. Confirm the project has taken a documented position. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\risknote{Before recording any line as failed, confirm that the rule's condition
actually holds. A conditional requirement whose condition is unmet is
\emph{inapplicable}, not \emph{failed} --- a component with a governing model
kind needs no legend, and a description with no prior evaluations has no
recommendations to consider. Record inapplicable lines as such, with the reason.
Equally, do not record a line as passed because the gap was documented:
disclosure under \rref{AD-009} or
\rref{AD-010} leaves the underlying obligation unmet.}

\chapter{Completeness report}

\section{Method}

Extraction proceeded in three passes. The first inventoried the whole source ---
definitions, applicability and conformance provisions, specification clauses,
lists, tables, figures and their accompanying explanations, notes, examples and
annexes. The second extracted and classified individual provisions, splitting
compound statements into independently reviewable rules while preserving each
governing condition, and consolidating genuine duplicates. The third reconciled
the catalogue against the inventory from the first pass, section by section.

Figures were inspected directly and used to confirm relationship cardinalities,
which are recorded in Chapter~4. Internal cross-references were followed to
recover dependent obligations. Publications referenced by the source were
\emph{not} used as a source of obligations; where the source mentions an
external publication, only the fact of the reference was carried, never its
content.

\section{Extraction gaps}

No section of the source was unreadable or unreviewed. The complete text was
available and inspected, including all figures and all annexes.

Two limitations on the catalogue's scope should be recorded explicitly.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Informative annex material was mined for guidance, not for
        obligations.} The annexes supply a viewpoint documentation template,
        commentary on terms and concepts, relationships to other publications,
        uses of descriptions, life-cycle discussion, and framework examples.
        Guidance drawn from this material appears in the \textbf{Meaning} and
        \textbf{Evidence} fields of the relevant rules and in Part~III. No rule
        in the catalogue rests on annex material alone, with the deliberate
        exception of the interpretation issues, which exist precisely to flag
        normative-looking wording found outside the operative clauses.
  \item \textbf{Obligations from referenced publications were not imported.}
        The source names other publications for vocabulary, for architecting
        processes, for information item content, for evaluation frameworks and
        as examples of frameworks and languages. None of their requirements were
        carried into this catalogue. Where your organisation is also bound by
        one of those publications, its obligations are additional to this
        catalogue and must be tracked separately.
\end{enumerate}
