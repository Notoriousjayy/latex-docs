\section{Catalog semantics}
These names track the book's 38 architecture entries in Chapters 13--15. Each card adds an original engineering interpretation, a set of related mechanisms for design exploration and a test suggestion. Mechanism relationships in this workbench are proposed examples, not an assertion that every combination exactly matches a diagram in the source.
\begin{takeaway}
Select by requirement, not architectural fashion. Every selected arrangement needs a deployment context, mechanism dependencies, ownership boundaries and measurable acceptance evidence.
\end{takeaway}

\section{Architecture selection decision table}
\small\begin{longtable}{p{0.26\linewidth}p{0.36\linewidth}p{0.29\linewidth}}
\toprule Requirement or constraint & Start with & Confirm before approval \\\midrule
Uneven request volume & Workload Distribution; Dynamic Scalability & Health signals, state and capacity limit \\
Host failure continuity & Virtual Server Clustering; Zero Downtime & Shared failure domain and actual outage time \\
Loss of data center & Resilient Disaster Recovery & Location separation, restore sequence and data loss \\
Cross-jurisdiction data & Distributed Data Sovereignty & Data, backup and metadata location \\
Storage cost/performance & Storage Workload Management; tiering & Measured workload and movement cost \\
Intermittent field connectivity & Edge Computing; Fog Computing & Local operation and synchronization failure \\
Cloud platform diversity & Multicloud; Metacloud; Federation & Identity, policy, cost and exit boundaries \\\bottomrule
\end{longtable}\normalsize

\begin{workbox}[title={Pattern composition challenge}]
Design a critical API for seasonal demand and regulated data. Choose two fundamental architectures, one advanced architecture and no more than one specialized architecture. Explain every inclusion with a measurable requirement and every exclusion with an avoided cost or risk.
\end{workbox}
