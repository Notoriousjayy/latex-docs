\section{Learning model and mastery criteria}
The curriculum follows dependency logic: vocabulary before mechanisms, mechanisms before architectures, architectures before procurement and service commitments. Chapters 1--2 are orientation; foundation mastery precedes design exercises. The numbered weeks, labs, hours and rubrics are a new study plan, not part of the book.
\textbf{Suggested effort:} 5--7 hours per week plus a 2-hour final review. Suggested audience: software, platform, security and enterprise architects.

\section{Weekly modules}
\Needspace{8\baselineskip}\subsection{Week 1: Scope, cases and value proposition}
\textbf{Reading:} Chapter(s) 1, 2, 3.\\
\textbf{Prerequisite gate:} No prior cloud assumptions.\\
\textbf{Practical lab:} System context and business-goals traceability.\\
\textbf{Completion test:} Explain a cloud service, its consumer/provider boundary, and two evidence-backed success measures.
\begin{itemize}
\item Submit a one-page rationale (inputs, decision, alternatives).
\item Include a diagram or structured matrix with named boundaries and owners.
\item Attach positive and negative evidence or explain what cannot be tested in a lab.
\end{itemize}

\section{Capstone assessment and grading}
\small\begin{longtable}{p{0.37\linewidth}p{0.15\linewidth}p{0.36\linewidth}}
\toprule Evaluation dimension & Weight & Acceptable evidence \\\midrule
Requirements, service model and ownership & 20\% & Context, actors and responsibility matrix \\
Mechanisms and architecture coherence & 25\% & Traceable composition and alternatives \\
Security and trust boundaries & 20\% & Threat-control-test map, negative cases \\
Continuity and SLA validation & 20\% & Fault injection and measured objectives \\
Commercial and operational feasibility & 15\% & Metering forecast, cost risk and escalation \\\bottomrule
\end{longtable}\normalsize
\textbf{Pass criterion:} 80 points out of 100 with no unresolved critical trust-boundary or data-recovery defect. This is a suggested study benchmark, not an accreditation rule.
