\textbf{What distinguishes up-front and ongoing costs?} & Adoption includes migration, integration and initial setup as well as recurring infrastructure consumption, operations and support.\\[3pt]\textcolor{petrol}{\emph{Practice:} Create an ownership-based cost inventory.}\\[8pt]\midrule
\textbf{Which usage metrics affect charges?} & Network transfer, server allocation, storage capacity, I/O and subscriptions or user counts can affect bills independently.\\[3pt]\textcolor{petrol}{\emph{Practice:} Model costs using three resource dimensions.}\\[8pt]\midrule
\textbf{What can reserved allocation change?} & Commitments can lower unit cost when utilization is stable, but expose a mismatch risk when demand shifts.\\[3pt]\textcolor{petrol}{\emph{Practice:} Compare two consumption scenarios with commitment.}\\[8pt]\midrule
\textbf{Why is egress important?} & Outbound and cross-region or cross-environment traffic can materially change a design's total expense.\\[3pt]\textcolor{petrol}{\emph{Practice:} Add a data-transfer budget to a migration estimate.}\\[8pt]\midrule
\textbf{What should multicloud cost governance include?} & Tagging, unit normalization, budgeting, exception review and shared attribution help avoid unowned costs.\\[3pt]\textcolor{petrol}{\emph{Practice:} Build a cost allocation dictionary.}\\[8pt]\midrule
\textbf{What is a defensible forecast?} & Document usage assumptions, unit rates, one-time cost, growth sensitivity and review period; compare against observed invoices.\\[3pt]\textcolor{petrol}{\emph{Practice:} Calculate best, expected and stress scenarios.}\\[8pt]\midrule

\section{Synthesis and architecture connections}
Connect architecture selection to operating commitments. Attribute each obligation to a consumer or provider, describe its measurement source and produce traceable evidence for risk, cost and service quality.
\begin{workbox}[title={Applied deliverable}]
Construct a defensible cost estimate and cost-governance plan.\\[4pt]Create a one-page architecture or operations artifact with: (1) assumptions, (2) a decision and alternatives, (3) control ownership, (4) observable acceptance criteria, and (5) a failed-case test.
\end{workbox}
