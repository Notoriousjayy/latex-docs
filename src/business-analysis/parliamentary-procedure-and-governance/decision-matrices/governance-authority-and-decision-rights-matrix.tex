\section{Authority boundaries}
Use this original organizational-control matrix with the actual adopted instruments. “Decision owner” identifies the usual controlling actor, not a grant of authority. Legal advice may be needed to resolve charter or statute conflicts.

\small\begin{longtable}{@{}p{.19\linewidth}p{.20\linewidth}p{.26\linewidth}p{.27\linewidth}@{}}\toprule Instrument & Normal decision authority & Evidence & Constraint\\\midrule\endhead
Law and charter & External legal authority & Charter or counsel review & Cannot be set aside by an ordinary vote\\
Bylaws & Organization membership & Bylaws text and amendment record & Special notice and thresholds may apply\\
Special rules of order & Assembly under applicable rules & Rule adoption and amendment proof & Controls procedure within higher authority\\
Parliamentary authority & Assembly through adoption & Citation and adoption clause & Applies where not superseded\\
Standing rules & Assembly or properly delegated body & Adopted administrative rule & Do not use to override bylaws\\
Custom & Practice only where rules silent & Historical examples and consistency check & Contrary valid written rule prevails\\
Board decisions & Board within granted authority & Delegation clause and board minutes & No implied authority above membership\\
Committee recommendations & Appointed committee & Charge, report and assembly action & Not binding absent grant of power\\
\bottomrule\end{longtable}\normalsize
\section{Escalation}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=4pt]
\item Locate source text and version.
\item State conflict and affected members.
\item Check legal hierarchy and delegated authority.
\item Obtain qualified interpretation if uncertain.
\item Document final basis in the meeting record.
\end{enumerate}
