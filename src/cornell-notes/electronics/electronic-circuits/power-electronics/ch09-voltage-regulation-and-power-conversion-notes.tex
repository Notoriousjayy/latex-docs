\begin{CornellOverviewBox}{Concept}
Power systems convert an uncertain source into controlled rails and currents while managing heat, ripple, efficiency, isolation, transients, faults, EMI, and stored energy. The chapter spans linear regulators, rectified supplies, switching topologies, offline converters, references, modules, batteries, capacitors, protection, and current limiting.
\end{CornellOverviewBox}
\begin{CornellOverviewBox}{Concept}
\textbf{Prerequisites.} Diodes, capacitors/inductors, feedback, transistor/MOSFET switching, thermal resistance, filters, and safe measurement practice.

\textbf{After studying this chapter, you should be able to:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Budget input range, load transients, dropout, dissipation, efficiency, ripple, and stability for linear regulation.
  \item Explain buck, boost, inverting, flyback, forward, and bridge energy flow.
  \item Select references, modules, batteries, and capacitors from accuracy, noise, energy, lifetime, and fault needs.
  \item Design current limiting, overvoltage protection, startup, layout, and thermal paths.
\end{itemize}\textbf{Central design questions.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item What are the source/load extremes and every startup, shutdown, short, surge, and reverse-energy path?
  \item Is efficiency, noise, isolation, size, cost, or transient response the dominant architectural constraint?
  \item Where are switching current loops and how are heat and stored energy safely controlled?
\end{itemize}\end{CornellOverviewBox}
\section{Cornell Notes}
\begin{CornellNotesTable}
\CornellNoteRow{9.1--9.2: Feedback regulation}{A reference, error amplifier, pass element, and feedback divider form a regulator. Output accuracy depends on reference, divider, amplifier offset, loop gain, load/line regulation, and thermal drift. The pass device needs headroom and SOA. Frequency compensation must remain stable across output capacitance, ESR, load, and input conditions.}
\CornellNoteRow{9.3: Integrated linear regulators}{Fixed, adjustable, low-dropout, and current-reference ICs differ in ground current, minimum load, dropout mechanism, output-capacitor requirements, reverse-current tolerance, noise, PSRR, enable behavior, and protection. LDO stability may require a capacitance/ESR range. `Low dropout' does not imply low quiescent current, low noise, or safe reverse bias.}
\CornellNoteRow{9.4: Heat and power}{Linear loss is approximately $(V_{in}-V_{out})I_{out}$ plus control current. Convert power to junction temperature through the complete thermal network and derate at worst ambient. Transistor SOA can be more restrictive than wattage. Heatsink interface, copper area, airflow, enclosure, neighboring sources, and transient duration all matter.}
\CornellNoteRow{9.5: AC to unregulated DC}{Line input stages require fuse, switch, surge/inrush limiting, EMI filtering, safety-rated capacitors, transformer/isolation or approved offline design, rectification, reservoir capacitance, and discharge. Reservoir ripple is roughly $\Delta V\approx I/(f_{ripple}C)$ but charging pulses, transformer regulation, diode drops, and line tolerance determine actual minimum input and component stress.}
\CornellNoteRow{9.6: Switching topologies}{Buck transfers energy to reduce voltage; boost raises it; inverting reverses polarity. Inductor volt-second balance and capacitor charge balance establish ideal conversion. Duty cycle, ripple, discontinuous/continuous mode, switch/diode losses, dead time, current limit, and control mode modify it. Flyback stores energy in coupled inductance; forward/bridge topologies transfer energy while on and need magnetics reset.}
\CornellNoteRow{9.7--9.8: Offline switchers}{Offline converters rectify hazardous mains to a high-voltage bus and use high-frequency isolation, control, feedback, and EMI networks. Creepage/clearance, insulation system, Y/X capacitors, common-mode current, surge, inrush, leakage, transformer construction, optocoupler aging, and safety standards are design inputs. Reference designs are starting points only when layout, parts, magnetics, and conditions match.}
\CornellNoteRow{9.9: Inverters and class-D}{Switching bridges synthesize ac or modulated power efficiently. Dead time prevents shoot-through but causes distortion; load current direction determines diode or synchronous-device conduction. Gate drive, bootstrap refresh, common-mode voltage, output filtering, and regenerative energy require analysis. Never assume the load only consumes power.}
\CornellNoteRow{9.10: Voltage references}{Zener, bandgap, JFET, floating-gate, shunt, and series references trade voltage, current, tempco, initial accuracy, drift, noise, dynamic impedance, headroom, warm-up, and long-term stability. Filter reference noise without violating startup or load requirements. A reference is not automatically a load-driving regulator; buffer it while preserving accuracy and stability.}
\CornellNoteRow{9.11: Power modules}{Modules reduce design risk but still require input/output capacitors, layout, thermal derating, EMI filtering, enable sequencing, isolation verification, and fault analysis. Efficiency curves are condition-specific. Verify minimum load, remote sense, trim limits, parallelability, output discharge, certification scope, and availability.}
\CornellNoteRow{9.12: Batteries and capacitors}{Batteries are electrochemical sources with voltage, capacity, rate, temperature, aging, protection, charging, and safety constraints. Energy is not simply nominal amp-hours times nominal voltage under every load. Capacitors provide high power and cycle life but energy scales as $CV^2/2$ and usable voltage falls during discharge. ESR sets pulse drop and heating.}
\CornellNoteRow{9.13: Protection and extensions}{Crowbars intentionally short a rail after overvoltage and therefore require an upstream fuse/current limit that clears safely. Foldback reduces short-circuit dissipation but can prevent startup into capacitive or constant-current loads. External pass elements extend current/voltage but complicate sharing, drive, stability, and SOA. Sequence and protect reverse/backfeed paths.}
\end{CornellNotesTable}
\begin{CornellSummaryBox}{Summary}
A power supply is an energy-management and protection system wrapped around a feedback loop. Architecture follows efficiency, noise, isolation, transient, size, and safety needs. Verify worst-case input/output, losses, magnetics, loop stability, thermal path, EMI, startup/shutdown, stored energy, and faults. Nominal voltage regulation alone is not evidence of a robust supply.
\end{CornellSummaryBox}
\section{Worked Examples}
\begin{CornellExamBox}{Linear-regulator thermal check}
A $12\,\mathrm{V}$ input produces $5\,\mathrm{V}$ at $0.45\,\mathrm{A}$. Pass loss is $(12-5)(0.45)=3.15\,\mathrm{W}$. With $\theta_{JA}=28^{\circ}\mathrm{C/W}$ and $T_A=40^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$, the simple estimate gives $T_J=128^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$. This has little margin; use a better thermal path, lower input, or switcher, and verify SOA.
\end{CornellExamBox}
\begin{CornellExamBox}{Buck duty and current ripple}
An ideal buck converts $18\,\mathrm{V}$ to $6\,\mathrm{V}$, so $D\approx1/3$. With $L=47\,\mu\mathrm{H}$ and $f_s=250\,\mathrm{kHz}$, on-time ripple is $\Delta I=(18-6)D/(Lf_s)=0.340\,\mathrm{A}$. Use peak current for saturation/current-limit checks and include losses in actual duty.
\end{CornellExamBox}
\begin{CornellExamBox}{Reservoir capacitor}
A full-wave $60\,\mathrm{Hz}$ rectifier supplies $0.30\,\mathrm{A}$ with allowed $1.5\,\mathrm{V}$ droop. $C\approx I/(120\Delta V)=1.67\,\mathrm{mF}$. Choose at least $2200\,\mu\mathrm{F}$ after tolerance/aging, then verify ripple current, diode surge, transformer heating, and regulator minimum input.
\end{CornellExamBox}
\section{Design and Troubleshooting}
\begin{CornellExamBox}{Design and Troubleshooting}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Specify source range/transients, every rail/load profile, noise, efficiency, isolation, sequencing, environment, and faults.
  \item Choose architecture and topology; calculate duty/headroom, ripple, peak/RMS currents, losses, energy, thermal path, and control range.
  \item Select rated magnetics, switches, rectifiers, capacitors, reference/control, protection, and safety components; design layout by current loops.
  \item Bring up with isolation/current limiting and safe loads; test line/load corners, startup, transient, short, thermal, efficiency, ripple, EMI, and shutdown.
\end{enumerate}\end{CornellExamBox}
\begin{CornellExamBox}{Symptoms, likely causes, and checks}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Low output under load: dropout, current limit, excessive resistance, inductor saturation, or source sag.
  \item Ripple/oscillation: wrong output capacitor/ESR, poor compensation/layout, burst mode, grounding, or insufficient input bypass.
  \item Switch overheating: conduction/switching loss, gate drive, diode recovery, shoot-through, or magnetic saturation.
  \item EMI failure: large hot-loop area, fast edges, transformer capacitance, poor filter placement, or shield/ground path.
  \item Fails at startup only: inrush, UVLO chatter, foldback, soft-start/load interaction, bootstrap starvation, or prebiased output.
\end{itemize}\end{CornellExamBox}
\begin{CornellWarningBox}{Safety and Limitations}
Offline and high-energy power conversion is hazardous. Use approved isolation, fusing, creepage/clearance, rated components, discharge paths, insulated enclosures, and safe differential probes. Never defeat protective earth or rely on a scope ground at a floating switching node. Batteries require chemistry-specific charging and protection against short circuit, reverse connection, and thermal runaway.
\end{CornellWarningBox}
\section{Key-Equation Sheet}
\begin{CornellExamBox}{Key Equations}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Linear loss: $P\approx(V_{in}-V_{out})I_{out}$; $T_J\approx T_A+P\theta_{JA}$ within the thermal model.
  \item Reservoir droop: $\Delta V\approx I/(f_{ripple}C)$.
  \item Ideal CCM buck: $V_o=DV_{in}$; boost: $V_o=V_{in}/(1-D)$.
  \item Inductor ripple: $\Delta I=(V_L/L)\Delta t$; capacitor ripple follows $\Delta V=(1/C)\int i_Cdt$ plus ESR step.
  \item Efficiency: $\eta=P_{out}/P_{in}$; loss $=P_{in}-P_{out}$ must map to physical components.
  \item Stored energy: $E_L=LI^2/2$, $E_C=CV^2/2$.
\end{itemize}\end{CornellExamBox}
\section{Study and Review}
\subsection{Design checklist}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Input/output normal, transient, and fault ranges are complete.
  \item Dropout/duty, peak/RMS stress, SOA, saturation, and thermal margins pass.
  \item Loop stability and capacitor/ESR requirements pass all modes.
  \item Hot loops, gate loops, sense/reference returns, and EMI filters are laid out deliberately.
  \item Isolation, fuse/protection coordination, discharge, sequencing, and reverse paths are verified.
\end{itemize}\subsection{Common misconceptions}
\begin{itemize}
  \item A regulator's current rating does not guarantee acceptable temperature or SOA.
  \item An LDO is not necessarily quiet, efficient, or stable with any capacitor.
  \item Ideal duty-cycle equations do not predict losses or current stress.
  \item A module does not eliminate system-level EMI, thermal, or safety work.
  \item A crowbar is unsafe without a coordinated upstream clearing device.
\end{itemize}\subsection{Active-recall questions}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item What sets linear-regulator dropout and heat?
  \item Why can LDO output ESR matter?
  \item State inductor volt-second balance.
  \item How do buck and boost energy flows differ?
  \item What distinguishes flyback from forward conversion?
  \item Which loops dominate switching EMI?
  \item Why is SOA separate from power rating?
  \item What parameters define a voltage reference?
  \item How does capacitor ESR affect pulses?
  \item Why can foldback block startup?
\end{enumerate}\subsection{Original practice problems}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A $9\,\mathrm{V}$ to $3.3\,\mathrm{V}$ linear regulator supplies $120\,\mathrm{mA}$. Find loss and ideal efficiency ignoring ground current.
  \item An ideal boost converts $5\,\mathrm{V}$ to $12\,\mathrm{V}$. Find duty.
  \item A $470\,\mu\mathrm{F}$ capacitor at $16\,\mathrm{V}$ is discharged to zero. Find stored energy.
\end{enumerate}\subsection{Answer key / solution outline}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Loss $=0.684\,\mathrm{W}$; efficiency $\approx3.3/9=36.7\%$.
  \item $D=1-5/12=0.583$; real duty is higher and stress/losses must be checked.
  \item $E=CV^2/2=0.0602\,\mathrm{J}$; actual delivery is reduced by ESR and voltage limits.
\end{enumerate}\begin{CornellSummaryBox}{Summary}
Design the full energy path and every abnormal state. Regulation, efficiency, heat, stability, magnetics, EMI, isolation, sequencing, and protection are inseparable; validate them at source/load/temperature extremes with safe instrumentation.
\end{CornellSummaryBox}
