\begin{CornellOverviewBox}{Concept}
Digital logic represents information with bounded voltage ranges and timing relationships. The chapter develops codes, gates, combinational reduction, flip-flops, counters, registers, practical circuits, low-power CMOS, and logic pathologies. Correct Boolean function is necessary but insufficient: noise margin, drive, timing, synchronization, reset, power integrity, and unused inputs determine real behavior.
\end{CornellOverviewBox}
\begin{CornellOverviewBox}{Concept}
\textbf{Prerequisites.} Voltage/current, RC timing, transistor switching, logic-level concepts, and basic binary arithmetic.

\textbf{After studying this chapter, you should be able to:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Interpret truth tables, Boolean expressions, number codes, and assertion-level notation.
  \item Minimize combinational logic and identify hazards or invalid input combinations.
  \item Analyze synchronous sequential logic, setup/hold, metastability, counters, and registers.
  \item Design robust low-power logic with valid levels, edges, decoupling, reset, and loading.
\end{itemize}\textbf{Central design questions.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Are voltage thresholds, noise margins, drive currents, and rise/fall times compatible?
  \item Which signals are asynchronous, and how are metastability and reset handled?
  \item What happens during power-up, illegal states, simultaneous switching, and slow/noisy inputs?
\end{itemize}\end{CornellOverviewBox}
\section{Cornell Notes}
\begin{CornellNotesTable}
\CornellNoteRow{10.1: Logic concepts}{Digital states are ranges separated by undefined regions. A bit has physical level and logical assertion; active-low notation prevents polarity confusion. Binary, hexadecimal, unsigned, two's-complement, and coded formats require explicit width and interpretation. Truth tables define functions; gates implement them. Propagation delay and finite edges mean real outputs are not instantaneous Boolean values.}
\CornellNoteRow{10.2: CMOS and bipolar families}{Logic families specify $V_{IL}$, $V_{IH}$, $V_{OL}$, $V_{OH}$, input leakage/current, output drive, capacitance, delay, supply, and ESD/absolute limits. CMOS static power is low but switching power and shoot-through rise with frequency and slow edges. TTL-style inputs have different current behavior. Three-state outputs share buses only with guaranteed ownership; open-drain/collector outputs require pull-ups.}
\CornellNoteRow{10.3: Combinational logic}{Boolean identities and Karnaugh maps reduce gate count, but minimization can introduce static or dynamic hazards when inputs do not change simultaneously. Multiplexers, decoders, encoders, adders, and comparators package common functions. Define invalid inputs and output defaults. Wide fanout and capacitive wiring slow edges and increase dynamic current.}
\CornellNoteRow{10.4: Sequential logic}{Latches are level-sensitive; edge-triggered flip-flops sample on a clock edge. Setup and hold windows define legal timing. Violating them can cause metastability whose probability falls with resolution time but never becomes mathematically zero. Use synchronizer chains for single-bit asynchronous levels and protocol/FIFO methods for multi-bit data. Analyze state transitions, reset, and clock enables synchronously where possible.}
\CornellNoteRow{10.5: Registers and counters}{Registers store words; shift registers serialize, delay, or expand I/O; counters divide frequency and sequence states. Ripple counters accumulate delay and can glitch when decoded; synchronous counters update together. Modulo-$N$ counters need a defined reset/terminal-count strategy. Programmable logic consolidates functions but still obeys electrical/timing constraints.}
\CornellNoteRow{10.6: Complete digital circuits}{Timing generators, multiplexed displays, and pulse sequencers combine counters, decoding, latching, and drive. Avoid combinational decoding glitches at external loads; register outputs or design hazard-free logic. Multiplexed displays trade peak current, duty cycle, brightness, refresh rate, and EMI. Budget simultaneous-switching current and provide local bypassing.}
\CornellNoteRow{10.7: Micropower design}{CMOS dynamic power is approximately $\alpha C_LV^2f$. Lower voltage and capacitance are powerful, while slow or floating inputs can increase current. Gate unused clocks, choose static pull states, limit pull-up dc current, and avoid excessive level-shifter loss. Leakage becomes dominant at low frequency and high temperature. A low-power state must define every input and peripheral rail.}
\CornellNoteRow{10.8: Logic pathology}{Dc failures include incompatible thresholds, excess fanout, floating inputs, back-powering, and ground offset. Switching failures include bounce, ringing, crosstalk, poor termination, clock skew, metastability, hazards, and supply droop. Fast logic should be treated as high frequency regardless of toggle rate because edge time sets interconnect behavior. Probe with short ground connections and preserve signal return paths.}
\end{CornellNotesTable}
\begin{CornellSummaryBox}{Summary}
Digital correctness combines Boolean logic, sampled-time behavior, and analog electrical integrity. Specify codes and polarity, validate level/drive compatibility, synchronize asynchronous signals, design deterministic reset and illegal-state recovery, and preserve power/return paths for fast edges. Low average toggle rate does not make a fast-edge circuit electrically slow.
\end{CornellSummaryBox}
\section{Worked Examples}
\begin{CornellExamBox}{Noise margins}
A driver guarantees $V_{OH}\ge2.7\,\mathrm{V}$ and $V_{OL}\le0.4\,\mathrm{V}$. The receiver requires $V_{IH}\ge2.0\,\mathrm{V}$ and $V_{IL}\le0.8\,\mathrm{V}$. High margin is $0.7\,\mathrm{V}$ and low margin is $0.4\,\mathrm{V}$ before ground shift, crosstalk, and load-induced degradation.
\end{CornellExamBox}
\begin{CornellExamBox}{CMOS dynamic power}
A node with $C_L=35\,\mathrm{pF}$ toggles with activity $\alpha=0.25$ at $20\,\mathrm{MHz}$ from $3.3\,\mathrm{V}$. $P\approx\alpha C_LV^2f=1.91\,\mathrm{mW}$. Total device power includes internal capacitance, short-circuit current, leakage, and other nodes.
\end{CornellExamBox}
\begin{CornellExamBox}{Modulo counter width}
A $10\,\mathrm{MHz}$ clock must generate an event every $75\,\mu\mathrm{s}$, so $N=750$. Since $2^9=512<750\le1024=2^{10}$, at least 10 counter bits are needed. Decode or compare 749 and reset/update synchronously to avoid output glitches.
\end{CornellExamBox}
\section{Design and Troubleshooting}
\begin{CornellExamBox}{Design and Troubleshooting}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Define function, code widths, active levels, clock domains, reset state, latency, and illegal-state behavior.
  \item Verify electrical compatibility and load; develop combinational equations and synchronous state/timing models.
  \item Analyze setup/hold, clock skew, CDC, propagation, fanout, edges, power, and interconnect return paths.
  \item Simulate logic and timing; prototype with local decoupling and measure levels, edges, clocks, reset, metastability exposure, and corner states.
\end{enumerate}\end{CornellExamBox}
\begin{CornellExamBox}{Symptoms, likely causes, and checks}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Random state: floating input, reset issue, clock integrity, metastability, or supply droop.
  \item Correct slowly but fails fast: propagation/setup violation, fanout/capacitance, crosstalk, or termination.
  \item Glitch on decoded output: unequal combinational paths or ripple-counter transitions.
  \item High current: contention, slow midrail input, excessive switching, back-powering, or damaged ESD diode.
  \item Only probe changes behavior: probe capacitance/ground inductance, weak pull-up, or marginal timing.
\end{itemize}\end{CornellExamBox}
\begin{CornellWarningBox}{Safety and Limitations}
Digital outputs can be damaged by contention, overvoltage, back-powering, or driving high-current loads directly. Limit external load current, sequence mixed-voltage rails safely, and use appropriate transistors/drivers for inductive or high-energy loads. Do not connect earth-referenced instruments to hazardous or floating digital systems without an approved measurement method.
\end{CornellWarningBox}
\section{Key-Equation Sheet}
\begin{CornellExamBox}{Key Equations}
\begin{itemize}
  \item High noise margin: $NM_H=V_{OH(min)}-V_{IH(min)}$; low: $NM_L=V_{IL(max)}-V_{OL(max)}$.
  \item CMOS dynamic estimate: $P\approx\alpha C_LV^2f$.
  \item Counter division: $f_{out}=f_{clk}/N$ for a defined modulo-$N$ event.
  \item Minimum counter bits: $n=\lceil\log_2N\rceil$.
  \item Timing constraint conceptually requires data-path maximum delay plus setup to fit the clock period after clock uncertainty/skew.
  \item Open-drain rise is approximately an RC transition; choose pull-up from sink current and required rise time.
\end{itemize}\end{CornellExamBox}
\section{Study and Review}
\subsection{Design checklist}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Logic polarity, encoding, widths, and invalid states are documented.
  \item Input/output levels, drive, fanout, and rise/fall times are compatible.
  \item Setup/hold, CDC, reset, and metastability strategy are reviewed.
  \item Decoupling, return paths, termination, connector ground pins, and probing are planned.
  \item Power-up, power-down, contention, back-power, and unused inputs are safe.
\end{itemize}\subsection{Common misconceptions}
\begin{itemize}
  \item A logic 1 is a voltage range, not one exact voltage.
  \item A two-flop synchronizer reduces but does not eliminate metastability probability.
  \item A low clock frequency can still have transmission-line problems when edges are fast.
  \item Combinationally equivalent expressions can differ in hazards and delay.
  \item Unused CMOS inputs must not float.
\end{itemize}\subsection{Active-recall questions}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Define high and low noise margins.
  \item How do open-drain and three-state outputs differ?
  \item What are setup and hold times?
  \item Why can metastability not be eliminated?
  \item How should multi-bit CDC differ from one-bit synchronization?
  \item Why can ripple-counter decoding glitch?
  \item What sets CMOS dynamic power?
  \item Why do slow CMOS inputs raise current?
  \item What does active-low notation clarify?
  \item Which property determines whether a wire is a transmission line?
\end{enumerate}\subsection{Original practice problems}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A receiver needs $V_{IH}=0.7V_{DD}$ at $V_{DD}=3.0\,\mathrm{V}$. Find threshold.
  \item A counter divides $24\,\mathrm{MHz}$ by 2400. Find event frequency and period.
  \item A $4.7\,\mathrm{k\Omega}$ pull-up drives $80\,\mathrm{pF}$. Estimate $10$--$90\%$ rise time.
\end{enumerate}\subsection{Answer key / solution outline}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $2.1\,\mathrm{V}$; compare with the driver's guaranteed $V_{OH}$ under load.
  \item $10\,\mathrm{kHz}$ and $100\,\mu\mathrm{s}$.
  \item Approximately $2.2RC=0.827\,\mu\mathrm{s}$; verify against protocol timing and receiver thresholds.
\end{enumerate}\begin{CornellSummaryBox}{Summary}
Digital systems are analog during transitions and probabilistic at asynchronous boundaries. Make levels, timing, reset, CDC, drive, power integrity, and interconnect behavior explicit alongside the Boolean function.
\end{CornellSummaryBox}
