\begin{CornellOverviewBox}{Concept}
Field-effect transistors (FETs) control channel current with an electric field and offer very high dc input resistance. The chapter spans JFET and MOSFET linear stages, analog switches, logic-level switching, and high-power operation. Good designs distinguish threshold from usable gate drive, dc gate leakage from dynamic gate charge, and ideal channel resistance from capacitance, body-diode, thermal, and safe-operating-area limits.
\end{CornellOverviewBox}
\begin{CornellOverviewBox}{Concept}
\textbf{Prerequisites.} Resistive bias networks, small-signal gain, RC behavior, BJT building blocks, feedback, and power/thermal calculations.

\textbf{After studying this chapter, you should be able to:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Distinguish JFET, enhancement MOSFET, and depletion MOSFET behavior.
  \item Bias and analyze common-source, source-follower, current-source, and differential stages.
  \item Evaluate analog-switch signal range, on-resistance, leakage, charge injection, and bandwidth.
  \item Select and drive power MOSFETs using gate-charge, loss, thermal, and transient criteria.
\end{itemize}\textbf{Central design questions.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item What gate-source voltage is guaranteed at the required drain current and temperature?
  \item Is the FET being used as a transconductance device, a resistor, or a switch?
  \item Which parasitic -- leakage, capacitance, body diode, or heat -- sets the real limit?
\end{itemize}\end{CornellOverviewBox}
\section{Cornell Notes}
\begin{CornellNotesTable}
\CornellNoteRow{3.1: FET families}{JFETs are normally on and use a reverse-biased junction gate; enhancement MOSFETs are normally off; depletion MOSFETs can conduct at zero gate bias. Gate voltage controls drain current, but characteristic curves vary widely. $V_{GS(th)}$ identifies a small test current and is not the recommended on-state drive. Respect maximum $V_{GS}$ and ESD sensitivity.}
\CornellNoteRow{3.2: Linear circuits}{Common-source stages provide inversion and voltage gain; source followers buffer with gain below unity; source resistors create degeneration; current sources require drain-voltage compliance. JFETs are attractive where low current noise and high input impedance matter. Differential stages share a tail current. A FET used as a voltage-controlled resistor remains approximately resistive only for small drain-source voltage and a permitted signal range.}
\CornellNoteRow{3.3: JFET detail}{The transfer curve is often estimated by $I_D=I_{DSS}(1-V_{GS}/V_P)^2$, but $I_{DSS}$ and $V_P$ have broad spreads. Small-signal $g_m=dI_D/dV_{GS}$ depends on bias. Finite output conductance, drain-voltage modulation, gate leakage, and capacitances set gain, accuracy, and bandwidth. Self-bias improves robustness but does not eliminate device spread.}
\CornellNoteRow{3.4: Analog and logic switches}{An analog switch must keep the channel on over the full signal and supply range. Evaluate $R_{\mathrm{on}}$, flatness, leakage, off isolation, crosstalk, bandwidth, charge injection, and break-before-make timing. Logic MOSFET switching requires a valid gate-source voltage referenced to the source. High-side n-channel devices often need a gate voltage above the supply or a dedicated driver.}
\CornellNoteRow{3.5: Power MOSFETs}{Conduction loss is approximately $I_{\mathrm{rms}}^2R_{DS(on)}$ at the actual junction temperature. Switching loss depends on voltage-current overlap, frequency, gate charge, and driver impedance. The gate is capacitive dynamically even though dc current is tiny. Body-diode reverse recovery, common-source inductance, Miller plateau, avalanche energy, and transient SOA can dominate fast or inductive applications.}
\CornellNoteRow{3.6: Linear MOSFET operation}{A MOSFET rated for low switching loss may be unsuitable in its linear region because current crowds into hot cells. Check dc and pulse SOA rather than relying on total power alone. Paralleled MOSFETs generally share steady current better than BJTs because $R_{DS(on)}$ rises with temperature, but unequal gate paths, threshold at light current, layout inductance, and transient switching can still cause imbalance.}
\end{CornellNotesTable}
\begin{CornellSummaryBox}{Summary}
FETs combine voltage control and high input impedance with significant device spread and capacitance. Linear design chooses a bias point and uses degeneration or feedback; switching design treats the gate as a charge-transfer problem and evaluates channel loss, transition loss, diode behavior, layout, and heat. Threshold voltage is not an on-state specification, and a switching-optimized MOSFET is not automatically safe as a linear pass element.
\end{CornellSummaryBox}
\section{Worked Examples}
\begin{CornellExamBox}{Gate-driver current}
A power MOSFET has total gate charge $Q_g=38\,\mathrm{nC}$ at the intended operating point. To traverse the main transition in $80\,\mathrm{ns}$, the driver needs average current $I_g=Q_g/t=0.475\,\mathrm{A}$. This is a transient demand, not dc gate current. Add margin for pull-down current, driver resistance, Miller behavior, and package/layout inductance.
\end{CornellExamBox}
\begin{CornellExamBox}{Conduction and switching loss}
A MOSFET carries $6\,\mathrm{A}$ rms with hot $R_{DS(on)}=55\,\mathrm{m\Omega}$, so $P_{cond}=6^2(0.055)=1.98\,\mathrm{W}$. If it switches $24\,\mathrm{V}$ and $6\,\mathrm{A}$ with combined rise/fall time $70\,\mathrm{ns}$ at $100\,\mathrm{kHz}$, a first estimate is $P_{sw}\approx\tfrac12VI(t_r+t_f)f=0.504\,\mathrm{W}$. Total device loss is at least $2.48\,\mathrm{W}$ before gate-drive and diode losses.
\end{CornellExamBox}
\begin{CornellExamBox}{JFET self-bias estimate}
For a JFET with illustrative $I_{DSS}=8\,\mathrm{mA}$ and $V_P=-4\,\mathrm{V}$, target $I_D=2\,\mathrm{mA}$. The square-law estimate gives $V_{GS}=V_P(1-\sqrt{I_D/I_{DSS}})=-2.0\,\mathrm{V}$. With the gate near $0\,\mathrm{V}$, $R_S\approx2.0\,\mathrm{V}/2\,\mathrm{mA}=1.0\,\mathrm{k\Omega}$. Real spread requires testing, feedback, or trimming.
\end{CornellExamBox}
\section{Design and Troubleshooting}
\begin{CornellExamBox}{Design and Troubleshooting}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Choose FET family and operating mode from signal range, load, speed, input current, noise, and power requirements.
  \item Use guaranteed transfer or on-resistance data at actual $V_{GS}$ and temperature; establish compliance and bias margins.
  \item Model gate charge, parasitic capacitances, body diode, driver loop, switching node, and thermal path.
  \item Prototype with current limiting; measure gate and drain waveforms with low-inductance probing and compare hot behavior with calculations.
\end{enumerate}\end{CornellExamBox}
\begin{CornellExamBox}{Symptoms, likely causes, and checks}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Device will not turn fully on: gate drive is referenced incorrectly, below guaranteed value, or collapses during the Miller plateau.
  \item Unexpected turn-on: drain $dv/dt$ couples through $C_{GD}$, pull-down is weak, or common-source inductance lifts the source.
  \item High analog error: $R_{on}$ variation, leakage, charge injection, or signal outside supply rails.
  \item Excess heat: hot $R_{DS(on)}$, slow transitions, diode recovery, oscillation, or underestimated duty cycle.
  \item Linear-mode failure: dc SOA or localized thermal stability was violated.
\end{itemize}\end{CornellExamBox}
\begin{CornellWarningBox}{Safety and Limitations}
MOSFET gates are ESD-sensitive and can be damaged by small static discharges or excessive $V_{GS}$. Fast switching creates high $dv/dt$, $di/dt$, overshoot, and electromagnetic interference. Use controlled gate impedance, a defined off-state, short current loops, rated clamps, and current-limited bring-up. Verify that heatsinks or tabs are not electrically exposed.
\end{CornellWarningBox}
\section{Key-Equation Sheet}
\begin{CornellExamBox}{Key Equations}
\begin{itemize}
  \item JFET estimate: $I_D=I_{DSS}(1-V_{GS}/V_P)^2$ within the model's applicable region.
  \item $g_m=dI_D/dV_{GS}$ at the chosen bias point; gain is often $-g_m(R_D\parallel r_o\parallel R_L)$ without degeneration.
  \item $P_{cond}=I_{rms}^2R_{DS(on),hot}$.
  \item First switching estimate: $P_{sw}\approx\tfrac12VI(t_r+t_f)f_s$.
  \item Average gate-drive power: $P_g\approx Q_gV_{drive}f_s$; peak driver current is set by desired transition time and loop impedance.
  \item Thermal estimate: $T_J=T_A+P\theta_{JA}$ only when the stated thermal model and steady-state conditions apply.
\end{itemize}\end{CornellExamBox}
\section{Study and Review}
\subsection{Design checklist}
\begin{itemize}
  \item $V_{GS}$ is referenced to the source in every state.
  \item Guaranteed current or $R_{DS(on)}$ data match the drive and temperature.
  \item Gate charge, driver current, Miller coupling, and turn-off path are adequate.
  \item Body-diode, avalanche, SOA, transient, and fault ratings pass.
  \item Layout minimizes gate and power-loop inductance and provides realistic heat removal.
\end{itemize}\subsection{Common misconceptions}
\begin{itemize}
  \item $V_{GS(th)}$ is not the voltage for a fully enhanced switch.
  \item An insulated gate draws little dc current but can demand large transient current.
  \item All MOSFETs are not safe linear pass devices.
  \item The body diode is not an ideal fast diode.
  \item Paralleled devices still need symmetric electrical and thermal layout.
\end{itemize}\subsection{Active-recall questions}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item How do enhancement and depletion FETs differ at $V_{GS}=0$?
  \item Why is JFET self-bias only approximate?
  \item What sets source-follower output resistance?
  \item Which analog-switch parameters produce dc error?
  \item What is charge injection?
  \item Why does the Miller plateau matter?
  \item How does hot $R_{DS(on)}$ affect loss?
  \item What causes $dv/dt$ turn-on?
  \item Why check pulse and dc SOA separately?
  \item When is a high-side gate driver necessary?
\end{enumerate}\subsection{Original practice problems}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A MOSFET with $Q_g=24\,\mathrm{nC}$ is switched at $250\,\mathrm{kHz}$ from a $10\,\mathrm{V}$ driver. Estimate gate-drive power.
  \item Find conduction loss for $I_{rms}=3.5\,\mathrm{A}$ and hot $R_{DS(on)}=90\,\mathrm{m\Omega}$.
  \item A source follower is biased at $4\,\mathrm{mA}$ with $g_m=7\,\mathrm{mS}$. Estimate intrinsic $1/g_m$.
\end{enumerate}\subsection{Answer key / solution outline}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $P_g=Q_gVf=24\,\mathrm{nC}(10)(250\,\mathrm{kHz})=60\,\mathrm{mW}$, excluding driver quiescent and transition losses.
  \item $P=3.5^2(0.09)=1.10\,\mathrm{W}$.
  \item $1/g_m=143\,\Omega$; the actual output resistance also includes bias-network and load effects.
\end{enumerate}\begin{CornellSummaryBox}{Summary}
Select FETs with guaranteed data at the real gate drive and temperature. In switching, move charge quickly but controllably and analyze the entire current loop; in linear service, bias with feedback and verify the device's linear SOA.
\end{CornellSummaryBox}
