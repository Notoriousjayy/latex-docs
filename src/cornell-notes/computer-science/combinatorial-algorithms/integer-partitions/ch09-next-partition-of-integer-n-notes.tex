\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=Teal!5,colframe=Teal,title=Essential question]How can the immediate antilexicographic successor of an integer partition be computed without expanding repeated parts or looping over $n$?\end{tcolorbox}
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white,colframe=Mist,title=Learning objectives]\begin{itemize}\item Distinguish partitions from compositions.\item Define the antilexicographic order used.\item Derive the two successor cases.\item Use distinct-part and multiplicity arrays.\item Explain why the routine is loop-free per successor.\end{itemize}\textbf{Prerequisites:} integer division, remainders, ordered integer partitions.\end{tcolorbox}
\section*{Cornell notes}
\begin{longtable}{@{}Q!{\color{Mist}\vrule width 1pt}A@{}}\cornellhead
\cue{What is an integer partition?}{A partition of $n$ is a sum of positive parts written in nonincreasing order. Unlike a composition, permuting parts does not create another object. NEXPAR lists partitions from $(n)$ down to $(1+\cdots+1)$ in reversed dictionary order.}
\cue{What is the simple successor case?}{If the final expanded part is greater than one, decrease it by one and append a new part $1$. This is the nearest later partition because all earlier coordinates stay fixed and the suffix changes minimally.}
\cue{What if the suffix consists of ones?}{Let the rightmost part larger than one be $r_j$, followed by some number of ones. Preserve the earlier prefix. Form a residual total $o$ from $r_j$ and the trailing ones, set $f=r_j-1$, then use as many copies of $f$ as fit: $q=\lfloor o/f\rfloor$. If $s=o\bmod f$ is positive, append one part $s$. This is the first partition of the residual total with largest part $f$.}
\cue{How is the state compressed?}{Store only distinct parts $r_1>\cdots>r_d>0$ and their positive multiplicities $m_1,\ldots,m_d$. This avoids explicitly scanning or writing long runs of equal parts, especially the trailing ones.}
\cue{What does NEXPAR update?}{If the smallest distinct part is one, combine its multiplicity with the preceding part and remove the distinct-one entry. Remove one copy of the active part $r_d$, set $f=r_d-1$, insert $\lfloor o/f\rfloor$ copies of $f$, and add the positive remainder as a new smaller distinct part.}
\cue{Correctness invariant}{At exit, distinct parts remain strictly decreasing, multiplicities remain positive, and $\sum_{i=1}^{d}m_ir_i=n$. The residual is represented by the lexicographically greatest multiset whose maximum allowed part is $f$, so no valid partition lies between consecutive outputs.}
\cue{Initialization and end}{Initialize with $d=1$, $r_1=n$, and $m_1=1$. The final state has $r_1=1$ and $m_1=n$. The more-to-come flag is false exactly at that all-ones state.}
\cue{Cost and cautions}{The formal update has no loop whose length depends on $n$ because multiplicities replace repeated listings. Integer division and remainder must use positive operands. An expanded-output adapter may still need $\Theta(n)$ time to print the all-ones partition.}
\cue{Retrieval practice}{\begin{enumerate}\item What order is used?\item Why compress equal parts?\item How is the residual total repartitioned?\item What invariant proves the sum remains $n$?\item What is the terminal representation?\end{enumerate}}
\end{longtable}
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=Ink!4,colframe=Ink,title=Cornell summary]
NEXPAR generates integer partitions in antilexicographic order while storing distinct parts and their multiplicities. A terminal part above one is decreased and followed by a one. When trailing ones are present, the earlier prefix is fixed, the nearest active part is reduced to $f$, and the residual total is filled with as many $f$'s as possible plus one positive remainder. This produces the first legal suffix under the reduced maximum and therefore the immediate successor. Multiplicity storage keeps each formal transition independent of long repeated runs, while the invariant $\sum m_ir_i=n$ and strictly decreasing distinct parts guarantees every output is a valid canonical partition.
\end{tcolorbox}
