\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=Teal!5,colframe=Teal,title=Essential question]How does the bijection between weak compositions and separator positions produce a direct successor algorithm?\end{tcolorbox}
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white,colframe=Mist,title=Learning objectives]\begin{itemize}\item Define a composition with ordered nonnegative parts.\item Derive the count $\binom{n+k-1}{n}$.\item Translate separator motion into changes in the part vector.\item Apply NEXCOM's retained index to avoid a fresh scan.\item Recognize initialization and termination states.\end{itemize}\textbf{Prerequisites:} binomial coefficients, combinations, power-series products, and lexicographic generation.\end{tcolorbox}
\section*{Cornell notes}
\begin{longtable}{@{}Q!{\color{Mist}\vrule width 1pt}A@{}}\cornellhead
\cue{What is a composition here?}{A weak composition of $n$ into $k$ ordered parts is a vector $(r_1,\ldots,r_k)$ with $r_i\ge0$ and $\sum_i r_i=n$. Order matters, so permuting unequal parts generally produces another composition.}
\cue{Why are there $\binom{n+k-1}{n}$?}{Arrange $n$ indistinguishable balls and $k-1$ interior separators in $n+k-1$ positions. The numbers of balls between consecutive separators are the parts, and every weak composition produces one such arrangement. Choosing either the ball positions or separator positions gives the binomial count.}
\cue{What other interpretation is given?}{In a product of $k$ power series, the coefficient of $x^n$ is a sum over all $k$-tuples of exponents totaling $n$. Thus compositions index the terms contributing to a product coefficient. The generating function $(1-x)^{-k}$ yields the same count.}
\cue{How does subset generation transfer?}{The $k-1$ interior separators form a $(k-1)$-subset of $n+k-1$ available positions. Advancing that subset lexicographically moves a separator and resets a suffix of separators. Differences between adjacent separator positions translate directly into updates of the composition parts.}
\cue{What is the direct transition?}{Locate the first nonzero part $r_h$ in the active search direction. Save $t=r_h$, set $r_h=0$, set $r_1=t-1$, and increment $r_{h+1}$. The sum remains $n$: the saved mass is redistributed as $(t-1)+1$. The order induced is the separator subset's lexicographic order.}
\cue{How is scanning avoided?}{NEXCOM retains $h$ and the previous saved value $t$. If the relevant previous value exceeds one, the next active position resets; when it equals one, the active index advances. This is the same state-retention idea used to avoid repeatedly discovering an active suffix in combination generation.}
\cue{Initialization and termination}{Initialize at $(n,0,\ldots,0)$. The final state is $(0,\ldots,0,n)$. A more-to-come flag is true until the last part contains all mass. For $k=1$, the only output is $(n)$.}
\cue{Correctness invariant}{After every transition, all parts are nonnegative and their sum is $n$. The separator bijection proves uniqueness and completeness because NEXCOM mirrors a complete lexicographic listing of the associated separator subsets.}
\cue{Historical or legacy context}{The routine stores state in the caller's part array and flag. A modern iterator should own $r$, $h$, and $t$, and should distinguish a newly initialized iterator from the valid first composition.}
\cue{Retrieval practice}{\begin{enumerate}\item Why are zero parts allowed?\item Which subset represents the separators?\item How does the transition preserve the sum?\item What is the first output?\item What marks the last output?\end{enumerate}}
\end{longtable}
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=Ink!4,colframe=Ink,title=Cornell summary]
Weak compositions become easy to count and generate when viewed as distributions of indistinguishable balls among labeled cells. The $k-1$ cell boundaries occupy a subset of $n+k-1$ positions, giving $\binom{n+k-1}{n}$ objects and transferring lexicographic combination generation to part vectors. NEXCOM performs the translated separator move directly: it clears an active part, transfers all but one unit to the first part, and adds one unit to the following part. Retained state identifies the active position without a fresh full scan. The separator bijection supplies the completeness argument, while the invariant $r_i\ge0$ and $\sum r_i=n$ establishes validity after each local update.
\end{tcolorbox}
