\section*{Cornell notes}
\begin{longtable}{@{}Q!{\color{Mist}\vrule width 1pt}A@{}}\cornellhead
\cue{What is a Young tableau?}{A partition $\lambda$ gives a left-justified shape with row lengths $\lambda_1\ge\lambda_2\ge\cdots$. A tableau fills its $n$ cells with $1,\ldots,n$ so entries increase across every row and down every column. A corner has no cell directly right or below.}
\cue{What is a hook length?}{For a cell, count the cell itself, all cells to its right in the same row, and all cells below it in the same column. The number of tableaux of shape $\lambda$ is
\[
N(\lambda)=\frac{n!}{\prod_{c\in\lambda}h(c)}.
\]}
\cue{How are tableaux paths?}{The largest label must occupy a corner. Delete that corner, then repeat for $n-1,n-2,\ldots$. A complete corner-deletion walk from $\lambda$ to the empty shape uniquely specifies a tableau, placing descending labels in the removed corners.}
\cue{What is the $Y$-vector?}{$y(i)$ is the row containing label $i$. Because row entries are increasing and the shape is fixed, this vector uniquely reconstructs the tableau and makes successor changes easier than storing the full grid.}
\cue{How does NEXYTB find a successor?}{Find the least $j$ that does not occupy the last available corner of its subtableau on $1,\ldots,j$. Move $j$ to the next lower corner. Refill the cells of that subtableau with $1,\ldots,j-1$ in the lexicographically first manner, column by column. Larger labels remain fixed.}
\cue{What are the endpoints of the order?}{The first tableau fills consecutive labels down columns from left to right. The last fills consecutive labels across rows from top to bottom. On a new sequence, the shape array initializes the first $Y$-vector; on continuation, the current $Y$-vector determines the successor.}
\cue{How does RANYTB place one label?}{In the current shape, choose a cell uniformly. If it is not a corner, move to a uniformly chosen other cell in its hook. Continue until a corner is reached. Place the current largest label there, delete the corner, decrement the label, and repeat.}
\cue{Why is the sampler uniform?}{The hook walk reaches each corner with exactly the probability needed for the recurrence
\[
N(\lambda)=\sum_{c\text{ corner}}N(\lambda-c).
\]
Induction then gives every tableau probability $1/N(\lambda)$. The same corner-probability calculation yields a proof of the hook-length formula.}
\cue{Implementation cautions}{The input shape must be nonincreasing and sum to $n$. The implementation also tracks the conjugate partition to navigate hooks. Uniform-square selection uses rejection from the smallest containing rectangle; skew shapes can increase rejection. Preserve the shape if callers expect it unchanged.}
\cue{Retrieval practice}{\begin{enumerate}\item Why must $n$ be in a corner?\item What does $y(i)$ store?\item Which label moves in NEXYTB?\item What is a hook walk?\item How does the corner recurrence prove uniformity?\end{enumerate}}
\end{longtable}
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=Ink!4,colframe=Ink,title=Cornell summary]
Young tableaux of fixed shape are recursively organized by their removable corners. A corner-deletion path records the positions of $n,n-1,\ldots,1$. NEXYTB encodes a tableau by the row of each label, advances the least label whose path edge can increase, and resets the smaller-label subtableau to its first filling. RANYTB chooses each corner with the correct recursive weight through a hook walk: start in a uniform cell and repeatedly move uniformly within the remaining hook until a corner is reached. Repeating on the smaller shape produces a uniform tableau. The analysis of the hook walk also validates the corner recurrence and the hook-length counting formula.
\end{tcolorbox}
