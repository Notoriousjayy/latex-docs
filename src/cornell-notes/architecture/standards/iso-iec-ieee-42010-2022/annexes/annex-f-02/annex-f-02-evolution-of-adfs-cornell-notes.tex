\begin{CornellNotesTable}
\CornellNoteRow{Purpose / key concept}{In systems and software engineering, the notion of ADF dates back to the 1970s.}
\CornellNoteRow{Core details}{\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep,topsep=1pt]
\item The motivation for definition of the term (3.5) and its specification (see 7.1) Here is to provide a means of evolving existing and future ADFs in a uniform manner to promote sharing of information about entities, architectures and techniques for AD, better uniformity to enable improved understanding of the architectures being described, and interoperability between architecture communities who use different conceptual foundations.
\item The uniform definition of architecture viewpoints and coordinated collections of associated specifications can promote reuse of tools and techniques by the communities using these frameworks.
\item The specification of ADF is intended to establish the relationships between an ADF and other concepts Here (illustrated in Figure 2 and Figure 6).
\item ADFs often include additional content, prescriptions and relationships, such as process guidance, life cycle connections, and documentation formats, not defined by This section.
\end{itemize}}
