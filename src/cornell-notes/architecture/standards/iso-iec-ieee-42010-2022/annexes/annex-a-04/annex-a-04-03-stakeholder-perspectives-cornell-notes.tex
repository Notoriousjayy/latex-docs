\begin{CornellNotesTable}
\CornellNoteRow{Purpose / key concept}{This section uses the term stakeholder perspective to mean a particular way of thinking about an entity, especially one that is influenced by one’s beliefs or experiences.}
\CornellNoteRow{Core details}{\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep,topsep=1pt]
\item The way one thinks about an entity (i.e. one’s perspective) can be influenced by organizational role, training, experience, knowledge, personality, character traits, culture, peer pressure, etc.
\item Different ways of thinking about an architecture are often employed when architecting. (See examples in 5.2.4.)
\item The determination of relevant stakeholder perspectives is dependent upon the interests of, and stances adopted by, the various stakeholders that are relevant to the architecture.
\item For a given stakeholder perspective there are usually multiple concerns and aspects to be considered.
\end{itemize}}
\CornellNoteRow{Example / application}{From UAF: strategic, operational, services, personnel, resources, security, projects, standards, actual resources.}
