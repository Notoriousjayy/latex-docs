\vspace{3pt}

\begin{CornellNotesTable}
\CornellNoteRow{Purpose / key concept}{Architecting contributes to the conceptualization, development, operation and maintenance of an entity from its initial conception through its operation, refurbishment or final retirement from use, and eventual disposal.}
\CornellNoteRow{Core details}{\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep,topsep=1pt]
\item Architecting can take place throughout the life cycle of the entity, not necessarily only within the early stage of its life.
\item Therefore, an entity’s architecture potentially influences processes throughout the entity’s life cycle.
\item While it is expected that the architecture of an entity remains stable over a longer period, from time to time the architecture of the entity (or a part of the entity) can incrementally evolve.
\item Less frequently an entity can go through a significant transformation, with necessary re-architecting being performed.
\end{itemize}}
\CornellNoteRow{Interpretive note}{\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep,topsep=1pt]
\item ISO/IEC/IEEE 15288 and ISO/IEC/IEEE 12207 describes the architecture definition process. ISO/IEC/IEEE 42020 complements this definition toward a complete set of architecture processes in support of architecting efforts.
\item Annex C demonstrates how This section can be used when applying the life cycle processes of ISO/IEC/IEEE 12207 and ISO/IEC/IEEE 15288. ISO/IEC/IEEE 42020 specifies a set of processes for architecting, and for architecture governance, management, and enablement within the context of a life cycle.
\end{itemize}}
