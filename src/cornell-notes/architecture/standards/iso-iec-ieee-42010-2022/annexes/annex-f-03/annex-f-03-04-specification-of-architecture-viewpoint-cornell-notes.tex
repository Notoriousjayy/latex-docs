\begin{CornellNotesTable}
\CornellNoteRow{Purpose / key concept}{An ADF generally specifies architecture viewpoints providing specifications of model kinds to frame typical concerns and to govern the architecture views and ease understanding of the legends.}
\CornellNoteRow{Core details}{\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep,topsep=1pt]
\item ADFs often utilize one or more structural categories to represent distribution of architecture viewpoints in a two-dimensional grid or matrix.
\item There are some ADFs, such as GERA (ISO 15704) and SABSA, which use three or more categories to convey the complexity of describing architectures.
\end{itemize}}
\CornellNoteRow{Example / application}{Reference Model for Open distributed (RM-ODP) defines the following concepts: enterprise, information, computational, engineering and technology viewpoint specifications.}

\end{CornellNotesTable}


\begin{CornellSummaryBox}{Summary}
An ADF generally specifies architecture viewpoints providing specifications of model kinds to frame typical concerns and to govern the architecture views and ease understanding of the legends. This material is informative guidance and does not itself create a conformance requirement.
\end{CornellSummaryBox}
