\begin{CornellNotesTable}
\CornellNoteRow{Purpose / key concept}{ISO 15704 specifies a reference base of concepts and principles for enterprise architectures that enable enterprise development, enterprise integration, enterprise interoperability, human understanding and computer processing and further specifies requirements for models and languages created for expressing such enterprise architectures.}
\CornellNoteRow{Core details}{\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep,topsep=1pt]
\item ISO 15704 specifies those terms, concepts and principles considered necessary to address stakeholder concerns and to carry out enterprise creation programs as well as any incremental change projects required by the enterprise throughout the whole life of the enterprise and forms the basis by which enterprise architecture and modelling standards can be developed or aligned.
\item ISO 15704 does not present or adopt specific methodologies for creating or using enterprise architectures or models but does utilize This section as a source of some terminology and overall characterization of an architecture description.
\item However, the focus is on establishing a reference base capable of supporting specific enterprise programs, rather than a design intended to fulfil the stated requirements.
\item ISO 15704 identifies an extensive collection of potential artefacts for expressing an enterprisereferencing architecture and its associated methodologies.
\end{itemize}}
\CornellNoteRow{Study relationship / visual insight}{The modelling-framework figures organize architectural information across lifecycle phases, genericity levels, model-content aspects, purpose, and implementation means, and illustrate recursive architecting of related enterprise/project/product entities.}
\CornellNoteRow{Interpretive note}{\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep,topsep=1pt]
\item Most ISO standards can be represented as partial models or reference models.
\end{itemize}}
