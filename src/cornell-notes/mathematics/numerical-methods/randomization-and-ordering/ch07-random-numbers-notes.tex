\section*{Chapter Overview}
This chapter organizes the mathematical and computational ideas behind random numbers. The notes preserve the numbered section sequence while emphasizing method selection, explicit assumptions, numerical reliability, cost, and verification.

\section*{Learning Objectives}
Explain the governing problem class; compare Uniform Deviates, Completely Hashing a Large Array, Deviates from Other Distributions, and Multivariate Normal Deviates; design defensible stopping and verification checks; distinguish problem conditioning from algorithmic stability.

\section*{Prerequisites}
Probability distributions, expectation, variance, bit operations, and basic linear algebra.

\section*{Operational Workflow}
Choose a generator for the quality requirement; seed and split streams explicitly; transform uniforms with a validated method; test distributional behavior; report sampling uncertainty.

\subsection*{Formula and notation anchor}
\[
\widehat{I}_N=\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^{N} f(X_i),\qquad \operatorname{SE}(\widehat{I}_N)\approx\frac{s_f}{\sqrt{N}}
\]
The inverse-square-root rate is robust but slow; dependence among samples changes the effective sample size.

\begin{warnbox}{Numerical warning}
Short or correlated streams, biased integer mapping, accidental seed reuse, rejection inefficiency, singular covariance, underestimated Monte Carlo error, and misuse for cryptography.
\end{warnbox}

\section*{Worked example}
\begin{exambox}{Independent worked example}
Estimate $\int_0^1 x^2\,dx$ from points $0.1,0.4,0.7,0.9$: $\widehat I=(0.01+0.16+0.49+0.81)/4=0.3675$.  Compare with $1/3$ and report a standard error from the sample variance.  Four samples are far too few for a strong accuracy claim, regardless of the generator.
\end{exambox}
