\section*{Chapter Overview}
This chapter organizes the mathematical and computational ideas behind minimization or maximization of functions. The notes preserve the numbered section sequence while emphasizing method selection, explicit assumptions, numerical reliability, cost, and verification.

\section*{Learning Objectives}
Explain the governing problem class; compare Initially Bracketing a Minimum, Golden Section Search in One Dimension, Parabolic Interpolation and Brent's Method in One Dimension, and One-Dimensional Search with First Derivatives; design defensible stopping and verification checks; distinguish problem conditioning from algorithmic stability.

Calculus, gradients, linear algebra, constraints, and numerical tolerances.

\section*{Worked example}
\begin{exambox}{Independent worked example}
Minimize $f(x)=(x-3)^2+1$.  The derivative vanishes at $x=3$ and $f''(x)=2>0$, so the minimum value is $1$.  A bracketed search around $[0,6]$ confirms the same point.  In floating point, objective changes near the solution may round away; use a scaled step or gradient test.
\end{exambox}
