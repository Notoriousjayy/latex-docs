\section*{Chapter Overview}
This chapter organizes the mathematical and computational ideas behind fast fourier transform. The notes preserve the numbered section sequence while emphasizing method selection, explicit assumptions, numerical reliability, cost, and verification.

\section*{Learning Objectives}
Explain the governing problem class; compare Fourier Transform of Discretely Sampled Data, Fast Fourier Transform, FFT of Real Functions, and Fast Sine and Cosine Transforms; design defensible stopping and verification checks; distinguish problem conditioning from algorithmic stability.

\section*{Prerequisites}
Complex arithmetic, sampled signals, sums, periodicity, and array indexing.

\section*{Operational Workflow}
Fix transform sign and normalization; map physical samples to indices; choose a factorization and storage layout; transform; interpret bins and symmetry; validate with inverse recovery and energy checks.

\subsection*{Formula and notation anchor}
\[
X_k=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}x_n e^{-2\pi i kn/N},\qquad x_n=\frac1N\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}X_k e^{2\pi i kn/N}
\]
Other sign and normalization conventions are valid, but forward and inverse definitions must be paired consistently.

\begin{warnbox}{Numerical warning}
Aliasing, spectral leakage, wrong normalization, off-by-one frequency mapping, overwriting packed real data, non-power-of-two assumptions, and excessive data movement.
\end{warnbox}
